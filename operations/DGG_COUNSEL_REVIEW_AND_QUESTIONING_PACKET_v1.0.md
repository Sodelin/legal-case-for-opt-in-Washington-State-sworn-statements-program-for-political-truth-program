> **Decision rule.** This packet is designed to make counsel review concrete. It does not claim to be “airtight.” No responsible memorandum can eliminate unknown facts, legal change, adverse authority, or fact-specific application. The correct goal is a traceable draft that states its assumptions, separates strong law from untested theory, presents the strongest objections, and blocks use until named reviewers close the gates assigned to them.

## 0. Executive decision brief

### 0.1 Proposed decision

Approve continued **synthetic design work and nonpublic feasibility testing only**. Do not approve a named participant, public release, remote or electronic notarization, government partnership, official-capacity use, prosecution referral arrangement, or a claim that the format guarantees false-swearing/perjury consequences.

The first operational objective is not to “put an interview under oath.” It is to test a narrower legal object: a short, exact, participant-authored or expressly adopted **Canonical Sworn Record (CSR)** containing standalone factual statements, stated bases, qualifications, a wet-ink signature, and a contemporaneous verification certificate. The ordinary interview and all DGG commentary remain in a separate, expressly unsworn **Public Media Record (PMR)**.

### 0.2 Current legal assessment

| Proposition | Assessment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Washington authorizes a verification on oath or affirmation of a statement in a tangible record. | **Strong statutory support** | A narrow signed CSR is legally plausible. |
| A notary certifies substantive truth or state approval. | **Incorrect** | Repeat the limitation beside every form, clip, and publication. |
| A knowingly false statement in this private format necessarily violates RCW 9A.72.040. | **Plausible text; untested application** | Counsel-only disclosure; no promise of liability, prosecution, or conviction. |
| A private DGG verification is necessarily an “official proceeding” for first-degree perjury. | **Colorable text; unresolved and weak as a product claim** | Do not call it a perjury interview or use felony rhetoric. |
| Notarization makes the CSR true, admissible, self-authenticating for all purposes, or conclusive proof. | **Incorrect/overbroad** | Hearsay, relevance, authentication, completeness, best-evidence, and privilege issues remain. |
| An independent state-commissioned notary makes all DGG editorial conduct state action. | **Not supported categorically** | Keep the notarial act discrete and private; reanalyze any governmental direction, joint action, compulsion, or entwinement. |
| A private, unfunded study automatically falls under the Common Rule. | **Incorrect** | Obtain an HRPP/IRB determination anyway before randomized or publishable human-subject data collection. |
| Government sponsorship would be a cosmetic change. | **Incorrect** | Treat it as a new intervention and a new public-law project. |

### 0.3 Why the format may still be useful

The defensible civic value does not depend on punishment. The format may create a better evidence object by making these features inspectable:

- one proposition at a time;
- the participant’s complete standalone statement rather than a bare “yes” or “no”;
- personal-knowledge versus record-based basis;
- defined scope, exceptions, and qualifications;
- source and strongest contrary-source disclosure;
- exact record ID, version, pages, and proposition index;
- visible uncertainty, lack of recall, inability to verify, and decline without penalty; and
- append-only correction and provenance.

The format fails if the notarial cue raises trust without accuracy, if refusal becomes a dishonesty label, if participants become more evasive, or if a government connection produces perceived compulsion or official endorsement.

### 0.4 Counsel decision requested

Counsel should return one of four decisions for each component: **approve as drafted; approve with written conditions; hold for facts/research; or do not approve.** Counsel is not asked to certify that the project is risk-free. Counsel is asked to identify the lawful form, the factual assumptions, the remaining risks, and the conditions that must precede each phase.

# I. Project statement: what it is, how it is used, and why

## 1. The project in one paragraph

DGG proposes a private, optional format in which a willing adult reviews a short set of disclosed factual propositions, authors or expressly adopts complete standalone answers, states the basis and qualifications for each answer, and—only after the exact paper record is frozen—signs the selected statements under an oath or affirmation administered by an independent Washington notary. DGG may then conduct a separate ordinary interview. The notary certifies the notarial act and required determinations, not factual truth. The first phases are nonpublic and use only synthetic or fully consented materials.

## 2. Intended uses

### 2.1 Authorized design uses

1. **Memorandum building.** Analysts can connect a factual proposition to supporting and contrary sources, authorities, qualifications, and corrections.
2. **Question discipline.** Interviewers must define actor, conduct, date, scope, unit, and knowledge route before asking.
3. **Evidence literacy.** Viewers can distinguish a claim about an underlying event from a claim that a record says something.
4. **Calibration research.** Researchers can test accuracy, uncertainty, source inspection, correction, coercion, and audience overtrust.
5. **Detective practice.** A reviewer can reproduce the reasoning chain instead of accepting a commentator’s confidence.

### 2.2 Uses that are not authorized

- a truth-certification service, honesty score, red/green credibility badge, or public blacklist;
- a free-form “interview under oath”;
- a government hearing, official investigation, ballot/debate qualification, employment screen, licensing condition, benefit condition, or law-enforcement intake;
- a device for threatening a participant with prosecution or reputational punishment;
- an automatic evidentiary shortcut;
- a substitute for fact-checking, discovery, cross-examination, source verification, or counsel; or
- a national or remote protocol inferred from Washington’s current in-person paper design.

## 3. Why advance disclosure and voluntary participation are core controls

Advance disclosure is designed to reduce accidental falsity and coercion. It permits the participant to inspect records, correct premises, identify privilege or confidentiality concerns, distinguish personal knowledge from staff reporting, consult independent counsel, and decide that an item belongs in the unsworn lane. The process is not a memory contest or surprise examination.

Voluntariness applies at four levels: the entire format, each question, the precise wording of each adopted statement, and publication. A participant may stop, qualify, state uncertainty, decline, or request counsel. DGG must not attach a dishonesty inference or a practical penalty to any choice.

## 4. Record architecture

| Record | Contents | Sworn status | Controller |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canonical Sworn Record (CSR) | Exact adopted statements, basis, qualifications, proposition index, signature, jurat | Only identified adopted content is sworn | Participant authors/adopts; custodian preserves; notary controls act/certificate |
| Public Media Record (PMR) | Interview, video, audio, transcript, commentary, source analysis, clips | Unsworn | DGG editorial team, subject to release and publication controls |
| Source packet | Supporting and materially contrary sources, search record, source limits | Unsworn unless exact text is deliberately incorporated | Fact editor |
| Administration packet | Invitation, consents, manifest, custody, incident and correction logs | Unsworn | Assigned controllers |
| Notary records | Journal and any legally required notarial records | Separate; not part of CSR/PMR | Notary under applicable law |
| Counsel file | Legal advice, sign-offs, assumptions, issue list | Not part of public CSR; privilege is not guaranteed | Counsel/client under engagement terms |

The CSR must never incorporate the PMR or source packet by blanket reference. A later clip, caption, edit, or host statement cannot expand the oath.

# II. Government relationship and state-action boundary

## 5. Recommended Phase 1 position

> **DGG is a private, voluntary media/research project with one regulated legal interface: an independent Washington-commissioned notary performs a discrete notarial act. The commission authorizes the notary; it does not make DGG state-sponsored, state-approved, or a government truth tribunal.**

This is a fact-specific position, not a categorical immunity. State-action analysis focuses on the challenged conduct. The Supreme Court identifies limited routes such as performance of a traditional exclusive public function, government compulsion, and joint action. Licensing, public-interest purpose, regulation, and even a contract do not alone answer the question. Government coercion, significant encouragement, joint participation, or pervasive entwinement can change the result. See [*Manhattan Community Access Corp. v. Halleck*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-1702_h315.pdf), [*Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co.*](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep457/usrep457922/usrep457922.pdf), [*Blum v. Yaretsky*](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep457/usrep457991/usrep457991.pdf), [*Rendell-Baker v. Kohn*](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep457/usrep457830/usrep457830.pdf), and [*Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Ass’n*](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep531/usrep531288/usrep531288.pdf).

## 7. Washington public-records and public-resource consequences

DGG is not an agency merely because it uses a commissioned notary. But the Washington Public Records Act covers writings relating to government conduct that an agency prepares, owns, uses, or retains. Official-capacity messages can be public records even on a private device. [Chapter 42.56 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.56&full=true); [*Nissen v. Pierce County*](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/908753.pdf).

A private entity may also become the functional equivalent of an agency for disclosure purposes. The Washington Supreme Court’s 2025 *Horvath* opinion applies the nonexclusive *Telford* factors—governmental function, public funding, government involvement, and government creation—along with the PRA’s transparency purpose. [*Horvath v. DBIA Services*](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/1033397.pdf). Federal state action and Washington functional-equivalency are different tests; failure of one does not decide the other.

Official participation may also implicate:

- state ethics and use of public resources under [chapter 42.52 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.52&full=true);
- municipal conflicts and special privileges under [chapter 42.23 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.23&full=true);
- state procurement and contracting under [chapter 39.26 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=39.26&full=true);
- data-sharing agreements, security classification, and audit rights, including [RCW 39.26.340](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=39.26.340);
- public retention under [chapter 40.14 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=40.14&full=true); and
- open meetings if a public body is created or performs delegated public work under [chapter 42.30 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.30&full=true).

# III. Professional questioning standard

## 10. Design rule

The interviewer’s question is not itself sworn. The participant’s **complete standalone adopted statement**, stated basis, and printed qualification are the only substantive sworn content. A bare “yes” or “no” is prohibited because it can silently import disputed premises from DGG-authored wording.

The question, supporting and contrary sources, DGG analysis, interviewer commentary, decline status, and counsel approvals remain expressly unsworn unless exact text is deliberately incorporated in a standalone adopted statement.

## 11. Question-admission test

A proposed item may enter the drafting schedule only if every applicable box is checked:

- [ ] It asks about a verifiable past or present fact, not opinion, advocacy, prediction, motive, character, or moral judgment.
- [ ] It contains one proposition; a single answer cannot be true in one respect and false in another.
- [ ] It identifies the actor, act/event/condition, relevant date or period, and place when material.
- [ ] Material terms, units, thresholds, exceptions, and comparison sets are defined neutrally.
- [ ] It avoids an unbounded “always,” “never,” or universal negative unless the search universe is defined.
- [ ] It does not presume misconduct or embed a disputed accusation.
- [ ] It can be answered from personal knowledge, identified records, or a precisely limited combination.
- [ ] The participant received the exact wording, definitions, and source/counter-source packet in advance.
- [ ] A similarly situated person with different politics would receive materially the same formulation.
- [ ] DGG has stated what each response would and would not establish.
- [ ] No located evidence makes the proposed response known or probably false or materially misleading.
- [ ] Legal/media review has considered defamation, false light, privilege, privacy, campaign, and recording issues.
- [ ] The sworn format adds a reasoned value beyond ordinary reporting.

Any unchecked item moves to the unsworn lane, returns for revision, or is rejected.

## 12. Response taxonomy

The exact adopted statement controls if any response code conflicts with its text.

| Code | Response path | What may be adopted |
|---|---|---|
| T-PK | True — personal knowledge | A complete factual statement within stated personal knowledge |
| F-PK | False — personal knowledge | A complete factual denial within stated personal knowledge |
| T-R | True — identified records | Prefer “the identified record states X” unless the records and capacity support the underlying event |
| F-R | False — identified records | State the defined record universe and search/verification limit |
| Q | Qualified | Complete proposition with every material limit and exception on the same page |
| U | Unknown | “As of [time], I do not know whether [defined proposition] is true.” |
| R | Not recalled | “As of [time], I do not presently recall facts sufficient to answer [defined proposition].” |
| V | Cannot verify | “After reviewing [identified materials], I cannot verify whether [defined proposition] is true.” |
| D | Decline | No underlying proposition or knowledge-state assertion is sworn; omit from the Adopted Proposition Index |

Counsel must decide whether U, R, and V are sworn statements about present knowledge, recall, or verification ability, or merely administrative responses. The form below permits inclusion only through the Adopted Proposition Index. A decline is never included.

## 13. Questioning sequence

### 13.1 Green zone — unsworn review

1. Confirm written consent and that no recording/transcription system is active before the approved consent condition.
2. Explain the sworn/unsworn boundary, right to counsel, response taxonomy, no-inference rule, and correction process.
3. Present the exact question, definitions, support, and strongest contrary material.
4. Ask the participant to identify the proposition they are actually prepared to state—not merely answer “yes” or “no.”
5. Ask for the basis: personal observation/action, communication, identified record, combination, or present knowledge state.
6. Ask for every limit, exception, date qualification, capacity qualification, and contrary fact.
7. Read the complete standalone proposed statement back. The participant or participant’s counsel authors or expressly adopts it.
8. If a follow-up changes substance, create a new draft; do not treat oral clarification as part of the future oath.

Permitted neutral prompts include: “What exact date range can you support?”; “Are you stating what the record says, or that the underlying event occurred?”; “What exception should appear in the sentence?”; and “What facts are outside your personal knowledge?”

Prohibited prompts include: “Are you sure you want to risk perjury?”; “Why won’t you tell the truth?”; “A decline will look bad”; “Just answer yes or no”; and any threat, promised benefit, preferred-answer coaching, or publicity leverage.

### 13.2 Amber zone — freeze

Generate the final paper record and external pre-sign manifest. Reconcile ID, version, page count, proposition index, all answer fields, qualifications, and certificate attachment. Remove or strike unused blank answer space. The participant initials each sworn-payload page. Any substantive change returns the process to Green and creates a new version.

### 13.3 Red zone — notarial act

The session lead yields control to the independent notary. The notary—not DGG—decides whether to proceed, determines identity/signature and other required matters, administers the approved oath or affirmation, witnesses the signature, completes the certificate and journal contemporaneously, and may refuse. The notary does **not** approve the proposition content, source sufficiency, legal theory, editorial fairness, or criminal consequences.

### 13.4 Green zone — close

The notarial act is formally closed. Every later statement is unsworn unless a new complete CSR and notarial act occur. Transfer the original under custody receipt, create the executed-scan digest, perform privacy/redaction review, and hold the record nonpublic in Phases 0 and 1.

## 14. Drafting examples

**Prefer:** “Between January 1 and June 30, 2026, did you personally authorize transaction T-014?”  
**Avoid:** “Can you swear you were never bought by special interests?”

**Prefer:** “As of August 20, 2026, did filed report S-004 list a $5,000 contribution from Entity X?”  
**Avoid:** “Did Entity X secretly give you money?”

**Prefer:** “Before making the quoted statement on July 10, 2026, had you personally reviewed document S-009?”  
**Avoid:** “Did you knowingly spread misinformation?”

Even the preferred forms require neutral definitions, source/counter-source disclosure, a material-meaning check, and counsel review.

## 25. Evidence, admissibility, privilege, and legal process

Notarization does **not** establish that a statement is true, admissible for its truth, complete, privileged, or automatically self-authenticating in every proceeding. Washington’s evidence rules require case-specific analysis of relevance, authentication, hearsay and exceptions, completeness, original/duplicate rules, privilege, and—where criminal use is contemplated—constitutional confrontation and voluntariness issues. [Washington Rules of Evidence](https://www.courts.wa.gov/court_rules/?fa=court_rules.list&group=ga&set=ER).

| Issue | Risk | Required rule |
|---|---|---|
| Authentication/self-authentication | A jurat and signature may help prove execution but do not authenticate all source materials or prove truth. ER 902(h) concerns acknowledged documents and should not be overextended to this jurat without counsel. | Call the CSR an audit/research record; make no automatic-admissibility claim. |
| Hearsay/completeness | A participant’s out-of-court assertion and DGG republication may face multiple hearsay and context questions. | Preserve complete context and source provenance; counsel identifies any offered purpose/exception. |
| Privilege | DGG counsel, participant counsel, and research counsel may represent different clients; third parties and recording can defeat confidentiality. | Written engagement/client map; privilege-safe channels; keep counsel file out of CSR/PMR/repository. |
| Reporter shield | RCW 5.68.010 may protect qualifying news media/newsgathering records but status, activity, record type, and exceptions are fact-specific. | No promise of immunity; media-counsel opinion; separate editorial/research/admin stores. |
| Subpoena/discovery/legal hold | Private records remain discoverable or subpoenaable; vendor copies exist; deletion may be suspended. | Named legal-demand custodian; request log; counsel review; preservation; narrow lawful production; participant notice when lawful. |
| Self-incrimination/active matters | A voluntary CSR may be used against a speaker; government cooperation or threats increase risk. | Independent participant counsel; exclude active/privileged/classified/sealed matters absent specialized written review. |

## 30. Human-subjects decision path

1. **Journalism or research?** A case-specific portrayal of one person may fit the scholarly/journalistic activity category deemed not “research.” A systematic randomized study designed to generalize about the format is research. [45 CFR 46.102](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-A/section-46.102).
2. **Human subjects?** Direct interaction, audiovisual capture, manipulation, or identifiable private information can create human-subject research.
3. **Does the Common Rule legally apply?** It generally applies when a Common Rule agency conducts or supports the research; a private unfunded study is not automatically covered, but an institution may extend its FWA/policies. [OHRP lesson](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/education-and-outreach/online-education/human-research-protection-training/lesson-2-what-is-human-subjects-research/index.html).
4. **Possible exemption?** Some low-risk adult survey/vignette or benign-behavioral work may fit 45 CFR 46.104, subject to identifiability, disclosure risk, prospective agreement, deception rules, and limited review. Do not self-classify an operative oath with identifiable publication and potential legal/reputational exposure as benign.
5. **Written determination.** Obtain a written HRPP/IRB determination before randomized or publishable data collection and before any real-person speaker experiment. Use [OHRP’s decision charts](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/decision-charts-2018/index.html) and the full regulatory text.
6. **Independent ethics even if uncovered.** Consent, risk minimization, equitable selection, monitoring, privacy, confidentiality, third-party/community harm, and adverse-event review remain necessary even if federal regulation does not attach.

## 31. Measurement rules

The speaker primary endpoint should be false assertions across **all assigned objectively resolvable items**, not accuracy only among answered items, which rewards selective decline. Code accurate assertion, false assertion, appropriate qualification, appropriate unknown/not-verifiable, decline, evasion/irrelevance, and indeterminate reference status separately.

Use a preregistered reference-adjudication protocol, not a claim of “ground truth” for disputed political facts: defined evidence packet, two blinded coders, independent adjudicator, disagreement/uncertainty code, and public criteria. Audience models should account for participant, item, and speaker variation. Report intent-to-treat, uptake, attrition, exclusions, missingness, coder reliability, effect sizes, confidence intervals, multiplicity, and partisan interactions.

Do not exclude participants from primary analyses merely because they fail a post-treatment manipulation check.

## 32. Candidate safety bounds and stopping rules

An independent statistician must finalize powered thresholds. Candidate gates are:

- observed notary-role comprehension at least 90%, with a one-sided 95% lower confidence bound at least 85%;
- upper 95% confidence bound for increased false truth-certification belief below 5 percentage points;
- upper 95% confidence bound for status-label dishonesty inference below standardized `d = 0.15`;
- no practically meaningful partisan interaction under preregistered equivalence bounds;
- a prespecified accuracy or calibration benefit over a plain accuracy commitment, not merely an ordinary interview; and
- zero critical operational incidents.

Immediately suspend enrollment and publication after unconsented capture; ID/journal exposure; wrong-version execution; post-freeze alteration; participant confusion about legal consequences at execution; coercive prosecution/referral rhetoric; identifiable unapproved allegation; known-false/fabricated source; data breach, doxxing, credible threat or severe related distress; or inadvertent public release.

Report critical events within 24 hours to the research lead, safety officer, counsel, and HRPP/IRB when applicable. Restart only after independent review and documented corrective action. Use a prespecified independent harm look; do not repeatedly peek at accumulating results informally.

# IX. Process-integrity review

## 41. Systematic-review audit

This packet is a **targeted, issue-driven legal and empirical synthesis**, not a preregistered systematic review. It used the supplied project files, candidate v1.1 reconciliation, current official statutes/rules, official court opinions where available, official agency materials, and independent adversarial review lanes. It did not claim exhaustive database coverage, duplicate blinded screening, or a complete commercial citator history.

For a publication-grade legal review, counsel should record databases, date limits, jurisdiction filters, terms, citator results, negative treatment, and an adverse-authority log. For the behavioral review, preregister databases, strings, eligibility, duplicate screening, extraction, risk-of-bias assessment, and synthesis; report under PRISMA 2020 where applicable.

**Process-integrity judgment:** strong enough for a counsel-review candidate and Phase 0 design; not sufficient for live legal reliance or a claim of exhaustive authority.

## 42. Robustness and meta-analysis critique

The cited evidence streams concern different interventions and outcomes: laboratory honesty commitments, social-media accuracy prompts, political fact-checking, and misinformation correction. Their effect sizes must not be pooled or used directly to power the full DGG intervention. The honesty-commitment literature is heterogeneous and has publication-bias concerns; accuracy-prompt work measures sharing discernment rather than sworn political testimony; political fact-checking weakens in campaign/motivated contexts; corrections often leave residual influence.

There is no defensible meta-analytic effect estimate for the complete DGG protocol. A future review should separately map honesty commitment, accuracy salience, advance disclosure, source transparency, authority/notarial halo, refusal stigma, chilling/selection, and correction. A DGG-specific meta-analysis should wait for multiple independent comparable studies and then use multilevel random-effects methods, dependent-effect handling, heterogeneity and prediction intervals, and sensitivity analyses.

The most important adverse hypotheses are that notarization adds no value over a plain accuracy commitment; increases false certainty; suppresses participation or correction; shifts error into legalistic evasion; or creates partisan/asymmetric invitation and interpretation effects. A credible project must preregister and publish those tests.

## 43. Final design conclusion

The project has a legally plausible Washington notarial architecture and a serious research rationale. Its strongest form is a private, voluntary, exact, source-linked, participant-controlled record with visible uncertainty and correction. Its weakest form is a dramatic “perjury interview” that depends on a government truth halo or public punishment for declining.

The present packet is therefore **lawyer-facing but not lawyer-approved**. It should let a lawyer see the complete affirmative theory, the strongest objections, the professional form, the government boundary, the research needed, and the decisions still required. Until those decisions are signed, the authorized use remains blank forms, synthetic examples, and Phase 0 tabletop work.
