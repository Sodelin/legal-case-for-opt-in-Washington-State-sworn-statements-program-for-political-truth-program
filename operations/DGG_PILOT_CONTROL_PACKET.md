## 1. Authorized Phase 0 shape

The pilot has three lanes that must remain visibly and operationally separate:

| Lane | Content | Legal treatment | Publication treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — test record | Fictional assertions in a mock-only replica; a tangible paper CSR is a future design model | Current work uses fictional facts only with no real signer, oath, or notarial act. Any later authorized verification would be limited to literally true, personal-knowledge statements about the test itself, with the exact CSR as the sole oath object | No participant exists or is identified in current Phase 0 |
| B — ordinary interview | Analysis, policy, predictions, opinions, advocacy, and follow-up | Unsworn | Label as unsworn; ordinary editorial process applies |
| C — no sworn participation | Synthetic decline, question-level decline, or no response | No adverse legal inference | Do not publish identified invitation/refusal status through Phase 2 |

Current Washington law contains a safeguarded record-based remote-notarization route, but it is quarantined research for this project. Present DGG Phase 0 is only a pure fictional mock with no real signer, oath, or notarial act. A nonpublic, in-person, Washington-only, wet-ink tangible-paper workflow is retained solely as a possible future design model after reviewed supersession and every gate. That future preference would be a project policy choice, not a claim that remote notarization is generally unavailable.

## 2. Stop conditions

The Phase 0 lead must stop or move the item to the synthetic unsworn lane if any of these conditions occurs:

- the question is vague, predictive, rhetorical, value-laden, compound, or outside personal knowledge;
- a real target/public-figure participant is proposed;
- the synthetic participant script has not received the prompt and notice in advance;
- the participant asks to consult counsel, pause, qualify, say unknown, or decline;
- voluntariness, capacity, identity, or language comprehension is in doubt;
- the notary refuses, pauses, or cannot complete the lawful in-person workflow;
- the displayed record differs from the record to be signed;
- any participant has not consented to each applicable recording;
- the notary is not physically in Washington, lacks a current commission, or has a disqualifying direct beneficial interest;
- the exact tangible CSR, compliant certificate, identity process, journal handling, or record-integrity controls cannot be completed;
- anyone believes an answer or the record may be inaccurate; or
- counsel has not cleared the participant's location and the publication plan.

No host, producer, or campaign representative may overrule the notary.

## 3. Role separation

| Role | May do | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| DGG session lead | Explain choices, ask approved prompts, pause, document status | Administer the oath, select the notarial certificate, threaten referral, or characterize guilt |
| Participant | Review, answer, qualify, say unknown, decline, correct, sign, consult counsel | Be pressured to guess or waive required rights |
| Independent notary | Control lawful completion or refusal, identity, oath, signature, certificate execution, and any required journal | Approve proposition content, sources, wording, legal sufficiency, or certificate selection for DGG; certify truth, capacity, competence, or voluntariness; endorse DGG; surrender original journal control; accept editorial control |
| Production custodian | Maintain DGG's consented master, transcript, hash, access log, and correction chain | Obtain KBA answers, credential images, seal credentials, notary journal, or the notarial archive for publication |
| Counsel | Select/approve the requested act and certificate form; approve exact forms, claims, jurisdiction, release, preservation, and incident response | Treat this packet as a substitute for fact-specific advice or ask a nonattorney notary to practice law |
| Editorial reviewer | Check exact status, context, symmetry, disclaimers, and material edits | Imply that notarization proves truth or refusal proves dishonesty |

## 5. Question admission test

A question enters Lane A only if every answer below is yes:

1. Can evidence establish the proposition as true or false?
2. Does it identify the relevant actor, action or condition, and date or period?
3. Are every material term and exception defined neutrally?
4. Can the participant answer from personal knowledge or an identified record basis?
5. Does the form allow unknown, qualification, and decline without penalty?
6. Would DGG ask the same formulation of a similarly situated person with different politics?
7. Is swearing this proposition more useful than ordinary reporting?
8. Has counsel approved its inclusion, without asking the notary to approve content?

Any no moves the item to Lane B or removes it.

## 7. Controlled session sequence

This sequence is retained only as a tabletop model of a possible future actual test verification containing literally true statements about the test itself. It must not be executed while the remand remains open. The current pure mock may simulate the controls but must not administer an oath, obtain a sworn signature, or perform a notarial act.

1. **Model opening and roles.** Simulate explaining that a future sworn record and ordinary interview would be separate.
2. **Model layered consent controls.** Compare a reasonably effective recorded announcement and any stricter written pre-consent policy. Distinguish a hypothetical legally required notarial record from DGG's hypothetical production recording and publication license.
3. **Model voluntariness.** Display the future rights to stop, consult counsel, answer unknown, qualify, or decline without an adverse inference.
4. **Model the in-person workflow—do not enter it.** Show that a future notary would control lawful completion/refusal, identity, execution of the requested act, certificate, and any required journal entry.
5. **Model fixed-record review.** Use a fictional participant placeholder to review the complete mock version, attachments, and qualifications.
6. **Model correction before signing.** Resolve fictional ambiguities without coaching an answer.
7. **Model signature and oath fields—do not sign, swear, affirm, or notarize.** Use only conspicuous placeholders; complete no participant signature, oath, certificate, journal entry, or notarial act.
8. **Model immutability.** Demonstrate that a future executed CSR would not be edited and that a substantive correction would require a completely new act and append-only link; promise no statutory defense.
9. **Model express closure.** Mark the end of the fictional Lane A before any Lane B discussion.
10. **Model reconciliation.** Hash the fictional CSR and mock production master; simulate certificate and access-log checks without creating or obtaining any notarial record.
11. **Quarantine the mock output.** Retain only fictional Phase 0 review material; no participant record or participant publication exists or is authorized.

Remote interruption rules remain research only. If a future approved route causes a remotely located individual to exit the workflow, WAC 308-30-310 requires identity verification to restart; a transient glitch that does not cause workflow exit is not automatically the same trigger.

## 8. Synthetic participant-notice test—controlled substance

The final notice must be written by or approved by counsel. At minimum, it must communicate:

- participation and every question are voluntary;
- the participant may stop, consult counsel, say unknown, qualify, or decline;
- refusal or silence does not imply dishonesty, falsity, guilt, or unwillingness to answer;
- the notarial certificate evidences the act and specified legal determinations—not factual truth, legal sufficiency, capacity, competence, or voluntariness;
- a knowingly false statement under a Washington-authorized oath may have legal consequences, with false swearing the clearest possible reference point;
- first-degree perjury, Washington jurisdiction, charging, conviction, and interstate process require additional independent elements and decisions;
- an unqualified assertion about something the participant does not know can be treated under RCW 9A.72.080 like a knowingly false statement;
- correction must occur before execution; a substantive post-execution correction requires a completely new act and is not a promise of immunity or a statutory defense; and
- the recording, publication, retention, and correction terms.

| Participant accepted ordinary interview only | “Participated in an ordinary unsworn interview; did not use the voluntary sworn-record format.” |
| Session failed technically or legally | “A sworn record was not completed.” Add a reason only with documented consent and editorial/legal approval. |

In a synthetic presentation, place this disclaimer next to every decline or no-response example with equal prominence:

> Participation is voluntary. Declining or not responding does not imply dishonesty, falsity, guilt, or unwillingness to answer; reasons may include scheduling, legal advice, or preference for another format.

Prohibited descriptions include “refused to tell the truth,” “failed the oath,” “would not risk perjury,” “notary-verified truth,” and any assertion that an offense occurred without an authoritative adjudication and counsel review.

## 10. Suspected-inaccuracy protocol

If anyone believes a Lane A answer may be inaccurate or outside the participant's knowledge:

1. pause questioning and any stream or publication;
2. do not accuse, coach, negotiate, threaten, or promise immunity;
3. offer correction, qualification, unknown, decline, and counsel access;
4. before execution, permit participant-controlled correction and refreeze the complete CSR; after execution, require a completely new act for any substantive correction;
5. preserve every executed original without alteration, deletion, backdating, or silent replacement;
6. mark all related production material restricted and unpublished;
7. notify authorized counsel under the incident plan;
8. document the pause, choices offered, access, and decisions;
9. create any later correction as an append-only new record; and
10. obtain counsel approval before publication, referral, response to legal process, or destruction.

The notary remains responsible for any legally required journal and, for a future remote route, audiovisual archive. As project policy, DGG must not direct destruction or place notarial compliance records in the public repository. The ordinary journal rule has a documented attorney-notary exception, and an authorized third-party audiovisual repository does not shift the notary's responsibility.
