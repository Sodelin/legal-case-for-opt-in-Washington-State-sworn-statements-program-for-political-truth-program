# Digital Ground Game sworn-record pilot control packet

**Control date:** 2026-08-30  
**Status:** Phase 0 design control; not a live notarial form or participant release  
**Controlling memorandum:** [`memorandum/DGG_WASHINGTON_SWORN_STATEMENT_MEMORANDUM.md`](../memorandum/DGG_WASHINGTON_SWORN_STATEMENT_MEMORANDUM.md)

This packet translates the audited research into a bounded dry-run protocol. It must not be used with a public figure until Washington counsel, media/privacy counsel, any required participant-state counsel, and the commissioned notary approve the exact documents and workflow in writing.

## 1. Authorized pilot shape

The pilot has three lanes that must remain visibly and operationally separate:

| Lane | Content | Legal treatment | Publication treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — sworn record | Narrow, defined, falsifiable statements in a versioned record | Participant signs and swears or affirms before an authorized notary | Publish only the approved signed record, certificate, provenance, and limitations |
| B — ordinary interview | Analysis, policy, predictions, opinions, advocacy, and follow-up | Unsworn | Label as unsworn; ordinary editorial process applies |
| C — no sworn participation | Explicit decline, question-level decline, or no response | No adverse legal inference | Publish only the exact neutral status and adjacent disclaimer |

During 2026, a remote Lane A session must use a defined electronic record, electronic signature, and verification on oath or affirmation through a compliant Washington remote-notary workflow. A generic video call and a free-form oral oath are not the legal core.

## 2. Stop conditions

The session lead must stop or move the item to Lane B if any of these conditions occurs:

- the question is vague, predictive, rhetorical, value-laden, compound, or outside personal knowledge;
- the participant has not received the prompt and notice in advance;
- the participant asks to consult counsel, pause, qualify, say unknown, or decline;
- voluntariness, capacity, identity, or language comprehension is in doubt;
- the notary refuses, pauses, loses the connection, or cannot complete the required workflow;
- the displayed record differs from the record to be signed;
- any participant has not consented to each applicable recording;
- the notary is outside Washington, lacks a required endorsement, or has a direct beneficial interest;
- the technology cannot complete identity proofing, same-record confirmation, tamper-evident association, recording, or retention;
- anyone believes an answer or the record may be inaccurate; or
- counsel has not cleared the participant's location and the publication plan.

No host, producer, or campaign representative may overrule the notary.

## 3. Role separation

| Role | May do | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| DGG session lead | Explain choices, ask approved prompts, pause, document status | Administer the oath, select the notarial certificate, threaten referral, or characterize guilt |
| Participant | Review, answer, qualify, say unknown, decline, correct, sign, consult counsel | Be pressured to guess or waive required rights |
| Independent notary | Control identity, voluntariness, oath, signature, certificate, journal, and required archive | Certify factual truth, endorse DGG, surrender journal control, or accept editorial control |
| Production custodian | Maintain DGG's consented master, transcript, hash, access log, and correction chain | Obtain KBA answers, credential images, seal credentials, notary journal, or the notarial archive for publication |
| Counsel | Approve exact forms, claims, jurisdiction, release, preservation, and incident response | Treat this packet as a substitute for fact-specific advice |
| Editorial reviewer | Check exact status, context, symmetry, disclaimers, and material edits | Imply that notarization proves truth or refusal proves dishonesty |

## 5. Question admission test

A question enters Lane A only if every answer below is yes:

1. Can evidence establish the proposition as true or false?
2. Does it identify the relevant actor, action or condition, and date or period?
3. Are every material term and exception defined neutrally?
4. Can the participant answer from personal knowledge or an identified record basis?
5. Does the form allow unknown, qualification, and decline without penalty?
6. Would DGG ask the same formulation of a similarly situated person with different politics?
7. Is swearing this proposition more useful than ordinary reporting?
8. Have counsel and the notary approved its inclusion?

Any no moves the item to Lane B or removes it.

## 6. Record-control fields

The counsel-approved Lane A record should include at least:

- unique record ID and version;
- creation timestamp and cryptographic hash;
- participant's public name and relevant capacity, with no unnecessary identifier;
- voluntary-choice and notary-limitation notice;
- defined scope, time period, and terms;
- each exact prompt and exact selected or qualified response;
- source-of-knowledge field;
- unknown, qualification, and decline options;
- pre-signing review and correction acknowledgment;
- counsel/notary-approved oath or affirmation language;
- participant signature and timestamp; and
- the notary's contemporaneous certificate, completed under the notary's control.

Never edit the signed record. A substantive change requires a new version, signature, oath or affirmation, certificate, journal entry, and append-only link to the superseded record.

## 7. Controlled session sequence

1. **Open and identify roles.** State that the sworn record and ordinary interview are separate.
2. **Capture layered consent.** Record an effective announcement and affirmative response from the participant, notary, interviewer, interpreter, and crew. Distinguish the statutory notarial archive from DGG's production recording and publication license.
3. **Confirm voluntariness.** Repeat the right to stop, consult counsel, answer unknown, qualify, or decline without an adverse inference.
4. **Enter the notary workflow.** The notary controls identity, technology, record matching, and interruption handling.
5. **Review the fixed record.** The participant sees the complete version to be signed, including attachments and qualifications.
6. **Correct before signing.** Resolve ambiguities without coaching the answer.
7. **Sign and swear or affirm.** Complete the participant signature and notarial act contemporaneously.
8. **Offer an immediate correction.** Before closing, ask whether any answer requires correction, qualification, retraction, or withdrawal. Do not promise a statutory defense.
9. **Close expressly.** Record the end of Lane A before any Lane B discussion.
10. **Seal and reconcile.** Hash the signed record and DGG master; confirm the certificate, access log, and separate custodians without obtaining the notary's journal or compliance archive.
11. **Hold for review.** No publication until legal, notarial, privacy, and editorial checks pass.

If a remote participant exits the compliant notarial workflow before completion, treat the notarial session as interrupted and restart identity verification as current rules require.

## 8. Participant notice—controlled substance

The final notice must be written by or approved by counsel. At minimum, it must communicate:

- participation and every question are voluntary;
- the participant may stop, consult counsel, say unknown, qualify, or decline;
- refusal or silence does not imply dishonesty, falsity, guilt, or unwillingness to answer;
- the notary verifies the act, required identity, signature, and oath or affirmation—not factual truth;
- a knowingly false statement under a Washington-authorized oath may have legal consequences, with false swearing the clearest possible reference point;
- first-degree perjury, Washington jurisdiction, charging, conviction, and interstate process require additional independent elements and decisions;
- an unqualified assertion about something the participant does not know can be treated under RCW 9A.72.080 like a knowingly false statement;
- DGG's correction policy is not a promise of immunity or a statutory defense; and
- the recording, publication, retention, and correction terms.

## 9. Exact participation statuses

Use only a status supported by the invitation-and-response log.

| Event | Permitted status |
|---|---|
| Participant completed approved Lane A record | “Completed the voluntary sworn-record format on [date].” |
| Participant expressly rejected the format | “Invited [date]; declined participation in this format [date].” |
| Participant did not answer by cutoff | “Invited [date]; no response as of [date].” |
| Participant accepted ordinary interview only | “Participated in an ordinary unsworn interview; did not use the voluntary sworn-record format.” |
| Session failed technically or legally | “A sworn record was not completed.” Add a reason only with documented consent and editorial/legal approval. |

Place this disclaimer next to every decline or no-response status with equal prominence:

> Participation is voluntary. Declining or not responding does not imply dishonesty, falsity, guilt, or unwillingness to answer; reasons may include scheduling, legal advice, or preference for another format.

Prohibited descriptions include “refused to tell the truth,” “failed the oath,” “would not risk perjury,” “notary-verified truth,” and any assertion that an offense occurred without an authoritative adjudication and counsel review.

## 10. Suspected-inaccuracy protocol

If anyone believes a Lane A answer may be inaccurate or outside the participant's knowledge:

1. pause questioning and any stream or publication;
2. do not accuse, coach, negotiate, threaten, or promise immunity;
3. offer correction, qualification, unknown, decline, and counsel access;
4. keep the session open for a lawful same-session correction if the participant and notary agree;
5. preserve the original without alteration, deletion, backdating, or silent replacement;
6. mark all related production material restricted and unpublished;
7. notify authorized counsel under the incident plan;
8. document the pause, choices offered, access, and decisions;
9. create any later correction as an append-only new record; and
10. obtain counsel approval before publication, referral, response to legal process, or destruction.

The notary remains responsible for the legally required journal and audiovisual archive. DGG must not direct their destruction or place them in the public repository.

## 11. Prepublication audit

- [ ] Published record matches the signed version and hash.
- [ ] Certificate is complete, contemporaneous, and correctly associated; venue is the notary's physical Washington location.
- [ ] No post-signing edit is represented as part of the sworn record.
- [ ] DGG production footage is distinct from the notarial archive and within the release.
- [ ] No journal, credential, KBA, seal-security, address, or unnecessary identity data appears.
- [ ] Transcript and captions were checked against the unedited DGG master.
- [ ] Editing does not change the material meaning or create a false implication.
- [ ] Participation status exactly matches the log.
- [ ] Notary limitation and anti-inference disclaimers are adjacent and prominent.
- [ ] Correction or dispute is displayed accurately and append-only.
- [ ] Washington and participant-state counsel approved publication.
- [ ] Symmetry reviewer found no unexplained partisan difference in invitation, prompts, labels, or presentation.

## 12. Phase 0 acceptance test

Run the full workflow with synthetic facts and no public figure. Phase 0 passes only if:

- the notary can complete the approved act and retain required records without transferring restricted notarial data to DGG;
- each signed version, certificate, production master, hash, and access log reconciles;
- an intentional connection interruption triggers the required restart;
- an intentional wrong-record display is detected before signing;
- unknown, qualification, decline, and correction paths all work without pressure;
- the team successfully quarantines a simulated inaccurate answer;
- audience testing understands that notarization does not certify truth;
- the neutral status/disclaimer does not imply dishonesty; and
- counsel signs the go/no-go record.

Failure of any legal, consent, identity, record-integrity, privacy, comprehension, or symmetry control blocks Phase 1.

## 13. Public-repository rule

The repository may contain the audited memorandum, source ledger, gap matrix, blank control documents, redacted examples, and synthetic dry-run data. It must not contain privileged advice, participant contact or identity data, signed live records before authorized publication, raw recordings, notarial journals, identity-proofing artifacts, access credentials, incident files, or legal-hold material.

## 14. Required approvals before live use

| Approval | Minimum scope |
|---|---|
| Washington criminal/notary counsel | Exact act, record, jurat, warning, correction, jurisdiction, preservation, and public claims |
| Media/privacy counsel | Recording, release, editing, publication, security, retention, and incident response |
| Participant-state counsel | Local notary recognition, criminal law, recording, privacy, publicity, defamation, and interstate process |
| Commissioned notary | Commission/endorsements, provider, identity flow, record, certificate, journal, archive, and right to stop |
| DGG editorial lead | Symmetry, exact statuses, disclaimers, corrections, and no misleading implication |

No single approval substitutes for the others. Until all applicable approvals exist, this packet authorizes only synthetic dry runs.
