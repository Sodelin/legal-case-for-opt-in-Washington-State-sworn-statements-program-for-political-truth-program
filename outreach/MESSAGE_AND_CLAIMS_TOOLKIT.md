## Message house

### Core message

> DGG is studying an optional, source-linked way for a speaker to place selected, narrowly framed factual claims into a stable sworn record. The signer declares the statement true under oath. The notary checks required identity and signature, administers the oath or affirmation, and completes the notarial certificate; the notary does not investigate or certify the political claim. DGG will draw and publish no adverse inference from nonparticipation. Because DGG cannot control third-party reactions, Phase 0 publishes no invitation, participation, refusal, or nonresponse status.

### Supporting point 1: provenance

The product is the exact statement, its scope and date, the speaker's knowledge basis, cited sources, version identifier, and correction history—not a “true/false” badge.

### Supporting point 2: separation

Facts suitable for a narrow record remain distinct from opinions, predictions, values, rhetoric, humor, and ordinary unsworn discussion.

### Supporting point 3: falsifiable research

The project tests whether the format improves precision, qualification, correction, or audience calibration without increasing coercion, partisan asymmetry, or false confidence.

### Mandatory limitation

No false answer automatically proves a crime or triggers prosecution. Any possible legal consequence depends on the exact statement, applicable elements, evidence, jurisdiction, and official discretion.

## Ready-to-test copy

These examples are candidate test stimuli, not approved public copy.

### 30-second description

> We are studying a voluntary format that separates a few narrow factual commitments from ordinary political discussion. A willing speaker could sign an exact, source-linked statement and swear or affirm it before an independent notary. The notary would check required identity and signature, administer the oath or affirmation, and complete the certificate—not investigate the political claim. People may qualify, say they do not know, use the unsworn lane, or decline, and no participation status would be published. The current work is a synthetic comprehension test, not a live candidate program.

### 90-second description

> Political interviews routinely combine checkable facts with opinions, predictions, and advocacy. This proposal asks whether a speaker and audience benefit when a few suitable factual claims are placed in a stable record with exact wording, a knowledge basis, sources, a version identifier, and a correction history. A notary could administer and certify a future authorized act but would not fact-check the claim. Washington law supplies possible oath and false-swearing questions, while perjury, jurisdiction, proof, and prosecution require separate elements and decisions. Strong constitutional authority also warns against government truth adjudication, candidate badges, public refusal lists, or coerced pledges. Present Phase 0 is only a private, nonpublic, candidate-neutral fictional mock with no signer, oath, or notarial act. DGG's legal entity, funding, control relationships, and government contacts remain launch gates and have not yet been verified. We are asking reviewers to test the explanation—not to sign or recruit anyone.

## Objection handling

### “Isn't this a government truth commission?”

Present Phase 0 is only a private, nonpublic, candidate-neutral fictional mock with no signer, oath, or notarial act. DGG's entity, funding, control relationships, and government contacts are not yet verified. The design contains no government truth verdict, complaint decision, badge, refusal registry, or benefit/penalty. A later government role would require a new constitutional, forum, disclosure, public-records, and election-law analysis.

### “A notary cannot prove a claim is true.”

Correct. The project must state that prominently. The signer declares truth; the notary checks required identity and signature, administers the oath or affirmation, and completes the certificate. The research question is whether exact provenance and formal commitment are useful—not whether the seal proves the content.

### “This is just a threat of prosecution.”

It should not be marketed that way. Potential Washington false swearing is a fact-specific legal reference point; perjury and other consequences require additional predicates. The format should be valuable even if no one is ever investigated or prosecuted.

### “People can dodge with qualifications or refuse.”

Those options are safeguards, not evasion. The study asks whether they reduce guessing and produce narrower, more accurate commitments. The project must not punish protected silence or opinion.

### “This will be used selectively against one party.”

That is a central adverse hypothesis. Before any later outreach, criteria, prompts, timing, and review must be symmetrical and independently audited. Phase 0 uses no named candidate.

### “Why not make the state run it?”

Government involvement adds constitutional truth-adjudication, compelled-speech, forum, disclosure, public-records, and election-administration problems. The current evidence supports testing a private provenance tool first.

## Comprehension and message-test plan

### Audience cells

Test separately with:

- Washington voters;
- journalists and civic communicators;
- campaign staff or counsel; and
- notary/legal reviewers.

Use only fictional names and facts. Do not use a real candidate's image, voice, statement, party, or race.

### Sequence

1. Begin with an initial planning range of five to eight cognitive interviews per audience cell; a methods reviewer must set any saturation or power rule rather than treating that range as evidence of adequacy.
2. Revise wording based on open-ended paraphrases, not preference alone.
3. Conduct usability and accessibility review.
4. Run a preregistered randomized comparison between the neutral explainer and candidate variants.
5. Repeat cognitive interviews after any material change.

### Must-pass questions

Without looking back, a reader must correctly state that:

- participation and question-level answers are optional;
- declining does not imply dishonesty;
- the notary does not certify factual truth;
- a false answer does not automatically establish perjury or prosecution; and
- the project is research, not a live candidate program.

### Proposed safety threshold

At least 90% correct comprehension on each high-risk proposition, using a predeclared confidence rule, and no meaningful increase in either dangerous misconception relative to neutral control copy. This threshold is a project recommendation—not a legal standard or established universal optimum.

### Additional measures

- unaided main-message recall;
- open-ended paraphrase;
- perceived coercion and unfair surprise;
- perceived partisan favorability and symmetry;
- false confidence in the notary or operator;
- trust calibration rather than raw trust;
- intended action;
- reading time and dropout;
- accessibility problems; and
- privacy concern.

If readers can repeat the disclaimer but still describe the product as a truth certification or refusal test, the copy fails.

| Question | Evidence required | Stop condition |
