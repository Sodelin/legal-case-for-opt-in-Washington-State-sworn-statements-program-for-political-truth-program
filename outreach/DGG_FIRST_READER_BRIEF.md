# A Washington pilot for voluntary sworn factual interviews

## The idea in one paragraph

Offer a willing political guest several concrete factual questions in advance. The guest may revise, qualify, say unknown, or decline. The final factual answers are placed in a fixed record and verified before a properly qualified Washington notary. The show then transitions visibly into ordinary unsworn analysis and opinion. The notary verifies the formal act and identity, not the truth of the political claim.

## Why this might be worth testing

Political interviews often blur direct knowledge, inference, prediction, rhetoric, and opinion. A short sworn record could create a clearer audience signal without forcing anyone to participate or putting the whole interview under oath.

The format is designed to make these outcomes visible:

- a factual answer the guest chose to certify;
- a qualified or limited answer;
- “I do not know” or “I do not recall”;
- an item the guest declined;
- ordinary unsworn commentary;
- a later correction linked to the original.

## Why Washington

Washington notaries may administer oaths and take verifications on oath or affirmation. Washington also has a false-swearing offense distinct from first-degree perjury. That gives the ceremony a plausible legal background, but the exact private political-media application has not been decided by a Washington appellate court.

Before January 1, 2027, a remote pilot should use a final electronic record through a compliant remote-online-notarization platform while the Washington notary remains physically in Washington. A bare Zoom call is not enough. Beginning January 1, 2027, enacted Washington law expressly adds standalone remote oral-oath authority, subject to a current-law and rule check.

## What the proposal does not claim

- The notary does not prove the answer is true.
- Every false answer is not automatically perjury.
- Declining does not prove dishonesty.
- Washington jurisdiction or extradition is not automatic.
- The project is not asking government to compel media participation.
- The goal is not to produce prosecutions.

## Minimum first test

- one low-risk, fully willing participant;
- five factual questions or fewer;
- at least forty-eight hours' advance disclosure;
- counsel-approved record and participant notice;
- experienced Washington remote notary;
- no livestream;
- a five-minute sworn-record block;
- ordinary unsworn interview afterward;
- a public correction process;
- an audience test checking whether viewers understand the notary's limited role.

## The ask

Authorize only a nonpublic tabletop test with Washington counsel and a qualified notary. The repository already contains the audited legal memorandum, source ledger, gap matrix, and control packet. The test should answer whether the process is workable and understandable before anyone treats it as a political or legislative project.
