## Research memorandum under internal adversarial audit remand

**Research current through:** 2026-08-30  
**Prepared for:** Digital Ground Game (DGG) and retained counsel  
**Status:** **REMANDED — CHANGES REQUIRED — NOT LAUNCH-CLEARED.** Phase 0 synthetic/tabletop work only; the counsel gates in Section 17 are necessary but not by themselves sufficient. See [`audit/CANONICAL_STATUS.md`](../audit/CANONICAL_STATUS.md).

> **Legal-advice boundary.** This memorandum identifies a supportable design and the material legal uncertainties. It is not a substitute for advice from Washington counsel, participant-state counsel, the commissioned notary, or DGG's media/privacy counsel about a particular session.

## Question presented

Can DGG use a private, voluntary, opt-in interview format in which willing public figures place a narrow set of factual statements into a Washington-notarized sworn record, and can DGG accurately say that knowingly false statements may have legal consequences without overstating perjury, interstate jurisdiction, or extradition?

## Short answer

**Possibly—but only with a narrower and more guarded theory than the archival drafts sometimes suggest.** Washington already authorizes notaries to administer oaths and take verifications on oath or affirmation. Current law includes a safeguarded record-based remote route, and legislation effective January 1, 2027 expands that framework. While the remand remains open, however, present Phase 0 is a pure fictional mock with no real signer, oath, or notarial act. A nonpublic, in-person, Washington-only paper record is only a possible future actual-test risk control after reviewed supersession and all gates; no DGG remote act is authorized at this stage.

If a participant knowingly makes a false statement in a valid sworn record, Washington **false swearing** is the clearest possible criminal reference point because its statutory elements do not require materiality or an official proceeding. A first-degree **perjury** theory is colorable but not dependable for public messaging: the statute includes a notary in the definition of official proceeding, yet no reported Washington appellate decision located in this review squarely holds that a private media-format session before a notary necessarily qualifies.

The criminal-law theory is only one layer. Territorial jurisdiction, proof beyond a reasonable doubt, limitations and tolling, prosecutorial discretion, and interstate process remain independent gates. DGG cannot create a prosecution by contract, and notarization does not certify that an answer is factually true.

The concept should therefore be evaluated as a **commitment-and-record system** with a possible legal backstop—not a private perjury trap. The present pure mock should model advance factual prompts, a real opt-out, personal-knowledge qualifications, participant-controlled correction before any hypothetical execution, a completely new act for any hypothetical substantive post-execution correction, independent notary control, separate recording/publication consent, evidence preservation, and prepublication review. Those controls are tabletop requirements, not authority to execute an act. No public refusal label is authorized in Phase 0.

# 0. Executive decision brief

## Decision

Proceed only with counsel-reviewed synthetic/tabletop Phase 0 work. While the remand remains open, use only fictional facts in a pure mock with no real signer, oath, or notarial act. A future reviewed supersession may propose an actual test verification, but its minimum design would contain only literally true, personal-knowledge statements about the test itself, with one tangible paper **Canonical Sworn Record** as the sole oath object and a separate unsworn conversation. No signer may ever swear to a fictional role-play assertion. Do not use a named target or public-figure participant, publish a refusal/nonresponse status, conduct a DGG remote act, or launch a free-form “everything said from this point forward is under oath” livestream.

## Why the concept is legally viable

1. Washington law expressly recognizes administering an oath and taking a verification as notarial acts. A verification is a declaration under oath that a statement in a record is true. [RCW 42.45.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.010).
2. Washington false swearing reaches a knowingly false statement under an oath required or authorized by law. An oath administered by a person authorized by state law is within the current definition. [RCW 9A.72.010(5), .040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true).
3. Washington amended that definition in 1995 after an older case had applied a narrower version, then expressly reenacted the amended language without change in 2001 in response to *State v. Thomas*. The authorized-oath-administrator route is the text on which the modern notarial theory relies. [1995 Wash. Laws ch. 285, § 30](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/1995-96/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1557-S2.SL.htm); [2001 Wash. Laws ch. 171, §§ 1–2](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2001-02/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1692.SL.htm).
4. Washington has an established record-based remote-notary framework, but DGG's present Phase 0 permits no actual notarial act. A future actual-test branch may propose an in-person tangible record only after reviewed supersession and all gates. The remote route remains quarantined research rather than an operating instruction. [RCW 42.45.280](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.280); [chapter 308-30 WAC](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=308-30&full=true).
5. Behavioral studies provide a rational basis for a pilot: some voluntary honesty-oath interventions reduced dishonest reporting in controlled online and laboratory settings. The effects varied, were not universal, and have not been validated for political interviews. The correct claim is “worth testing,” not “proven to fix political lying.”

## Five red lines

- Do not say the notary verifies truth.
- Do not say any false statement automatically is perjury.
- Do not imply that declining proves dishonesty.
- Do not promise Washington jurisdiction, prosecution, conviction, or extradition.
- Do not use a 2026 oath-only remote oral interview as the legal core.

## Minimum launch gates

- Washington criminal/notary counsel signs off on the exact record, jurat, script, correction procedure, and public claims.
- Media/privacy counsel signs off on recording consent, release, editing, publication, storage, and incident response.
- Participant-state counsel clears any interstate session.
- The commissioned notary confirms only that the requested act and workflow can be completed lawfully and remains free to refuse or stop; the notary does not approve content or provide DGG legal advice.
- DGG performs a dry run with no public figure and audits the complete record chain.
- Pilot outcomes and adverse effects are preregistered before the first invitation.

# 1. The project DGG should defend

DGG's strongest case is not that ordinary political disagreement should become criminal. The project is a voluntary format that lets a speaker make a narrower choice:

1. place defined, factual, personal-knowledge answers into a sworn record;
2. answer in the ordinary unsworn interview lane; or
3. decline either a particular sworn question or the sworn format altogether.

The distinction matters. Predictions, values, advocacy, interpretation, rhetoric, humor, and opinions are often not suitable for a truth-attestation form. The sworn lane should be limited to propositions capable of being framed with a time, subject, predicate, source-of-knowledge qualifier, and answer set.

No identified refusal status is authorized through Phase 2 under the present control. If a later phase proposes one, it must be reported descriptively, not inferentially. “Declined the sworn-format invitation” may describe a documented event; “refused to tell the truth” adds a defamatory or misleading implication the event does not establish.

If a later phase separately authorizes publication, the project's potential public purpose could be stated without criminal sensationalism. The following is future-phase wording only, not current publication permission:

> DGG would offer public figures a consistent way to separate factual commitments they are willing to swear or affirm from ordinary argument and commentary. The notary would verify the act, identity, signature, and oath—not the truth of the political claim. If authorized after review, DGG would publish the record and its provenance so audiences could judge it for themselves.

# 2. Washington's notarial chassis

## 2.1 What the notary can do

Washington's notarial statute defines a notarial act to include administering an oath or affirmation and taking a verification on oath or affirmation. It defines a verification as a declaration, made by an individual on oath or affirmation before a notarial officer, that a statement in a record is true. A record may be tangible or electronic. [RCW 42.45.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.010).

For a verification, the individual must make the statement and sign the statement in the notary's presence as the law defines presence. [RCW 42.45.030(2)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.030). Identity must be established through personal knowledge or satisfactory evidence, and the notary may demand more information or credentials. [RCW 42.45.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.050).

The notary is an independent legal actor, not DGG's stage prop. The notary may refuse if not satisfied that the participant is competent or has capacity, or that the signature is knowing and voluntary; the notary generally may refuse unless another law prohibits refusal. [RCW 42.45.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.060).

## 2.2 What the notary does not do

The notary does not:

- investigate the underlying political claim;
- warrant that an answer is accurate;
- determine whether an offense occurred;
- decide whether Washington has territorial jurisdiction;
- promise that a prosecutor will act; or
- waive the participant's legal rights.

The notarial certificate evidences the act and certifies the officer's compliance with the determinations specified by law. It does not conclusively prove factual truth, legal sufficiency, capacity, competence, or voluntariness. DGG must keep the certificate's function separate from editorial fact-checking and audience claims. [RCW 42.45.130(5)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.130).

Washington rules prohibit an endorsement or promotion when the notary's title or seal is used. As a broader project-independence policy, DGG must not solicit any notary testimonial, “certified honest” badge, content approval, or promotional notarial branding beyond the certificate and an accurate explanation of the act. [WAC 308-30-230](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=308-30-230).

## 2.3 In-person branch

Washington notaries may administer oaths generally, and the statutory oral-testimony form contemplates a promise to make “true answers.” [Chapter 5.28 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=5.28&full=true). The current pure mock may model—but must not execute—a possible future workflow using a concise tangible record presented and signed before the notary. A fixed record would make the proposition, scope, correction, version, and signature easier to prove and audit. In any later authorized act, personal appearance, identity, certificate, and generally applicable journal requirements would apply. The notary would retain refusal authority if not satisfied about capacity/competence or a knowing and voluntary signature; that would not be a certificate of voluntariness. [RCW 42.45.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.060).

A standalone in-person oral oath is legally stronger than a 2026 remote oath-only theory, but it creates a certificate/proof question: DOL guidance describes the verbal oath as primarily oral, while RCW 42.45.130 states that every notarial act must be evidenced by a certificate. Use an approved fixed-record jurat unless rulemaking, an authoritative interpretation, or controlling law resolves that tension; counsel and an individual notary cannot override RCW 42.45.130.

## 2.4 Current 2026 remote law—quarantined research route

No DGG remote act is authorized by this memorandum's current disposition. If counsel later evaluates a 2026 record-based remote verification, the legal sequence would need to include:

1. The participant receives and reviews a version-identified electronic answer sheet.
2. The participant joins a platform approved by a Washington notary who holds the required commission and electronic-records and remote-notary endorsements.
3. The notary establishes identity through personal knowledge, a qualifying credible witness, or the prescribed two-method identity-proofing route, and completes the communication-technology checks.
4. The participant confirms that the record displayed to the notary is the same record the participant will sign.
5. The participant signs electronically and swears or affirms that the statements in that record are true, subject to the written qualifications.
6. The notary completes the remote jurat, journal entry, and required audiovisual record and secures them for the required period.

Current WAC 308-30-290 lists a remote verification on oath or affirmation but does not list standalone remote administration of an oath. The certificate in WAC 308-30-320 is framed as “signed and sworn” by communication technology. Current RCW 42.45.280 also centers the remote process on a record. [Chapter 308-30 WAC](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=308-30&full=true).

The Washington notary must remain physically in Washington, and the certificate venue is the notary's Washington location—not the participant's location. A generic videoconference is insufficient. The complete system must supply the required endorsements, identity route, same-record confirmation, tamper-evident electronic-signature/certificate association, audiovisual recording, and retention. If the prescribed identity-proofing route is used, credential analysis and dynamic knowledge-based authentication apply. If the participant exits the workflow, identity verification must restart. A participant outside the United States also triggers the transaction-nexus and foreign-law conditions in RCW 42.45.280(3)(d).

This means the 2026 sworn record may summarize or attach defined responses from an interview, but DGG should not claim that every improvised sentence in a free-form remote conversation is independently under a valid Washington oath.

## 2.5 Remote branch beginning January 1, 2027

Substitute HB 2158, enacted as 2026 Washington Laws chapter 21, adds express remote procedures for tangible records and standalone administration of an oath or affirmation beginning January 1, 2027. The House bill report explains that remote oaths were not explicitly addressed in the earlier update. Implementation rulemaking began with CR-101 WSR 26-09-147, filed April 22, 2026; no adopted implementation rule was located by this memorandum's cutoff. [HB 2158 history](https://app.leg.wa.gov/BillSummary/?BillNumber=2158&Year=2026); [House Bill Report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Bill%20Reports/House/2158%20HBR%20CRJ%2026.htm); [WSR 26-09-147](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/WSR/2026/09/26-09-147.htm).

That future authority improves the case for a bounded oral sworn segment. “Standalone” does not facially eliminate RCW 42.45.130's certificate requirement. The expansion also does not eliminate the need for defined questions, recorded consent, identity, retention, territorial-jurisdiction analysis, or a written post-session record. DGG must recheck the RCW, adopted WAC, Department of Licensing guidance, and the notary's platform after the effective date.

# 3. Washington criminal consequences

## 3.1 False swearing is the clearest possible offense

Washington defines false swearing as making a false statement, known by the speaker to be false, under an oath required or authorized by law. It is a gross misdemeanor. The statute does not state a materiality or official-proceeding element. [RCW 9A.72.040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.040).

The Washington Supreme Court confirmed those two omissions in *In re Recall of Pearsall-Stipek*: false swearing contains no materiality limitation and can apply to knowingly false statements under an authorized oath in or out of an official proceeding. [141 Wn.2d 756, 774–78, 10 P.3d 1034 (2000)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wa-supreme-court/1486118.html). The retraction discussion appears at page 779. That was a recall-petition sufficiency decision, not a criminal conviction involving a private media format, so it confirms the elements without deciding DGG's application.

The modern authorized-oath element has two alternative routes: an oath specifically provided by statute or regulation, or an oath administered by a person authorized by state, tribal, or federal law to administer oaths. [RCW 9A.72.010(5)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.010). A properly commissioned Washington notary performing an authorized notarial act supplies a serious textual basis for the second route.

### The adverse case and why it is not ignored

In *State v. Hovrud*, the Court of Appeals reversed a false-swearing conviction arising from an insurance-policy examination because the then-current definition required the oath to be specifically provided by statute or regulation. The court applied strict construction and emphasized fair warning. [60 Wn. App. 573, 805 P.2d 250 (1991)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1991/60-wash-app-573.html).

In 1995, the legislature amended the definition to add the route now covering an oath administered by a legally authorized oath administrator. [1995 Wash. Laws ch. 285, § 30](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/1995-96/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1557-S2.SL.htm). In 2001, it expressly reenacted the definition without change in response to *State v. Thomas*. [2001 Wash. Laws ch. 171, §§ 1–2](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2001-02/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1692.SL.htm). The amended and reenacted text materially strengthens the DGG theory and limits *Hovrud*'s direct application. Even so, no reported appellate case located in this review applies the authorized-administrator route to a voluntary private political-media verification. The memorandum therefore calls false swearing the clearest **possible** offense, not a guaranteed charge.

## 3.2 First-degree perjury is colorable but uncertain

First-degree perjury requires all of the following:

- an official proceeding;
- a materially false statement;
- knowledge that the statement is false; and
- an oath required or authorized by law.

It is a class B felony. Knowledge of materiality is not required, but materiality itself is. [RCW 9A.72.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.020).

The argument for DGG begins with the definition of official proceeding, which includes a proceeding heard before a governmental agency or official authorized to hear evidence under oath, “including” a notary or other person taking testimony or depositions. [RCW 9A.72.010(4)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.010). A structured session in which an authorized notary hears a defined verification is therefore textually colorable.

The limitation is equally important. Research located no controlling Washington decision holding that a privately organized media verification becomes an official proceeding merely because a notary is present. *State v. Jacobson* held that a written affidavit submitted to and used in a court hearing could support perjury because it was given in aid of that official proceeding. [74 Wn. App. 715, 876 P.2d 916 (1994)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1994/74-wash-app-715.html). DGG lacks the court-hearing connection that made *Jacobson* straightforward.

The archival drafts' reliance on *State v. Smith* and *State v. Otton* must also be narrowed. Those opinions interpret “other proceeding” under an evidence rule governing prior inconsistent statements. They do not interpret “official proceeding” in RCW 9A.72.010. They may be used, if at all, as a limited reliability analogy—not as an element-satisfying chain.

Publicly defensible language is:

> A knowingly false statement in a valid Washington sworn record may implicate false swearing. First-degree perjury is a separate, less certain theory that would require proof of materiality and that the particular session qualified as an official proceeding.

Second-degree perjury is not the ordinary DGG theory. It requires either an examination under oath under an insurance contract or a materially false statement made with intent to mislead a public servant in the performance of a duty. An ordinary private interview directed to voters supplies neither branch merely because a notary participates. [RCW 9A.72.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.030).

## 3.3 Mistake, disagreement, and lack of knowledge

Neither offense punishes mere error. The prosecution would have to prove the required falsity and knowledge beyond a reasonable doubt. Ambiguous questions, changing circumstances, reasonable reliance on records or staff, memory failures, predictions, opinions, and disclosed uncertainty can defeat or complicate proof.

At the same time, Washington provides that every unqualified statement of what the speaker does not know to be true is equivalent to a statement the speaker knows to be false. [RCW 9A.72.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.080). DGG must never force a guess. Each prompt should offer:

- “Yes, based on my personal knowledge.”
- “No, based on my personal knowledge.”
- “I do not know and cannot certify this.”
- “I can answer only with the following qualification: ___.”
- “I decline this sworn question.”

## 3.4 Proof and corroboration are separate gates

A valid oath and a provably inaccurate answer do not alone establish a criminal case. Washington applies an unusually demanding corroboration rule to perjury: the State generally needs direct testimony contradicting the oath plus another direct witness or independent corroborating evidence strong enough to be inconsistent with innocence. *State v. Arquette* reversed first- and second-degree perjury convictions because the corroborating evidence did not satisfy that standard. [178 Wn. App. 273, 283–90, 314 P.3d 426 (2013)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/D2%2042546-7-II%20%20Published%20Opinion.pdf).

[WPIC 118.12, “Perjury or False Swearing—Requirements of Proof”](https://govt.westlaw.com/wciji/Document/Iefb2872ae10d11daade1ae871d9b2cbe?contextData=%28sc.Default%29&originationContext=documenttoc&transitionType=CategoryPageItem&viewType=FullText), extends a heightened corroboration instruction to false swearing, but it is committee guidance rather than a binding holding, and this review located no appellate false-swearing conviction squarely deciding that extension. DGG should therefore preserve accurate provenance without suggesting that a contradiction, clip, or fact-check by itself establishes guilt. RCW 9A.72.050's alternative-inconsistent-statement mechanism also depends on material statements made under oath in official proceedings, so DGG cannot assume that special route applies to its private format.

## 3.5 Oath formalities and irregularities

*State v. Lewis* supports a functional oath: no theatrical ritual or fixed religious wording is required when the person deliberately and unequivocally assumes the obligation of an oath. The case involved the notary's absence and a fact-limited irregularity holding; it does not validate defective notarization generally. The correct citation is 85 Wn.2d 769, 539 P.2d 677 (1975), not the conflicting citation in one archival draft. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1975/43613-1.html).

The case is not permission to improvise. Its holding was limited, and RCW 9A.72.070 provides that some oath irregularities or a particular administrator's lack of authority are not defenses when the oath itself was required or authorized by law. A defective session therefore creates uncertainty rather than a reliable “no liability” escape. DGG should use a counsel-selected, statutorily compliant certificate, prefer the statutory short form, and follow a notary-controlled checklist.

## 3.6 Correction and retraction

Washington's statutory retraction protection is narrow. It requires retraction in the same proceeding, before exposure is or will be manifest, and before the falsification substantially affects the proceeding. [RCW 9A.72.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.060). DGG cannot promise that an after-the-fact correction prevents liability.

The workflow should nevertheless create the best honest opportunity to correct:

1. After each answer, the participant may correct or qualify before final verification.
2. Before execution and the oath, the participant reviews and freezes the complete record.
3. Once the record is executed, no one edits or silently replaces it.
4. Any substantive post-execution correction requires a completely new record, signature, oath or affirmation, certificate, and any required journal entry.
5. The new act is append-only linked to the superseded record, and neither DGG policy nor a new act is represented as a guaranteed statutory defense.

This is an accuracy and fairness measure, not a promised safe harbor.

## 3.7 Maximum penalties, limitation periods, and charging discretion

First-degree perjury is a class B felony; the general statutory ceiling is ten years and/or a $20,000 fine. False swearing is a gross misdemeanor; the ceiling is 364 days and/or $5,000. [RCW 9A.20.021](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.20.021). Those are maximum statutory ceilings, not forecasts.

The ordinary limitation period for an otherwise unlisted felony is three years and for a gross misdemeanor is two years. A project-specific exception matters because DGG may approach officeholders: a felony committed by a public officer in connection with official duties, as a breach of public duty, or in violation of the oath of office has a ten-year period. Whether that branch covers a particular statement is fact-dependent; it could affect a first-degree-perjury theory but does not change false swearing's two-year gross-misdemeanor period. Washington's tolling rule stops the clock while the accused is not usually and publicly resident in the state. [RCW 9A.04.080(1)(c)(i), (1)(j)–(k), (2)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.080). In *State v. Willingham*, the Supreme Court distinguished a short trip from relocation; earlier cases applied tolling when the person lived outside Washington. [169 Wn.2d 192, 234 P.3d 211 (2010)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/2010/840369-opn.html). This issue may materially extend exposure for a nonresident, but only if Washington first has jurisdiction over the alleged crime.

Washington prosecutors, not DGG, prosecute criminal actions. [RCW 36.27.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=36.27.020). DGG may preserve a record and respond lawfully to process. It must not threaten a participant with prosecution, bargain over referral, or describe the format as deputized enforcement.

# 4. Interstate participants and Washington territorial jurisdiction

## 4.1 A Washington remote notary does not automatically create Washington criminal jurisdiction

RCW 9A.04.030 includes several possible territorial routes:

- a crime committed wholly or partly in Washington;
- an out-of-state act affecting persons or property in Washington that would be a crime if committed within the state; and
- an express route for specified out-of-state statements under chapter 5.50 RCW that would be perjury if made in Washington.

[RCW 9A.04.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.030).

The special chapter 5.50 route does not say “every statement notarized by a Washington notary.” A DGG jurat should not be publicly described as a jurisdiction-selection device.

The “partly in Washington” and Washington-effects routes are fact-dependent. The notary's physical location, platform servers, DGG's location, intended audience, subject matter, publication, and alleged harm could matter, but no DGG-like appellate decision was found. In *Jacobson*, an out-of-state transfer affected Washington residents' existing property rights and frustrated a Washington court order—a much stronger nexus than ordinary online political speech. [74 Wn. App. at 722–23](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1994/74-wash-app-715.html).

## 4.2 Extradition is a downstream legal possibility, not the product

Chapter 10.88 RCW supplies Washington's extradition procedures, but direction matters. RCW 10.88.250 governs Washington acting as the **asylum state**—Washington's governor may surrender a person found in Washington to another demanding state even when the person was not present there, subject to the statute. It does not determine whether another state will surrender a participant to Washington. For an outgoing Washington demand, RCW 10.88.400–.410 addresses the governor's demand; actual surrender, including discretionary treatment of a person never present in Washington, depends on the asylum state's law. The federal fugitive statute addresses a person who was present and fled. [RCW 10.88.250](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.88.250); [RCW 10.88.400](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.88.400); [RCW 10.88.410](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.88.410); [18 U.S.C. § 3182](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section3182&num=0&edition=prelim). RCW 10.96.060 permits criminal process inside or outside Washington for matters already within RCW 9A.04.030; it does not supply the missing jurisdictional premise. [RCW 10.96.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.96.060).

The accurate sequence is:

1. conduct allegedly satisfies every offense element;
2. Washington has territorial jurisdiction;
3. investigators develop admissible evidence and probable cause;
4. an authorized prosecutor brings a valid charge;
5. required judicial and executive processes occur; and
6. the receiving state's executive and judicial processes address the request and available challenges.

DGG should not estimate the practical likelihood of extradition without evidence from the relevant authorities. “Legally conceivable after multiple independent gates” is the outer claim this research supports.

## 4.3 Interstate launch rule

Before inviting a non-Washington participant, DGG should obtain a short written conflicts review covering:

- law of the participant's location;
- authority and territorial limits of the Washington notary;
- recording consent;
- privacy and publicity rights;
- defamation and false-light rules;
- criminal jurisdiction and limitations;
- professional or campaign-law rules; and
- subpoena, preservation, and cross-border data-transfer issues.

If that review is not complete, use the unsworn interview lane.

# 5. Federal and constitutional boundaries

## 5.1 No default federal perjury or false-statements theory

Federal perjury under 18 U.S.C. § 1621 requires, among other things, an oath in a case in which a law of the United States authorizes the oath. A Washington notarial act for a private interview does not by itself create that federal-law hook. The analysis changes if a statement is made as a qualifying declaration under 28 U.S.C. § 1746 or is submitted in a federally authorized proceeding. [18 U.S.C. § 1621](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1621&num=0&edition=prelim); [28 U.S.C. § 1746](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title28-section1746&num=0&edition=prelim).

The federal false-statements statute, 18 U.S.C. § 1001, generally concerns materially false statements in a matter within federal executive, legislative, or judicial jurisdiction. It is not a general federal ban on lies confined to a private media organization, but routing through a private intermediary does not necessarily foreclose the statute if the statement is intended for or used in a federal matter. [18 U.S.C. § 1001](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1001&num=0&edition=prelim).

DGG should not display federal criminal citations merely to increase perceived stakes.

## 5.2 Private voluntary format and state action

Assuming the unverified private-actor predicate in AS-001, the First Amendment generally restrains government, not a genuinely private media organization offering a voluntary interview format. *Manhattan Community Access Corp. v. Halleck* states the ordinary rule and the limited circumstances in which private action may be attributed to the government. A government license, contract, or monopoly is not enough by itself. [587 U.S. 802, 808–09, 812–14 (2019)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/587BV.pdf).

Applying that rule here is an inference: no controlling case addresses a publisher's use of a state-commissioned notary. The notary performs a legally authorized act, but that should not by itself convert DGG's separate invitations, questions, labels, or editing into state action. The analysis changes if officials direct, coerce, significantly encourage, jointly operate, or become entwined with DGG's decisions. DGG should preserve private editorial control, accept no governmental direction about whom to invite or how to label answers, and reject any exclusivity or access arrangement that turns “voluntary” into a practical government condition.

The format should remain an offer, not a legal prerequisite to ballot access, office, debate access controlled by the state, benefits, licenses, or government press credentials. A government-hosted repository would require a separate classification under *Shurtleff v. Boston*: history, public perception, the government's actual control over messages, and the program's access rules help distinguish government speech from a forum for private speech. [596 U.S. 243, 252–59 (2022)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/20-1800_7lho.pdf). Branding a registry as governmental would not by itself resolve the question.

## 5.3 False political speech is not categorically unprotected

The government does not acquire a general power to punish a political statement merely because it is false. In *United States v. Alvarez*, four Justices rejected a categorical exception for false statements and invalidated the Stolen Valor Act under exacting review; Justices Breyer and Kagan concurred in the judgment using a proportionality-oriented form of intermediate scrutiny. The fractured decision leaves room for carefully confined laws tied to fraud, perjury, legally cognizable harm, or other established contexts. It does not support a general public truth tribunal. [567 U.S. 709, 717–23, 729–36 (2012)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/567bv.pdf).

Washington's cases sharpen the warning. In *Rickert v. Public Disclosure Commission*, five Justices agreed that the challenged campaign-falsity rule could not constitutionally reach the nondefamatory statement at issue; Justice Sanders's broader denunciation of government as political “truth arbiter” spoke for four. In *State ex rel. Public Disclosure Commission v. 119 Vote No! Committee*, five Justices agreed that the earlier statute was invalid as applied, but only three joined the broadest rationale; two concurring Justices reserved the possibility of a narrow, defamatory, actual-malice rule. [161 Wn.2d 843, 848–50, 854–56, 168 P.3d 826 (2007)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/index.cfm?fa=opinions.showOpinion&filename=777691MAJ); [135 Wn.2d 618, 625–28, 957 P.2d 691 (1998)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1998/64332-6-1.html). The separate opinions and exact judgments matter; slogans drawn from a plurality are not holdings of the full court.

Current RCW 29B.30.070 reflects a materially narrower legislative approach: it addresses specified false, defamatory statements of material fact about a clearly identified candidate in political advertising, requires actual malice, and assigns a clear-and-convincing proof burden. It is not a general license to adjudicate political truth. [RCW 29B.30.070](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29B.30.070). *Susan B. Anthony List v. Driehaus* shows how a complaint regime can create pre-enforcement standing and chill, but it is a standing decision, not a merits holding invalidating every campaign-falsity law. [573 U.S. 149, 158–67 (2014)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/573BV.pdf).

An oath changes proof, formality, and potentially the applicable offense elements; it is not a constitutional solvent. No located controlling decision decides whether Washington may apply modern false swearing to this proposed private political-media record. DGG must therefore avoid any operator verdict that a claim is “false,” any public enforcement recommendation, and any suggestion that putting a political assertion under oath categorically removes First Amendment protection.

## 5.4 Do not convert the pilot into a government mandate or coercive pledge

A government requirement that a candidate adopt sworn assertions could compel the candidate's speech; a requirement that a publisher carry answers or prescribed status labels could intrude on editorial judgment. [*303 Creative LLC v. Elenis*, 600 U.S. 570 (2023)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-476_c185.pdf); [*Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo*, 418 U.S. 241, 256–58 (1974)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-418/pdf/USREPORTS-418-241.pdf). These holdings do not invalidate every governmental oath, election filing, or factual disclosure; they show why DGG's proposal should remain a private media practice rather than a proposed qualification for candidacy.

Government also may define the scope of a funded program without ordinarily compelling a participant to affirm the government's position outside that program. [*Agency for International Development v. Alliance for Open Society International, Inc.*, 570 U.S. 205, 214–21 (2013)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/570bv.pdf). The boundary is fact-dependent. Access conditions, funding, official endorsement, or government-directed labels could transform DGG's private invitation into an unconstitutional-conditions or compelled-speech problem.

Nor does the word “voluntary” end the inquiry. In *Ancheta v. Watada*, a federal district court treated an optional candidate pledge together with official identification of nonsigners and a censure mechanism as coercive and chilling. [135 F. Supp. 2d 1114, 1124–29 (D. Haw. 2001)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/135/1114/2503374/). That decision is persuasive only, outside Washington, and requires current citator review. It nevertheless identifies the precise features DGG should exclude: no blacklist, badge, public nonsigner list, censure, interim adverse label, or loss of access for declining.

## 5.5 Identity, disclosure, and a government registry require separate analysis

Political anonymity and association receive constitutional protection. *McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission* invalidated a broad ban on anonymous campaign literature, while *Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta* required narrow tailoring for compelled donor disclosure under exacting scrutiny. [514 U.S. 334, 341–57 (1995)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/514bv.pdf); [594 U.S. 595, 606–19 (2021)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-251_p86b.pdf). These cases do not prohibit a speaker's informed voluntary decision to attach identity to a record.

The counterweight matters. Disclosure regimes can survive when the governmental interests, burdens, safeguards, and fit are established on the actual record; *Doe v. Reed* sustained the challenged petition-disclosure requirement against a facial claim, and *Citizens United v. Federal Election Commission* sustained specified disclaimer and disclosure provisions even while invalidating an expenditure restriction. [561 U.S. 186, 196–202 (2010)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/561bv.pdf); [558 U.S. 310, 366–71 (2010)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/558bv.pdf). DGG therefore should not claim that all identity disclosure is unconstitutional. It should say that a government registry, compelled participation, public-record status, retention, security, and associational burden require their own narrow, evidence-based design and review.

## 5.6 Editorial choice is not immunity from defamation or deception

A private publisher ordinarily chooses whom and what to publish, but edited context or material omissions can create a provably false factual implication. Candidates generally must satisfy the constitutional actual-malice standard for defamatory candidate coverage, yet that is a fault standard, not immunity. [*New York Times Co. v. Sullivan*, 376 U.S. 254, 279–80 (1964)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-376/pdf/USREPORTS-376-254.pdf); [*Monitor Patriot Co. v. Roy*, 401 U.S. 265, 271–72 (1971)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-401/pdf/USREPORTS-401-265.pdf).

Washington's more precise rule is that literal accuracy does not foreclose liability if the complete presentation creates a provably false factual impression. Material omissions can create such an impression, but mere negative inference, omission of favorable context, or juxtaposition of substantially true facts generally is insufficient. False-light analysis likewise requires falsity, although an implied provable fact can qualify. [*Sisley v. Seattle Public Schools*, 180 Wn. App. 83 (2014)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/693166.pdf); [*Jha v. Khan*, 24 Wn. App. 2d 377 (2022)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/837681.pdf); [*Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.*, 497 U.S. 1, 18–21 (1990)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-497/pdf/USREPORTS-497-1.pdf); [*Masson v. New Yorker Magazine*, 501 U.S. 496, 516–17 (1991)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-501/pdf/USREPORTS-501-496.pdf). If a later phase authorizes statuses, DGG should apply uniform invitations, publish the exact status, preserve the invitation and response, and place an anti-inference disclaimer adjacent to every status:

> “Participation is voluntary. Declining or not responding does not imply dishonesty, falsity, guilt, or unwillingness to answer; reasons may include scheduling, legal advice, or preference for another format.”

Use distinct facts:

- Explicit rejection: “Invited [date]; declined participation in this format [date].”
- Silence: “No response as of [date].”
- Never infer: “refused to swear,” “would not tell the truth,” “failed,” or “would not risk perjury.”

The disclaimer reduces contextual risk; it is not a safe harbor and cannot cure a knowingly inaccurate status or a presentation designed to insinuate dishonesty.

## 5.7 Broadcast, press-exemption, coordination, and debate triggers

An ordinary website or private online interview has no general federal candidate-use rule. If DGG uses regulated distribution, however, counsel must analyze “equal opportunities,” bona fide news exceptions, and comparable opportunity under the facts—not promise identical airtime or assume an interview exemption. The framework can reach broadcast-station uses and separate cable-system, DBS, and SDARS origination rules. [47 U.S.C. § 315(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title47/pdf/USCODE-2024-title47-chap5.pdf); [47 C.F.R. § 73.1941](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-73/subpart-H/section-73.1941); [47 C.F.R. § 76.205](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-76/subpart-G/section-76.205); [FCC Media Bureau DA 26-68](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-68A1.pdf).

Federal campaign-finance treatment also depends on DGG's ownership, control, payment, candidate involvement, and actual format. The contribution and expenditure press exemptions can cover qualifying news, commentary, and editorial activity, including website or Internet distribution, but are narrowed when the facility is candidate-, party-, or political-committee-owned or controlled. Coordination requires the payer, content, and conduct elements; formal agreement is unnecessary, and “materially involved” is the regulatory phrase. A one-on-one interview series is not automatically a candidate debate; the debate rule requires at least two candidates, no structure promoting one, and pre-established objective selection criteria. [52 U.S.C. § 30101(8)(B)(i), (9)(B)(i)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title52/pdf/USCODE-2024-title52-subtitleIII-chap301-subchapI.pdf); [11 C.F.R. § 100.73](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-100/subpart-C/section-100.73); [11 C.F.R. § 100.132](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-100/subpart-E/section-100.132); [11 C.F.R. § 109.21](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-109/subpart-C/section-109.21); [11 C.F.R. § 110.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-110/section-110.13).

Before inviting candidates, DGG must document its entity and tax status, ownership, payment sources, paid amplification, candidate contacts, and application of each payer/content/conduct and distribution rule. Washington campaign-law classifications and adjusted thresholds require a separate Title 29B decision tree; WAC 390-05-400 adjusts RCW 29B.25.120(2)'s printed $100 amount to $1,000 effective January 1, 2026. [WAC 390-05-400](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=390-05-400). This memorandum does not complete that fact-specific analysis.

# 6. Pennsylvania comparator

Pennsylvania is a useful **notarial-mechanics analogue**, not a parallel private criminal-penalty jurisdiction.

Pennsylvania's RULONA authorizes verifications on oath, certificates, and remote notarial acts. A Pennsylvania notary located in Pennsylvania may remotely notarize for a signer elsewhere in the United States. Identity may rest on personal knowledge, a qualifying credible witness, or the prescribed identity-proofing route; electronic-certificate association must be tamper-evident, and the audiovisual record generally is retained for at least ten years. Final-form remote-notary regulations took effect March 28, 2026. [57 Pa.C.S. §§ 302, 304–306.1, 315, 320](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/57/57.HTM); [4 Pa. Code ch. 167, subch. H](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/004/chapter167/subchapHtoc.html); [Pennsylvania Bulletin](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pabulletin/data/vol56/56-13/438.html).

Pennsylvania also recognizes a valid notarial act performed under another state's law by an authorized notary. [57 Pa.C.S. § 311](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute&txtType=PDF&ttl=57). That recognition gives the act notarial effect; it does not import Washington criminal law or establish Washington territorial jurisdiction.

The criminal comparison is materially different:

- Pennsylvania perjury requires a materially false sworn statement in an official proceeding.
- “Official proceeding” is governmental, and testimony or a deposition taken before a notary must be connected to such a proceeding.
- Pennsylvania false swearing generally requires an official proceeding, an intent to mislead a public servant, or a statement that law requires to be sworn before an authorized officer.
- Pennsylvania unsworn falsification likewise depends on specified governmental or legally authorized gateways.

[18 Pa.C.S. §§ 4501, 4902–4904](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute&txtType=PDF&ttl=18).

As a statutory inference rather than a located appellate holding, a voluntary private DGG verification ordinarily does not fit Pennsylvania perjury or false swearing merely because it is notarized. Territorial jurisdiction under 18 Pa.C.S. § 102 cannot supply a missing offense element. Facts can change if the statement is submitted to government, used in an official proceeding, or required by law. DGG should not print “under penalty of Pennsylvania perjury/false swearing” unless Pennsylvania counsel identifies an independently applicable required oath or government proceeding.

Pennsylvania remains a credible operational alternative if DGG wants identity assurance, oath formality, remote appearance, and provenance without relying on a private criminal-deterrence theory. The public description should be “signed and affirmed before a notary,” not “facts verified by the notary.”

# 7. Recording, privacy, publication, and evidence governance

## 7.1 Washington requires a separate recording analysis

Washington generally requires the consent of all parties to record a private conversation or communication. Consent may be established by a recorded announcement to all parties when the announcement itself is recorded. [RCW 9.73.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.030). An unlawfully obtained recording is generally inadmissible, and chapter 9.73 provides civil remedies. [RCW 9.73.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.050); [RCW 9.73.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.060).

The remote-notary law's required audiovisual archive does not automatically authorize DGG to publish it. DGG should use two conceptually separate records:

| Record | Controller | Purpose | Publication default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notarial audiovisual archive and original journal | Notary or authorized repository under statute/contract; the notary remains responsible | Proof and compliance for the notarial act | DGG policy: never publish by default |
| DGG production master | DGG under participant release | Editing, publication, provenance, corrections | Publish only within release and policy |

The notarial audiovisual file must be secured against unauthorized access, backed up, retained for at least ten years, and must not include images of the electronic record that was notarized. [WAC 308-30-330](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=308-30&full=true).

The original notarial journal is the notary's exclusive property and generally must be secured under the notary's direct and exclusive control. A Washington attorney-notary need not journal an act if professional-practice documentation is otherwise maintained, and the ordinary ten-year journal period runs from the last act chronicled in the journal. As a stricter DGG privacy policy, DGG must not possess, photograph, publish, or place journal material in its repository. Likewise, the notary ordinarily may not keep a copy of the participant's record solely for the notarial transaction; DGG's retention of its own publication record must rest on a distinct release and custody policy. [RCW 42.45.180](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.180).

## 7.2 Consent should be layered and recorded

Before substantive discussion, the production master should capture affirmative consent to:

1. live audio and video communication;
2. the notary's legally required separate recording and retention;
3. DGG's production recording;
4. editing that does not materially alter meaning;
5. publication, excerpts, transcripts, captions, and syndication;
6. preservation of the original and later corrections;
7. identified security and retention periods; and
8. response to valid legal process.

Written releases should match the spoken consent. If any participant withdraws recording consent before the sworn act, stop and move to a nonrecorded option only if counsel and the notary approve; a remote notarial act generally cannot proceed without its required audiovisual record.

## 7.3 Data minimization

As a data-minimization and security policy, DGG should never receive or retain identity-proofing questions, credential images, knowledge-based-authentication answers, notary journals, seal credentials, or more personal data than the law and the production require. WAC 308-30-300 separately requires that the notary not be able to see or record dynamic knowledge-based-authentication questions or answers. The public repository must contain only blank forms, approved redacted samples, legal research, and synthetic dry-run data.

# 8. Recommended pilot architecture

## 8.1 Three clearly separated lanes for synthetic/tabletop testing

### Lane A — sworn factual record

- Questions delivered in advance.
- Only defined, falsifiable, personal-knowledge propositions.
- Participant may qualify, say “I do not know,” or decline any item.
- Final answers appear in one versioned, tangible paper Canonical Sworn Record.
- Only the exact CSR is the oath object; no transcript, source packet, production record, annotation, or later commentary is incorporated by reference.
- Counsel-selected compliant verification certificate; statutory short form preferred.
- No real signer, notarial act, participant/program publication, or named target/public-figure participant while the remand remains open; if a future supersession authorizes an actual test, the signer's identity and notarial records remain outside the public repository.

### Lane B — unsworn conversation

- Analysis, policy, advocacy, predictions, values, explanation, and follow-up.
- Visually and verbally separated from Lane A.
- Never marketed as under oath.

### Lane C — declined sworn format

- The participant may still take the ordinary interview.
- No identified invitation, decline, or nonresponse is published through Phase 2 under the present control.
- If a later phase considers a public status, it requires a new defamation, false-light, campaign, editorial, and methods review.

## 8.2 Question-design rule

A proposed sworn question should pass all of these checks:

- **Falsifiability:** Can evidence show the statement true or false?
- **Time:** What date or period does it concern?
- **Subject:** Who or what is the actor/object?
- **Predicate:** What precisely occurred or exists?
- **Knowledge:** Is the participant attesting personally, from specified records, or on information and belief?
- **Scope:** Are material exceptions disclosed?
- **Vocabulary:** Are key terms defined without partisan framing?
- **Answerability:** Can the participant truthfully choose “unknown,” qualify, or decline?
- **Relevance:** Why does the public need this sworn rather than ordinarily reported?
- **Symmetry:** Would DGG ask the same formulation of a similarly situated person with different politics?

If a prompt fails any check, move it to Lane B.

## 8.3 Form structure

Each record should contain:

1. record ID, version, date, and hash;
2. participant name and capacity, using no unnecessary identifiers;
3. purpose and voluntary-choice notice;
4. definitions and covered time period;
5. each prompt and the participant's exact answer;
6. source-of-knowledge and qualification field;
7. explicit “unknown” and “decline” choices;
8. pre-execution correction/retraction opportunity;
9. oath or affirmation language;
10. participant signature and timestamp;
11. counsel-selected compliant in-person notarial certificate, with the statutory short form preferred; and
12. a public explanation that notarization does not prove truth.

# 9. Participant notice—minimum substance

The notice should be written in plain language and separately acknowledged:

> Participation is voluntary. You may refuse the sworn format, refuse any question, consult counsel, ask for a break, say that you do not know, or add a qualification. DGG will not describe a refusal as proof of dishonesty or illegality.
>
> A Washington notary will verify the notarial act, your identity as required, your signature, and your oath or affirmation. The notary does not investigate or certify whether your factual answers are true.
>
> A knowingly false statement under an oath required or authorized by Washington law may create criminal exposure, including possible false swearing. First-degree perjury and Washington jurisdiction require additional elements. No charge or enforcement result is automatic, and DGG does not control prosecutors, courts, or interstate process.
>
> Washington treats an unqualified statement about something you do not know to be true like a knowingly false statement for this chapter. Use the “I do not know,” qualification, or decline options whenever appropriate.
>
> Correction rights under DGG policy are broader than any statutory defense. Correct or qualify anything inaccurate before execution. A substantive correction after execution requires a completely new notarial act and does not necessarily prevent liability.

Counsel should adapt the notice for each participant's location and the final procedure.

# 10. Accidental-trigger and correction protocol

If anyone believes a sworn answer may be inaccurate, inconsistent, outside the participant's knowledge, or misunderstood:

1. **Pause immediately.** Stop substantive questioning and do not publish or stream the segment.
2. **Do not accuse or coach.** The host may identify the ambiguity but must not supply a preferred factual answer.
3. **Return control to the participant and notary.** Ask whether the participant wants to correct, qualify, retract, decline, or consult counsel.
4. **Respect the execution boundary.** Before execution, allow participant-controlled correction and refreeze the complete record. After execution, do not edit the CSR; any substantive correction requires a completely new act and must not be promised as a statutory defense.
5. **Preserve the original.** Do not delete, overwrite, backdate, or silently substitute the original record, journal entry, or recordings.
6. **Segregate records.** The notary controls the legal archive; DGG controls its production copy. Each follows its own retention and access rules.
7. **Mark the item paused and unpublished.** Restrict access to need-to-know legal and security staff.
8. **Notify counsel.** Obtain Washington and participant-state advice before further questioning, publication, referral, or response to a third party.
9. **Create an append-only correction.** If publication later occurs, display the original and correction with timestamps and an explanation that does not adjudicate guilt.
10. **Document decisions.** Record who paused the session, what choices were offered, and why the final handling occurred.

No DGG employee should threaten referral, negotiate silence, destroy a record, or promise immunity.

# 11. Why a guarded pilot is worth doing

## 11.1 The legally honest value proposition

The format creates several benefits even if no criminal case is ever brought:

- a defined record rather than a moving clip;
- source-of-knowledge and scope qualifications;
- a visible boundary between fact and advocacy;
- a structured opportunity to say “unknown” rather than guess;
- a correction chain that preserves rather than rewrites history;
- consistent invitations and response labels; and
- data about whether speakers and audiences find the distinction useful.

## 11.2 Empirical support is promising but not transportable without testing

A 2025 *Nature Human Behaviour* megastudy tested 21 honesty-oath variants against a no-oath condition with 21,506 UK and US participants in an incentivized tax-reporting game. Ten variants significantly improved compliance by 4.5 to 8.5 percentage points; content mattered. [Zickfeld et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02009-0).

A 2021 online crowd-work experiment collected 1,410 observations and analyzed 1,366 after exclusions. A voluntary oath reduced payoff-maximizing reports of ten heads from 17.8% to 12.9%—4.9 percentage points, or about 27% relative—modestly reduced mean reporting from 6.33 to 6.06, and produced a nonsignificant change in shirking from 42% to 40%. Ten heads can occur truthfully, so the result is not a direct count of lies. The authors called the oath no panacea. [Jacquemet et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0244958).

A laboratory study found larger oath effects when the context expressly made the moral meaning of lying salient and weaker effects in a neutral setting. [Jacquemet et al., *Management Science*](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2892).

These results justify a falsifiable DGG pilot hypothesis. They do not establish effects for politicians, adversarial interviews, public reputational stakes, or criminal warnings. DGG should treat chilling, legalistic evasion, speaker selection, and audience overtrust as plausible adverse hypotheses to preregister and test—not as findings of the cited studies.

## 11.3 Preregistered outcomes

DGG should preregister at least:

- invitation and uptake rates by political/ideological category;
- question-level decline, unknown, and qualification rates;
- immediate and later correction rates;
- factual precision and source citation compared with matched unsworn interviews;
- audience comprehension that notarization does not certify truth;
- audience confidence calibration and false-confidence rate;
- participant perception of coercion or unfair surprise;
- notary refusal, technical failure, and privacy incident rates;
- editing disputes and correction turnaround; and
- evidence of unequal administration or partisan selection.

Success should require both improved factual discipline **and** no material increase in false audience confidence, coercion, or asymmetric treatment.

| Defamatory refusal implication | Medium to high | No identified status through Phase 2; later use requires fresh review, exact facts, and anti-inference controls |
| Unlawful or overbroad recording | High | All-party recorded consent plus written layered release |
| Publication of notarial archive | High | Segregate archive; no-publication default; strict access control |
| Participant guesses under pressure | High | Unknown/qualification/decline options; no surprise sworn questions |
| Ineffective later correction | Medium | Immediate same-session opportunity; append-only later correction |
| Evidence spoliation or chain dispute | High after incident | Hashing, immutable originals, access logs, legal hold process |
| Audience overtrust in notarization | High | Repeated disclaimer; comprehension testing as pilot guardrail |
| Partisan or selective administration | High to mission legitimacy | Published criteria, mirrored prompts, external audit |
| Notary conflict or loss of independence | Medium | Independent contractor terms; notary controls the act and may refuse |
| Criminal-threat coercion | High | Plain notice, counsel access, no referral threats, separate host/notary roles |

# 14. Systematic-review and process-integrity statement

This research used a consequential-claim method rather than citation accumulation:

1. Extracted each claim capable of changing launch, liability, or public messaging.
2. Prioritized Washington statutes, session laws, regulations, official guidance, and court opinions.
3. Searched adverse cases and statutory history, including *Hovrud*, the 1995 amendment, and the 2001 express reenactment.
4. Separated evidence-rule cases from criminal-element cases.
5. Separated present law from law effective January 1, 2027.
6. Treated territorial jurisdiction, offense elements, proof, charging, and extradition as independent gates.
7. Used behavioral studies only for the bounded empirical proposition that a pilot is rational.
8. Preserved unresolved issues rather than converting textual arguments into holdings.

No empirical meta-analysis was performed for this legal memorandum. The behavioral studies use different tasks, incentives, populations, and oath formulations; their results are not pooled here. The operational hypothesis should be tested prospectively in DGG's own setting.

# 15. Robustness and adversarial critique

The strongest critique is that DGG may add criminal symbolism without adding truth. A strategic participant could avoid falsehood through vague answers, selective qualification, or refusal. Audiences could mistake a valid jurat for government verification. Prosecutors may have no interest, and Washington may lack jurisdiction over the participants DGG most wants to invite.

Those critiques do not defeat a narrow pilot; they define its success conditions. DGG should proceed only if the product remains valuable when:

- no one is prosecuted;
- first-degree perjury is unavailable;
- many guests choose “unknown” or decline;
- interstate sessions are limited;
- the notarial archive is never published; and
- audience testing shows the disclaimer is understood.

If the format's value depends on viewers believing prosecution is easy or notarization establishes truth, the project should stop. If it improves precision, qualifications, provenance, corrections, and audience calibration without coercion or false confidence, it can succeed on a much more durable ground.

# 16. Recommendation

DGG should authorize only a **Phase 0 counsel-and-synthetic/tabletop build**. Any later pilot remains subject to cure of the internal adversarial audit remand and a new approval record.

### Phase 0 — legal and technical validation

- final Washington counsel opinion on the exact form and false-swearing language;
- participant-state checklist;
- independent notary consulted on lawful completion/refusal, without asking the notary to approve content, select the certificate for DGG, or certify voluntariness;
- layered consent/release and privacy schedule;
- pure mock using fictional facts with no oath or notarial act; separately document, but do not execute, any proposed actual-test branch until a reviewed supersession closes the remand and limits it to literally true, personal-knowledge statements about the test itself;
- accidental-trigger tabletop exercise;
- immutable record/hash and correction-chain test;
- comprehension test for the public disclaimer; and
- external symmetry/bias review.

### Phase 1 — deferred and not authorized

No Phase 1 activity begins until a reviewed supersession closes every mandatory remand item. Any later proposal must use the approved exact architecture, no livestream, a small number of advance prompts, a separately approved participant/location configuration, counsel review before any publication, full methodology/limitations, and a preregistered evaluation.

### Phase 2 — decision

Expand only if the pilot improves factual precision or audience calibration, the disclosure is understood, refusals remain neutral, and no serious legal, privacy, notary-independence, or partisan-symmetry failure occurs.

# 17. Counsel gates before any live use

Washington counsel must answer in writing:

1. Does the exact proposed oath/verification satisfy current RCW 42.45 and chapter 308-30 WAC?
2. Is the 2026 fixed-record branch correctly separated from the 2027 standalone remote-oath branch?
3. How should *Hovrud* and the 1995 amendment be stated in participant notice?
4. Is the false-swearing description fair and sufficiently noncoercive?
5. Can the particular implementation plausibly be an official proceeding, and may DGG mention that perjury theory publicly?
6. What Washington nexus is required for each participant/location configuration?
7. How do limitations and nonresident tolling apply?
8. What correction, preservation, and referral procedures minimize obstruction, spoliation, or witness-related risk?
9. Does the recording/release architecture comply with all applicable jurisdictions?
10. What facts would require DGG to stop, preserve, notify, or obtain emergency advice?

No document should be labeled “launch-cleared” until the internal adversarial audit remand is closed through a reviewed supersession and these answers, the participant-state review, and the notary's limited written workflow confirmation are stored outside the public repository.

# Conclusion

Washington law gives DGG enough legal infrastructure to continue a serious Phase 0 evaluation, not to claim launch readiness. The modern false-swearing text is stronger than the older adverse case because the legislature added an authorized-oath-administrator route in 1995 and expressly reenacted it in 2001. Current law contains a safeguarded fixed-record remote path and the 2027 law expands the framework, but present Phase 0 remains a pure fictional mock with no signer, oath, or notarial act. A nonpublic, in-person, Washington-only tangible-paper design is merely a future branch requiring reviewed supersession and every gate.

The defensible argument is therefore conditional and disciplined: a voluntary, provenance-rich sworn record may be viable after the remand and all fact-specific approvals are resolved. DGG must not sell notarization as truth certification or describe perjury, prosecution, or interstate surrender as expected. The project's civic value should be judged by better factual commitments, clearer qualifications, durable corrections, and better-calibrated audiences—not by whether anyone is prosecuted.
