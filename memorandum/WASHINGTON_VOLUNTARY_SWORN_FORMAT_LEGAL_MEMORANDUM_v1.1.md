# Washington Voluntary Sworn-Record Format

## Legal memorandum and decision brief — candidate v1.1

**Client/project:** Digital Ground Game (DGG) voluntary factual-record pilot  
**Law checked through:** August 30, 2026  
**Status:** Research memorandum; not legal advice, launch clearance, or a prediction that any offense will be charged  
**Supersedes for design purposes:** the supplied August 29, 2026 memorandum, subject to counsel review  
**Companion:** `operations/DGG_PARTICIPANT_SWORN_STATEMENT_PROTOCOL_v1.1.md`

> **Controlling limitation.** A Washington notary can certify a notarial act involving a signed record. The notary does not investigate or certify the truth of the participant's statements. No refusal, qualification, correction, or choice not to participate is evidence of dishonesty.

## Question presented

Can DGG build a voluntary format in which a participant adopts a short, exact factual record under oath before a Washington notary, while keeping the ordinary interview unsworn, and can the format be defended as a useful public-accountability tool without overstating criminal law, notarial effect, or empirical evidence?

## Short answer

**Yes, as a carefully bounded research pilot.** Washington's notary statute expressly recognizes a “verification on oath or affirmation”: a declaration before a notarial officer that a statement **in a record** is true. It authorizes notarial officers to perform notarial acts and requires the officer taking a verification to identify the appearing signer and the signature. Those provisions supply a strong statutory basis for a participant-authored, signed factual record with a contemporaneous jurat. [RCW 42.45.010(13), (18), .020, .030(2), .130–.140](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.45&full=true).

Washington false-swearing law supplies a **plausible but judicially untested** criminal-law backstop. False swearing requires a statement the speaker knows is false, made under an oath required or authorized by law. An oath is authorized when a statute specifically provides for it **or** when it is administered by a person whom state or federal law authorizes to administer oaths. [RCW 9A.72.010(5), .040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true). That text supports the argument that a properly administered notarial verification can qualify. No located Washington appellate decision applies that theory to a privately produced political-media record, however. DGG therefore may say only that knowingly false sworn statements **may** have legal consequences; it must not advertise automatic false-swearing liability.

Washington separately lists notaries public among the officers who may administer oaths and affirmations generally. [RCW 5.28.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=5.28.010). That reinforces the authorized-officer route; it does not resolve the remaining offense elements or the private-media application.

First-degree perjury is a materially weaker product claim. Its elements add materiality and an “official proceeding.” The statutory definition includes a proceeding before a notary or other person taking testimony, which creates a colorable textual argument. But no located decision holds that an ordinary private notarization created for a media project is such a proceeding, and materiality asks whether the statement could affect that proceeding's course or outcome. [RCW 9A.72.010(2), (4), .020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true). Perjury should remain a counsel-only issue, not a label, invitation hook, or promotional promise.

The strongest design is consequently **criminal-law quiet**: the civic product is an exact, versioned, source-linked factual commitment with visible uncertainty and append-only correction. Possible criminal consequences are a neutral disclosure controlled by counsel, not the project's value proposition.

## Recommendation

Authorize only:

1. **Phase 0:** synthetic dry runs and legal/technical tabletop testing.
2. **Phase 1:** a nonpublic, in-person Washington feasibility pilot using a tangible, wet-ink Canonical Sworn Record (CSR), with every captured person physically in Washington.
3. **Phase 2:** preregistered audience-safety and speaker-process experiments using synthetic or fully consented materials.

Do **not** authorize a named public-figure pilot, public nonparticipation status, livestream, electronic-record notarization, remote notarization, free-form “interview under oath,” or criminal-liability marketing until the specified counsel, notary, entity, campaign, recording, privacy, and empirical gates close.

# I. The legal object and its boundaries

## 1. The CSR is the only sworn object

The **Canonical Sworn Record** is a short tangible record containing:

- record ID, version, date, page count, and numbered proposition IDs;
- defined factual questions and the participant's exact answers;
- express choices for “I do not know,” “I cannot verify,” qualification, and question-specific decline;
- a source-of-knowledge designation for each adopted proposition;
- an adoption clause identifying the exact pages and proposition IDs sworn;
- the participant's wet-ink signature; and
- a contemporaneous Washington verification certificate/jurat securely attached to the record.

Only the numbered answers and their express qualifications are sworn. Definitions and any fact expressly incorporated into an answer may be part of the sworn payload if counsel makes that boundary unmistakable. Administrative metadata, DGG source annotations, contrary sources, editorial notes, and the public-media record must be marked **not part of the sworn statement**.

The participant—not DGG—authors or expressly adopts each answer. Counsel may assist the participant. DGG may format adopted language and test whether a proposition is clear and falsifiable, but it may not supply substantive language, pressure a guess, delete a material qualification, or convert an unsworn answer into a sworn one.

## 2. The ordinary interview remains unsworn

The **Public Media Record** (PMR) is the video, audio, transcript, host commentary, source explanation, analysis, predictions, advocacy, and policy discussion. It is unsworn. The PMR must never imply that the oath extends beyond the exact CSR.

The separation is substantive, visual, verbal, and temporal:

1. **Green — unsworn preparation:** draft, qualify, consult, decline, and review.
2. **Amber — freeze:** identify the final paper CSR, pages, proposition IDs, and pre-sign manifest; no substantive edits after the freeze.
3. **Red — notarial act:** the independent notary controls appearance, identification, oath or affirmation, signature, certificate, and journal.
4. **Green — closed:** the host states that the sworn segment has ended; every later statement is unsworn unless a completely new CSR and notarial act occur.

This “sworn airlock” prevents the audience, participant, editor, and later fact-finder from having to guess which minutes of a long interview were under oath.

## 3. The first pilot uses paper

Phase 1 must use a tangible wet-ink record. An electronic record requires an electronic-records notary commission and a selected tamper-evident technology even when the signer and notary share a room. [RCW 42.45.190](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.190). A normal PDF plus an ordinary e-signature is not an approved substitute.

The paper original is the canonical legal object. A scan is a derivative used for quality control or later publication; its hash identifies only those scan bytes. The project must not claim that a hash authenticates identity, proves truth, makes paper immutable, or replaces the notarial act.

# II. Washington notarial authority

## 4. Verification on oath fits a statement in a record

Chapter 42.45 RCW defines a record broadly to include information on a tangible medium and defines verification on oath or affirmation as an individual's declaration before a notarial officer that a statement in a record is true. RCW 42.45.020 authorizes a notarial officer to perform notarial acts, and RCW 42.45.030(2) requires the officer taking a verification to determine the identity of the appearing individual and that the signature on the verified statement is that individual's signature. [Chapter 42.45 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.45&full=true).

That is the memorandum's strongest legal foundation. The protocol does not need to invent a new category of “truth interview.” It uses a familiar notarial act on a deliberately narrow record.

## 5. The certificate certifies compliance, not truth

A notarial act must be evidenced by a contemporaneously executed certificate. The certificate identifies the jurisdiction, officer, date, office, and required stamp or electronic association. By executing it, the officer certifies compliance with the statutory appearance, identity, signature, and related determinations. [RCW 42.45.130](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.130).

The certificate does not say that the notary investigated the sources or found an answer true. DGG's approved description is:

> The participant signed the identified statement under oath before a Washington notary. The notary certified the required notarial act; the notary did not investigate or certify the statement's factual truth.

Counsel—not a nonlawyer notary—must select and approve the exact declaration and certificate. Washington restricts a nonlawyer notary from drafting legal records, giving legal advice, or helping another person select the certificate. [RCW 42.45.230(1), (4)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.230).

## 6. Independence and record segregation

At minimum, a notary may not act on a record in which the notary or specified close relations are parties or have a direct beneficial interest. [RCW 42.45.020(2)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.020). DGG should impose a stricter product rule: fixed, disclosed compensation; no editorial, campaign, participant, or outcome interest; and sole notary control of the notarial act.

The notary's journal is separate from the CSR and PMR. Washington generally requires contemporaneous journal entries, ten-year retention, and direct exclusive notary control. The journal includes identifying information and must never be placed in DGG's public repository or production footage. [RCW 42.45.180](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.180).

# III. Criminal-law theory and its limits

## 7. False swearing is the strongest possible offense theory

RCW 9A.72.040 states that a person commits false swearing by making a false statement, knowing it is false, under an oath required or authorized by law. RCW 9A.72.010(5) provides two authorization routes: the oath is specifically provided for by statute or rule, **or** it is administered by a person whom state, tribal, or federal law authorizes to administer oaths. [Chapter 9A.72 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true).

The proposed CSR has a coherent textual path:

1. Washington notary law specifically recognizes verification on oath concerning a statement in a record.
2. A commissioned notary is authorized to take that verification.
3. The signer deliberately adopts exact propositions and signs them before the notary.
4. If an adopted proposition was false and the signer knew it was false, the statutory elements may be implicated.

This is an argument from enacted text, not a holding about DGG. Neither the supplied research nor the independent review located a reported Washington appellate case applying RCW 9A.72.040 to a voluntary private political-media verification. Questions may remain about the oath, representation, proof of knowledge, venue, jurisdiction, constitutional application, and prosecutorial discretion.

The safe public formulation is therefore: **“A knowingly false statement made under a Washington-authorized oath may have legal consequences.”** Do not say “this creates false-swearing liability,” much less “this guarantees prosecution.”

## 8. Knowledge and the need for qualification choices

Washington equates an unqualified statement of what a speaker does not know to be true with a statement the speaker knows is false. [RCW 9A.72.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.080). The protocol must make uncertainty easier to express than guessing. Every proposition therefore offers:

- “true from personal knowledge”;
- “true based on the identified record(s)”;
- a participant-authored qualification;
- “I do not know / cannot verify”;
- “I do not recall”; and
- “I decline this proposition.”

These are accuracy controls, not demerits. A public scoring system that punishes an unknown or qualification would undermine the protocol's truth-seeking design.

## 9. First-degree perjury is colorable but unresolved

First-degree perjury requires a materially false statement, known to be false, under an authorized oath, in an official proceeding. “Materially false” means capable of affecting the course or outcome of the proceeding. The definition of official proceeding includes a proceeding before a notary or other person taking testimony. [RCW 9A.72.010(2), (4), .020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true).

The inclusion of “notary” is text DGG's counsel may analyze. But it does not answer whether every routine private notarization is a “proceeding,” what the relevant course or outcome would be, or whether the statement is material to it. The located cases do not provide a direct element-by-element holding for this product. Evidence cases discussing statements or proceedings serve only as context; they should not be represented as resolving the criminal definition.

Accordingly:

- do not call the format a “perjury interview”;
- do not place “felony” in outreach, thumbnails, status pages, or host scripts;
- do not ask the notary to opine on perjury or materiality; and
- do not treat perjury as the program's success condition.

Second-degree perjury generally is not the fit because it requires an insurance-contract examination or an intent to mislead a public servant in performing a duty. [RCW 9A.72.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.030).

### 9.1 Case law is supportive context, not the missing product holding

The supplied memorandum identifies several Washington cases that counsel should retain in a final citated version, with disciplined limits:

- *State v. Lewis*, 85 Wn.2d 769, 771–72 (1975), concerns what conduct can constitute taking an oath; it does not decide this private-media design.
- *State v. Smith*, 97 Wn.2d 856, 861–63 (1982), treated a particular voluntary, notarized witness statement as an “other proceeding” for an evidence rule after a case-specific reliability analysis.
- *State v. Otton*, 185 Wn.2d 673, 679–84 (2016), preserved *Smith*'s evidence-rule approach. The opinion itself says it concerns interpretation of an evidentiary rule; it is not an element-level holding under RCW 9A.72.010(4). [Official *Otton* opinion](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/916691.pdf).
- *In re Recall of Pearsall-Stipek*, 141 Wn.2d 756, 770–79, 10 P.3d 1034 (2000), is cited for the separation between false swearing and first-degree perjury, including false swearing's lack of an express materiality element.
- *State v. Hovrud*, 60 Wn. App. 573, 575–77, 805 P.2d 250 (1991), construed older oath-authorization language. Any reliance must address the later statutory route now codified in RCW 9A.72.010(5), which treats an oath as authorized when administered by a legally authorized officer.

These reporter pinpoints came from the supplied research and were spot-checked where an official opinion was readily available. A current citator and official-reporter verification remain required before counsel or DGG treats any case proposition as publication-ready. None supplies the absent holding that a routine private political-media verification is—or is not—an official proceeding.

## 10. Correction is an integrity tool, not promised immunity

The statutory retraction defense is narrow: the speaker must retract in the same proceeding before exposure becomes manifest and before the falsification substantially affects the proceeding. [RCW 9A.72.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.060). DGG cannot promise that an internal correction window or later supplemental statement satisfies that rule.

Nor should DGG rely on a deliberately defective oath. Washington provides that specified irregularity is not a defense if the oath was otherwise required or authorized. [RCW 9A.72.070](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.070).

The project correction policy is broader for editorial integrity:

- administrative metadata error: append a dated log; never alter the executed CSR;
- substantive error before publication: create a new CSR and repeat the entire oath, signature, certificate, and journal act;
- substantive error after publication: publish a new sworn CSR after a complete new act, or an expressly unsworn participant correction; and
- preserve the executed original under legal hold while media counsel decides whether a known-false public copy remains available, is access-restricted, or becomes a provenance stub.

# IV. Jurisdiction, remote acts, and future law

## 11. Keep Phase 1 physically in Washington

Notarial validity, Washington criminal territorial jurisdiction, charging authority, and interstate rendition are separate issues. A Washington notary's involvement does not automatically establish that Washington can prosecute every remotely located speaker. RCW 9A.04.030 contains multiple fact-specific jurisdiction routes; subsection (6) specifically addresses chapter 5.50 out-of-state declarations and should not be repurposed as a catch-all remote-oath rule. [RCW 9A.04.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.030).

Phase 1 eliminates that avoidable uncertainty: participant, notary, DGG session lead, and every person captured in the controlled session are physically in Washington; the certificate states the actual Washington venue and county; and no remote caller participates in the sworn act.

No DGG document may promise jurisdiction, referral, prosecution, arrest, extradition, or rendition. Each involves independent legal and discretionary gates.

## 12. Do not rely on remote authority in this pilot

Current chapter 42.45 RCW has an electronic-records remote-notarial framework with identity proofing, same-record confirmation, communication technology, audiovisual recording, retention, and foreign-location conditions. [Current and future versions of RCW 42.45.280](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.280). The operator burden and jurisdiction questions are unnecessary for the first test.

Effective January 1, 2027, Washington's 2026 amendments add express procedures for remote administration of an oath or affirmation and specified tangible-record methods. The official bill report describes the earlier omission of remote oral-oath authority as an oversight. [SHB 2158 bill report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Bill%20Reports/House/2158-S%20HBR%20PL%2026.htm). That future branch must be rechecked after the effective date against the enacted section, current codification, DOL rules, approved technology, signer-location law, and counsel advice. It is not part of v1.1.

# V. Publication, privacy, campaign, and constitutional gates

## 13. Recording consent must precede capture

Washington generally requires consent of all participants to record a private communication or conversation. The statute also recognizes a reasonably effective recorded announcement as consent in specified circumstances. [RCW 9.73.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.030). A production cannot begin recording in order to obtain the only consent to that very recording.

For every recorded dry run or later pilot:

1. obtain signed, layered consent before entry into the captured room or call;
2. begin with a clear recorded announcement and affirmative confirmation from every captured person;
3. stop when anyone new enters and repeat the approved consent process;
4. keep ID inspection, addresses, journal entries, identity-proofing data, and other notarial personal information outside the production frame and audio; and
5. treat the notarial journal/remote archive and DGG production master as different records with different controllers and purposes.

Consent should separately address communication capture, notarial requirements, DGG recording, editing, transcript/AI use, publication, clips/syndication, correction, retention, and legal process. A release is an operational risk control; it does not erase statutory exemptions, defamation, copyright, contract, election, tax, or privacy issues.

## 14. Chapter 63.60 requires a use-specific analysis

The supplied protocol overstates Washington personality-rights law by saying it categorically “requires” a written name/voice/image license for this project. RCW 63.60.050 reaches specified commercial, advertising, and fundraising uses without consent, but RCW 63.60.070 exempts many political, newsworthy, public-interest, program, article, public-affairs, and campaign uses when conditions—including no inaccurate endorsement—are met. [RCW 63.60.050, .070](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=63.60&full=true).

DGG should still obtain a plain, specific written media release because the project contemplates recording, editing, clips, syndication, promotion, possible fundraising, and AI-assisted processing. Counsel must determine which uses need consent, which are exempt, and how 2026 “forged digital likeness” provisions affect dubbing, voice cleanup, translation, synthetic imagery, or other alterations. Never create a false endorsement or materially alter a participant's meaning.

## 15. Defamation and adverse inference are central safety issues

The project owns its headlines, thumbnails, edits, status labels, source characterizations, and implications. A public figure's actual-malice burden is not editorial immunity. Known innocent reasons for declining—including counsel advice, privacy, scheduling, disability, disagreement with the format, or ordinary preference—make a dishonesty-oriented refusal label especially risky.

Therefore:

- do not publish invitee lists or identified nonparticipant statuses during Phases 0–2;
- do not use “Declined Sworn Format,” “refused truth,” credibility colors, honesty scores, or leaderboards;
- do not leave a materially misleading superseded clip uncorrected;
- keep qualifications and correction notices attached to every derivative; and
- require prepublication media-counsel review of every named public package.

Even later, any status must report only a verified event and carry an equally prominent no-inference notice. Audience testing—not intuition—must show that the wording does not create a material dishonesty inference.

## 16. Campaign, media, and nonprofit status are launch gates

DGG's website describes a creator/candidate collaboration and content-promotion model. Before public launch, obtain the operating entity's legal name, EIN, governing documents, IRS determination letter, tax filings, payer/funding records, editorial-control policy, campaign contacts, and insurance. A public claim of 501(c)(4) status is not a substitute for these records.

Relevant classifications are fact-dependent:

- Candidate-requested, coordinated, below-market, or candidate-prepared content can implicate Washington's broad “contribution” definition, while the regularly scheduled news-medium exclusion has specific conditions. [RCW 29B.10.160](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29B.10.160).
- Washington independent-expenditure reporting can begin at a low monetary threshold. [RCW 29B.25.120](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29B.25.120).
- Washington's news/media exclusion is conditional, not an automatic label for anyone publishing content. [RCW 29B.10.220](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29B.10.220).
- The FEC has treated an internet television entity as eligible for the press exemption where it was a press entity acting in its press function and was not owned or controlled by a party, committee, or candidate. That is an analogy requiring facts, not a blanket safe harbor. [FEC Advisory Opinion 2008-14 summary](https://www.fec.gov/updates/ao-2008-14-internet-tv-station-qualifies-for-press-exemption/).
- A section 501(c)(4) organization may engage in some political activity, but it cannot make political campaign activity its primary activity, and section 527(f) tax may apply. [IRS social-welfare organization guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/other-non-profits/social-welfare-organizations).

Until election and tax counsel classify the actual entity, relationships, requests, payments, content, and distribution, DGG should not coordinate candidate messaging, provide unpriced campaign value, promise favorable editing, or characterize the project as exempt press activity.

## 17. Voluntariness and political speech

The project is privately offered and voluntary, which reduces—not eliminates—constitutional and coercion concerns. Political falsehoods are not categorically outside constitutional protection, and any later state prosecution would be state action subject to ordinary constitutional limits. Voluntariness is therefore a **risk-reducing design feature**, not a “constitutional safe harbor.”

The invitation must be viewpoint-neutral, ask symmetric questions of similarly situated participants, disclose the complete question/source packet in advance, permit counsel and withdrawal, avoid a public refusal penalty, and keep prosecution rhetoric out of host interaction. DGG must not threaten referral, bargain for a preferred answer, or induce destruction, withholding, or alteration of evidence.

# VI. Evidence and evaluation

## 18. The civic argument does not depend on prosecution

The protocol's durable value is a better evidence object:

- exact proposition rather than a moving clip;
- declared personal-knowledge or record basis;
- visible uncertainty and qualification;
- stable version and custody history;
- source-to-claim links;
- separate fact, inference, and advocacy lanes; and
- a correction chain that shows rather than rewrites what changed.

This architecture supports the user's broader “detective” and memorandum-building goal: a later viewer can compare the proposition, source, counter-source, adoption, qualification, and correction without relying on a commentator's confidence or a selectively edited excerpt.

## 19. Existing behavioral evidence is indirect

Research on honesty commitments, accuracy prompts, and fact-checking makes a pilot rational, but it does not establish that a notarial political format improves speaker accuracy or audience calibration. The supplied evidence plan correctly treats mechanisms and outcomes as hypotheses and identifies audience overtrust, chilling, selection, legalistic evasion, and partisan asymmetry as possible harms.

Phase 2 should separate two causal questions:

1. **Audience experiment:** holding substantive content constant, does the format change comprehension, calibrated confidence, false truth-certification belief, refusal stigma, and correction uptake?
2. **Speaker-process experiment:** does exposure to the record/adoption process change objective accuracy, qualification, unknown use, source citation, evasion, correction, or participation?

The audience experiment cannot answer the speaker-accuracy question. Both require a preregistered ground-truth adjudication protocol, estimands, randomization unit, clustering plan, coding reliability, multiplicity control, retention schedule, and null/adverse-result publication.

### Provisional safety gates

Before data collection, an independent methods reviewer should convert these into numeric, powered criteria:

- at least 90% of tested viewers correctly answer that the notary did not verify substantive truth;
- the format produces no practically meaningful increase in false truth-certification belief relative to a content-matched control;
- a neutral nonparticipation description produces no practically meaningful dishonesty inference relative to no status label;
- no material partisan interaction in comprehension, inference, or invitation administration;
- zero unconsented capture, ID/journal exposure, live publication, wrong-record execution, or overwritten sworn-record events; and
- a prespecified beneficial result on accuracy or calibration without failure of a co-primary safety outcome.

These are design targets, not empirical findings. Sample size, equivalence margins, and decision rules require statistical review.

# VII. Required pilot architecture

## 20. Phase gates

| Phase | Activity | Public output | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — dry run | Synthetic facts; tabletop incidents; paper and custody tests | Blank forms, synthetic examples, methods | Counsel approves exact forms; independent notary approves workflow; zero critical failures in repeated runs |
| 1 — nonpublic feasibility | Small, in-person Washington cohort; wet-ink CSR | Aggregate process findings only; no named CSR/PMR | Consent, custody, correction, role, and certificate controls pass |
| 2 — experiments | Audience and separate speaker-process studies | Preregistration, methods, aggregate/null/adverse results | Numeric comprehension, no-inference, symmetry, and safety gates pass |
| 3 — limited public pilot | In-person Washington; few pre-disclosed factual propositions | Counsel-approved complete package only | Case-specific media/election/privacy/entity review and participant release |
| 4 — remote or interstate | New protocol version only | None until separate gate | 2027 law/rules/provider matrix and signer-location counsel review |

## 21. Launch blockers

No named or live pilot may proceed until counsel answers in writing:

1. Does the exact adoption/oath/certificate create a valid verification under current Washington law?
2. What propositions, qualifications, definitions, and pages are within the sworn payload?
3. How should false swearing be described without coercion or overclaiming?
4. Is any “official proceeding” theory supportable, and must it remain nonpublic?
5. What jurisdiction and venue facts are required for each contemplated participant configuration?
6. What recording, privacy, release, retention, legal-hold, and deletion rules apply?
7. What campaign, coordination, independent-expenditure, press, tax, and insurance classifications apply to the actual entity and relationships?
8. What defamation, false-light, copyright, personality-rights, and AI-use controls govern publication and correction?
9. Who owns the paper original, scan, PMR, source packet, access log, and correction chain?
10. What incident requires an immediate stop, preservation notice, regulator response, or outside counsel?

Counsel must also run a current citator and verify reporter pinpoints before relying on the case discussion. This v1.1 review verified primary statutory text and official agency materials but is not a substitute for a commercial citator, discovery, or privileged fact investigation.

## Conclusion

Washington law supplies enough notarial infrastructure to test a serious voluntary sworn-record format. The cleanest argument is narrow: a participant may deliberately adopt a signed factual record in a verification on oath before an authorized Washington notary; Washington's false-swearing text may apply to a knowingly false statement made under that authorized oath. The leap from that text to automatic liability—especially first-degree perjury, extraterritorial jurisdiction, or extradition—is unsupported.

The latest design therefore treats criminal law as a bounded background condition and builds value in the record itself. If the format produces clearer propositions, honest uncertainty, better sources, durable corrections, and better-calibrated audiences without coercion or a notarial truth halo, it can succeed even if nobody is ever investigated or prosecuted. If its value depends on viewers believing the notary proved truth or that declining signals guilt, the project should stop.
