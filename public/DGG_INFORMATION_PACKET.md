## The idea in one minute

Political communication often mixes checkable facts with predictions, values, interpretation, humor, and advocacy. This proposal tests whether a speaker can make the factual part easier to evaluate by placing a few narrowly worded statements into a stable, source-linked record.

If a later phase is approved, a willing speaker would choose among three real options:

1. **Sworn factual record:** sign a narrow factual statement and swear or affirm it before an independent notary.
2. **Ordinary conversation:** discuss the subject without placing the answer under oath.
3. **Qualify, say “I do not know,” or decline:** preserve an honest exit at the format and question level.

The durable public object would be the exact statement, its scope and date, the speaker's stated basis of knowledge, the cited source bundle, the version identifier, and any later correction. The signer would declare the statement true under oath. The notary would check required identity and signature, administer the oath or affirmation, and complete the notarial certificate. **The notary would not investigate or certify the political claim.**

That boundary is essential. The research question is whether the record design improves clarity, sourcing, and correction without making either DGG or the notary a truth judge.

## What this is—and is not

| This proposal is | This proposal is not |
|---|---|
| An optional format for a speaker's own narrow factual commitments | A government or private “ministry of truth” |
| A provenance record: who signed what, when, in which exact version, with which sources | A notary-certified fact-check |
| A test of whether clearer boundaries improve precision, corrections, and audience understanding | A promise that an oath makes politicians honest |
| A way to separate fact from unsworn commentary | A demand that opinions, predictions, or advocacy be sworn |
| A system with “unknown,” qualification, correction, and exit paths | A perjury trap or automatic criminal referral |
| Candidate-neutral research using fictional or tightly controlled test material | A refusal list, candidate badge, participation score, or ballot label |

DGG will draw and publish no adverse inference from nonparticipation. Because DGG cannot control third-party political reactions, Phase 0 publishes no invitation, participation, refusal, or nonresponse status.

## How a later approved record could work

### 1. Admit only a suitable factual proposition

A proposed sworn statement should identify:

- the person or thing being described;
- the relevant time period;
- one checkable predicate;
- the speaker's basis of knowledge;
- any necessary definition or qualification;
- the permitted answers, including “unknown” or a written qualification; and
- the sources the speaker wants attached.

Predictions, value judgments, slogans, broad policy interpretations, compound accusations, and questions that require guessing stay in the unsworn lane.

### 2. Freeze one exact record before the act

The speaker receives the exact wording and source bundle in advance. The record receives a version identifier and a SHA-256 cryptographic file fingerprint, which can detect changed bytes but does not prove substantive truth. The speaker may correct, qualify, decline, or move the subject to ordinary conversation before signing.

### 3. Keep the roles separate

- The **speaker** owns the substantive answer and stated basis of knowledge.
- DGG or another **publisher** designs the editorial format and preserves the record.
- **Counsel** reviews the statement, notice, release, and applicable law.
- The **notary** independently controls the notarial act and may refuse or stop as law permits.
- A later **fact-checker or audience** may assess the claim, but that assessment is not the notarial act.

### 4. Preserve, do not rewrite, corrections

A substantive change after execution would require a new record and new act. The earlier version would remain linked and clearly superseded rather than silently edited. Whether a correction has any legal effect would depend on the applicable law and facts; the project promises no immunity.

## Annotated synthetic demonstration

The following is fictional. It is a paper-design example only. No person made this statement, no notary administered an oath, and no legal conclusion should be drawn from it.

> **Synthetic claim unit SR-EX-001**  
> **Prompt:** “As of 5:00 p.m. Pacific time on August 1, 2026, did the fictional Pine County Project ledger attached as Source A record exactly three payments from Example Vendor totaling $12,450?”  
> **Selected response:** Yes.  
> **Final statement placed in the record:** “As of 5:00 p.m. Pacific time on August 1, 2026, the fictional Pine County Project ledger attached as Source A recorded exactly three payments from Example Vendor totaling $12,450.”

| Field | Synthetic content | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker | Jordan Lee, a fictional project treasurer | Identifies the declarant without using a real participant |
| Knowledge basis | Custodian of the fictional ledger; reviewed entries 101–103 | Makes personal knowledge explicit |
| Permitted response path | “Yes,” “No,” “I do not know,” or written qualification; the final proposition must match the selected path | Prevents pressured guessing and makes the signed proposition exact |
| Scope | One ledger, three entries, one cutoff time | Avoids a vague claim such as “our finances are accurate” |
| Source bundle | Fictional Source A plus a source manifest | Lets readers inspect the cited basis |
| Record version | SR-EX-001 v1; SHA-256 file fingerprint shown on the record | Detects post-signing byte changes; does not prove truth |
| Jurat | Placeholder only; exact act and certificate require counsel/requester selection and lawful notary completion | Avoids asking the notary to choose DGG's legal form |
| Public disclaimer | “The notary did not verify the ledger or the amount.” | Blocks the most dangerous misconception |
| Correction | A later v2 would link to v1 and explain the change | Preserves history and context |

This design does not prove the claim. It makes the speaker's exact commitment and supporting material easier to identify and audit.

## Why the proposal may be worth testing

The strongest case for the format does not depend on anyone being prosecuted. A carefully designed record could provide:

- fewer moving clips and paraphrase disputes;
- narrower factual wording and explicit definitions;
- a visible “unknown” option instead of a pressured guess;
- a source bundle tied to the exact statement;
- a correction history that preserves what changed;
- a clean boundary between factual commitment and ordinary advocacy; and
- measurable evidence about audience comprehension and speaker behavior.

Laboratory and online studies have found that some honesty-oath formulations changed reporting behavior, while other formulations had weaker or nonsignificant effects. None of the located studies tested politicians, adversarial interviews, public reputation, notarization, or voter understanding. The defensible conclusion is **“worth testing under safeguards,” not “proven to work.”** See [`EMP-TRUST-01`](../research/GAP_MATRIX.md) and the empirical source summaries in [`report-source.md`](../research/report-source.md).

## The legal idea—and its limits

Washington law recognizes administering an oath and taking a verification on oath or affirmation as notarial acts. A verification is the signer's declaration, under oath, that a statement in a record is true. Washington also has false-swearing and perjury statutes with different elements. These statutes identify legal questions worth testing. They do not establish that this private format triggers false-swearing or perjury liability in any particular case.

It does not supply a shortcut:

- Notarization is not substantive truth verification.
- A false answer is not automatically perjury.
- Potential false swearing depends on a knowingly false statement under an oath required or authorized by law; exact application to private political speech remains constitutionally and factually unresolved.
- First-degree perjury has additional materiality and official-proceeding requirements.
- Jurisdiction, admissible proof, charging discretion, limitations, and interstate process are independent questions.
- A private organization cannot create a prosecution by contract or branding.

Political speech also receives unusually strong constitutional protection. Washington's leading campaign-speech decisions and the U.S. Supreme Court's false-speech cases strongly caution against a government truth tribunal, broad complaint process, censure system, or public stigma for declining. A government-adjacent version would create additional forum, disclosure, public-records, and equal-treatment questions. The present Phase 0 is a private, nonpublic, candidate-neutral fictional mock with no signer, oath, or notarial act. DGG's legal entity, funding, control relationships, and government contacts remain unverified launch gates.

The detailed analysis and adverse authority are in the [`research memorandum`](../memorandum/DGG_WASHINGTON_SWORN_STATEMENT_MEMORANDUM.md), [`authority register`](../research/AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md), and [`gap matrix`](../research/GAP_MATRIX.md).

## What Phase 0 actually permits

Phase 0 is a safety and comprehension test, not a political launch. While the remand remains open, it may use only:

- a pure mock using fictional material, with no real signer, oath, or notarial act.

A private Washington in-person test limited to literally true statements about the test itself is a possible future branch, not current permission. It would require a reviewed supersession closing the remand as well as every written professional gate.

Phase 0 does not permit:

- a named candidate, officeholder, or public-figure target;
- promotional distribution, candidate outreach, or publication presented as an approved program; the repository copy is visible only as a review draft;
- a livestream or free-form “everything is now under oath” interview;
- a remote DGG act;
- a candidate badge, scorecard, invitation list, or refusal/nonresponse label;
- a statement that a participant committed a crime; or
- use of a notarial journal, identity credential, or statutory notarial recording as a DGG publicity asset.

## How the idea should be evaluated

The first release gate is comprehension, not enthusiasm. Intended readers should be able to explain, in their own words, that:

1. participation and question-level answers are optional;
2. DGG draws and publishes no adverse inference from declining and publishes no participation status in Phase 0;
3. the notary does not certify factual truth;
4. a false response does not automatically prove perjury or guarantee prosecution; and
5. the current project is research, not a live candidate program.

A proposed project rule is at least 90% correct comprehension on each of those high-risk propositions in a preregistered test, with no meaningful increase in “notarized means verified true” or “declining means dishonest” relative to neutral control copy. That is a safety recommendation, not a legal standard or a claim that 90% is universally optimal.

Other outcomes should include:

- unaided main-message recall;
- coercion or unfair-surprise perception;
- qualification, unknown, decline, and correction rates;
- partisan-symmetry and selection measures;
- false-confidence rate;
- accessibility and reading burden;
- privacy or security incidents; and
- evidence that the format improves precision without chilling protected speech.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the notary saying the claim is true?

No. The signer makes the declaration. The notary checks required identity and signature, administers the oath or affirmation, and completes the notarial certificate. The notary is not DGG's fact-checker or spokesperson.

### Is every false answer perjury?

No. Washington crimes have different elements, and their application depends on the exact statement, knowledge, materiality where required, kind of proceeding, authorization of the oath, jurisdiction, evidence, and official discretion. The project must never advertise an automatic felony.

### Why use an oath at all?

The testable theory is that a formal, exact, source-linked commitment may improve care, provenance, correction, or audience calibration. If the project has value only when viewers expect prosecution, it should stop.

### What if a person declines?

DGG will draw and publish no adverse inference about honesty. People may decline for scheduling, legal, strategic, privacy, religious, or format reasons. Because DGG cannot control third-party political reactions, the current project publishes no invitation, participation, refusal, or nonresponse status.

### Would government run the program?

Not in the current recommended design. A government or public-private version would require a new constitutional, forum, disclosure, public-records, retention, and election-law analysis. It could not simply inherit the private Phase 0 conclusions.

### Is this already proven to improve political truth?

No. The empirical evidence comes from different tasks and populations. Political-speaker and audience effects—including harmful effects—must be tested directly.

### What is the safest name for the idea?

Use **“voluntary source-linked sworn-record format”** or **“voluntary factual-commitment record.”** Avoid “truth-certified,” “notary-verified truth,” “lie detector,” “perjury trap,” and “official truth pledge.”
