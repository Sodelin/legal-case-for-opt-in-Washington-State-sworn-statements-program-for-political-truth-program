## Litigation and dispute postures to anticipate

These are planning scenarios, not predictions that litigation is ripe or advisable.

### Scenario A: challenge to a government mandate, label, or condition

Potential issues include compelled speech, political-speech burden, unconstitutional conditions, viewpoint discrimination, disclosure/association, election regulation, due process, and Washington constitutional protections. Material facts would include who controls the program, what consequence follows nonparticipation, whether an entry is government or private speech, who may complain, what interim label appears, and what appeal exists.

Potential relief could include declaratory or injunctive relief against a mandate, label, disclosure, or complaint process. Standing, ripeness, defendants, immunity, exhaustion, and remedy require fact-specific counsel analysis. *SBA List* illustrates pre-enforcement injury but does not decide merits.

### Scenario B: dispute over a private publication or status

Potential issues include defamation, false light, privacy, recording consent, contract/release, unfair or deceptive presentation, and Washington's Uniform Public Expression Protection Act. The decisive record would include the exact invitation, response, edit, caption, adjacent disclaimer, source basis, knowledge of error, correction timing, distribution, and damages.

The safest current control is to publish no named invitation, refusal, or nonresponse status and no operator “false” verdict.

### Scenario C: investigation or charge based on a sworn record

Potential issues include validity and authorization of the oath, statement wording, knowing falsity, materiality and official proceeding if perjury is alleged, territorial jurisdiction, corroboration, constitutional application, limitations, custody/authentication, prosecutorial discretion, and interstate process.

DGG cannot create or promise a charge. It should preserve potentially relevant evidence, stop public speculation, and obtain counsel before any referral, interview, correction, or deletion. A private accusation or affidavit does not itself become a prosecution; see *Stout v. Felix* and RCW 36.27.020.

### Scenario D: public-records or retention dispute

If a government agency receives, uses, or co-administers records, likely issues include whether the material relates to government conduct, who owns/uses/retains it, applicable exemptions, redaction, retention schedules, litigation holds, and production format. The project should assume that sending a record to government can change its disclosure posture.

### Scenario E: campaign-finance or lobbying complaint

The record must identify the speaker/payer, sponsor, purpose, candidate or ballot content, medium, audience, distribution date, spend, vendor, candidate contacts, material involvement, legislative call to action, and reporting timeline. A label such as “education,” “press,” or “research” is not dispositive.

## Evidence handling rules

For every later record:

1. assign a stable record and version ID before collection;
2. record source, custodian, collection method, time zone, and acquisition time;
3. preserve the received original read-only and compute a SHA-256 digest over a documented byte scope, recording time zone, tool/version, expected value, and every verification result;
4. separate public, confidential, privileged, notarial, identity, security, and legal-hold stores;
5. document every transformation, redaction, excerpt, and derivative;
6. preserve corrections as new linked records rather than rewriting an original;
7. suspend ordinary deletion when counsel issues a litigation hold;
8. never place privileged advice or protected identity material in this public repository; and
9. do not imply that hashing proves substantive truth—it shows only whether the hashed bytes changed.
