## Mission

Produce a decision-ready legal memorandum and pilot packet evaluating a private, voluntary, opt-in format that separates narrowly defined sworn factual statements from ordinary unsworn political commentary. Washington is the operating-law anchor. Pennsylvania is a comparator, not a presumed substitute. Federal law supplies constitutional and interstate constraints, not a default federal perjury theory.

## Nonnegotiable accuracy rules

1. Do not state that notarization proves or certifies factual truth.
2. Do not state that every false answer is perjury.
3. Do not state that a refusal implies dishonesty.
4. Do not state that Washington automatically has criminal jurisdiction over every out-of-state participant.
5. Do not state that extradition is automatic, ordinary, or likely.
6. Separate law effective on 2026-08-30 from law effective on 2027-01-01.
7. Treat criminal exposure as fact-, element-, jurisdiction-, and discretion-dependent.
8. Preserve a usable opt-out, question-specific decline, qualification, and correction path.
9. Use **Digital Ground Game (DGG)** consistently; archival drafts using “DDG” remain unchanged.
10. Require counsel review before any live pilot or public claim about criminal liability.

## Current project posture and evaluated routes

### Present Phase 0 design — pure fictional mock only

While PR #4's remand remains open, Phase 0 permits only a pure mock with fictional facts and no oath or notarial act. No real signer or notarial act is presently authorized. A future reviewed supersession may propose an actual test verification containing only literally true, personal-knowledge statements about the test itself. That future minimum design would be nonpublic, in-person, Washington-only, and paper-based, with one tangible Canonical Sworn Record as the sole oath object; the ordinary interview, production record, source packet, annotations, and later commentary would remain unsworn. This proposed boundary is a project risk-control choice, not a statement that remote notarization is generally unavailable.

### Evaluated 2026 remote route — quarantined from operations

Current law provides a safeguarded, record-based remote **verification on oath or affirmation** route for a properly endorsed Washington notary. Identity may be established by personal knowledge, a qualifying credible witness, or the prescribed identity-proofing route. No DGG remote act is authorized at the current stage; the route remains research only until the remand is cured and all written gates are satisfied.

### Evaluated 2027 expansion — future law

2026 Washington Laws chapter 21 (HB 2158), effective 2027-01-01, expands the remote framework to include express procedures for standalone remote administration of an oath or affirmation and for tangible records. Implementation rulemaking began in 2026. This route must be rechecked against adopted rules, Department of Licensing guidance, and the certificate requirement before any use.
