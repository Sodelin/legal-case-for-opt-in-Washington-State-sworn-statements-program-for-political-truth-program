# Legal authority verification — factual working memorandum

Research date: September 5, 2026. Inputs reviewed: the supplied 25-page Washington pilot PDF; Voluntary-Factual-Statements-Feasibility; the Washington memorandum's relevant legal sections; and the states-and-commonwealths interstate section. Current statutes and rules were checked on official legislative sites. Rickert was retrieved through CourtListener; Alvarez was read in a reproduction of the Court's opinion at Cornell LII. This memorandum records legal facts and unresolved applications. It expresses no position on adoption, legislation, funding, or political usefulness.

## Ten verified findings

1. **An oath, acknowledgment, verification, and truth determination are different acts.** Washington notaries have general statutory authority to administer oaths and affirmations. For a verification of a statement on oath, RCW 42.45.030(2) directs the officer to determine the individual's identity and attribution of the signature. The provision does not assign investigation of the factual assertion to the notary. Acknowledgment has its own subsection. **Exact factual boundary:** a certificate documents the applicable notarial act; the statute does not make that certificate a governmental factual-accuracy finding. Sources: [RCW 5.28.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=5.28.010); [RCW 42.45.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.45.030).

2. **Chapter 5.50 declarations are a separate statutory route.** RCW 5.50.030 gives a qualifying unsworn declaration equivalent effect where Washington law permits or requires a sworn declaration, with enumerated exceptions. RCW 9A.72.010(3)(c) includes chapter 5.50 declarations in its treatment of written statements as under oath. **Exact factual boundary:** printing a penalty-of-perjury phrase on an unspecified private pledge does not, on this record alone, establish satisfaction of chapter 5.50. This review does not resolve whether an identified final private declaration falls within that chapter. Sources: [RCW 5.50.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=5.50.030); [RCW 9A.72.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.010).

3. **False-swearing elements include a consequential rule about unsupported assertions.** RCW 9A.72.040 defines false swearing through a false statement, knowledge of falsity, and an oath required or authorized by law; the offense is a gross misdemeanor. RCW 9A.72.010(5) includes an oath administered by a legally authorized person in the authorized-oath definition. RCW 9A.72.080 treats an unqualified assertion of something the speaker does not know to be true as equivalent to a knowingly false statement. **Exact factual boundary:** a summary referring only to deliberate fabrication omits statutory text relevant to unqualified assertions beyond knowledge. The reviewed statutes do not establish how every qualification, ambiguous proposition, or knowledge claim in this proposed interview would be adjudicated. Sources: [RCW 9A.72.040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9a.72.040); [RCW 9A.72.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.010); [RCW 9A.72.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.080).

4. **Perjury cannot be settled by the label “private.”** First-degree perjury adds materiality and an official proceeding. The current definition includes proceedings before certain notaries or others taking testimony or depositions. Second-degree perjury has different contextual elements, including insurance examinations or intent to mislead a public servant performing a duty. **Exact factual boundary:** neither “every notarized interview is felony perjury” nor “private interviews cannot be official proceedings” follows from the cited text. No controlling decision applying these elements to this precise campaign format was established in this check. Sources: [RCW 9A.72.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.010); [RCW 9A.72.020–.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true).

5. **Current campaign citations are in Title 29B.** The candidate-advertising provision is RCW 29B.30.070, formerly 42.17A.335 and earlier 42.17.530. It addresses enumerated statements constituting libel or defamation per se, sponsored with actual malice, and requires clear-and-convincing proof. It contains specified self-statement exceptions. It is not a general prohibition of all incorrect campaign assertions. WAC 390-32-020 currently states that failure to subscribe to the Fair Campaign Practices Code is not a Title 29B violation. **Exact citation correction:** current-law references to 42.17A.335 are historical citations; 29B.30.070 is the current location. Sources: [RCW 29B.30.070](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=29B.30.070); [WAC 390-32-020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=390-32-020). The [PDC's current public case notices](https://www.pdc.wa.gov/rules-enforcement/enforcement/enforcement-cases/186503) identify January 1, 2026 as the recodification date.

6. **Rickert and Alvarez establish limits, not adjudication of this proposal.** Rickert v. State, PDC, 168 P.3d 826 (Wash. 2007), invalidated the then-operative candidate-advertising provision. Its lead opinion had four justices; Chief Justice Alexander separately concurred because the statute extended to nondefamatory speech. His paragraph 28 expressly distinguished permissible penalties for defamation from the broader language of the lead opinion. United States v. Alvarez, 567 U.S. 709 (2012), invalidated the Stolen Valor Act provision; Justice Kennedy's four-justice plurality and Justice Breyer's two-justice concurrence used different analyses. The plurality's treatment of traditional perjury concerned injury to judicial and governmental processes. **Exact factual boundary:** these decisions neither announce that all false speech is unprotected nor decide the constitutionality of criminal punishment for this voluntary private campaign verification. The existence of participant consent is not a holding resolving that question. Sources: [Rickert, opinion and concurrence ¶28](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2582429/rickert-v-state-public-disclosure-comn/); [Alvarez, opinion and concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/11-210).

7. **The remote-notarial statute has distinct current and future versions.** As of this research date, RCW 42.45.280(3) places the electronic-records notary in Washington and requires qualifying identity verification, same-record confirmation, and audiovisual recording. A replacement is enacted for January 1, 2027 under 2026 chapter 21. It adds tangible-record procedures, an express remote oath/affirmation provision in subsection (7), and a technology/storage-provider service-of-process provision. **Exact factual boundary:** future subsection (7) is not September 2026 authority. Its enactment does not itself answer every interpretive question about a standalone oral oath under the earlier version. Source: [RCW 42.45.280, both dated versions](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.45.280).

8. **Remote retention involves more than keeping a public video.** WAC 308-30-330 requires protected storage, a secure backup, at least ten years of audiovisual retention, and continuity arrangements for repository-contract termination. Subsection (1) says this recording must exclude images of the electronic record that was the subject of the act. Employer or repository storage does not relieve the notary's obligations. The journal has a different retention clock: ten years after the last notarial act chronicled in that journal, with a statutory professional-practice exception for qualifying attorney notaries. Washington's private-communication recording rule generally requires all-party consent and specifies a recorded announcement mechanism; whether a particular public or preliminary segment is private is fact-dependent. **Exact factual boundary:** a public interview, the retained notarial recording, and the notary's journal are not interchangeable records. Sources: [WAC 308-30-330](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=308-30-330); [RCW 42.45.180](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.180); [RCW 9.73.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9.73.030).

9. **Pennsylvania recognition does not transfer offense definitions.** 57 Pa.C.S. §311 gives qualifying out-of-state notarial acts the same effect under Pennsylvania law as its own notarial acts; the provision identifies the act, signature, title, and authority. Pennsylvania's 18 Pa.C.S. §4903(b), however, concerns a statement legally required to be sworn, whereas Washington's false-swearing statute includes oaths required or authorized by law. Pennsylvania subsection (a) separately covers official proceedings and intent to mislead a public servant. Washington's jurisdiction statute has its own territorial routes, including in-state conduct, specified effects, and a separate chapter 5.50/perjury route. **Exact factual boundary:** recognition of a Washington certificate does not establish Washington territorial jurisdiction, Pennsylvania criminal liability, evidentiary admissibility, or an interstate-surrender result. Sources: [57 Pa.C.S. §311](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/57/00.003.011.000..HTM); [18 Pa.C.S. §4903](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/18/00.049.003.000..HTM); [RCW 9A.04.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.030).

10. **Editorial correction and statutory retraction are different.** RCW 9A.72.060 bars conviction upon retraction satisfying its same-proceeding condition and two timing conditions: before exposure becomes manifest and before substantial effect on the proceeding. **Exact factual boundary:** an organization's correction, withdrawal, or appeal policy does not by itself satisfy the statute or confer immunity. Its application to a particular private verification is not established here. Source: [RCW 9A.72.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72.060).

## 11. Process description and limitations

This was targeted verification of consequential propositions, not a full citator search or a new systematic review. Several initially requested legislative URLs returned retrieval errors; official alternative URLs and the corresponding current section text were then retrieved where used above. CourtListener briefly rate-limited access; the complete combined Rickert opinion was subsequently retrieved, and the concurrence's precise language was inspected. The current and scheduled Washington versions were considered separately. No quantitative process score is assigned.
