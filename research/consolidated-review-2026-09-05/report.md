## 1. Abstract

This targeted review compares eleven documents concerning a voluntary, recorded factual-statement format and checks selected legal, organizational and empirical sources. The record describes advance questions, exact answers and qualifications, a distinct optional notarial act, ordinary commentary, evidence review and corrections. Differences remain concerning nonparticipant publication, evidence categories, sequence of testing and the strength of legal language. Washington's notarial framework and existing voluntary campaign programs establish relevant mechanisms; they do not demonstrate the complete proposal's legality, effectiveness or political acceptance. The consolidated specification makes the existing choices and unresolved branches explicit so that different reviewers can examine the same proposal and source record.

## 2. Introduction

The user's stated objective is a detailed architecture from which short explanatory packets can be derived. The comparison therefore asks a narrower, documentable question: **what is already specified, what conflicts, and what additional decisions would a reader need to understand the full operation?** It does not infer what the GOP, DNC, any other party, or a prospective host wants from an absence of consultation data.

The proposal has several different objects: a claim, a participant's adoption of that claim, a notarial act, a published interview, an editorial review, and a possible legal dispute. Each has different evidence and responsible actors. One event does not answer every question about the others. [D4-D6, D9-D11; L1-L8]

# 4.3 Legal facts and limits

| Question | Source-grounded answer | What remains separate |
|---|---|---|
| Can a Washington notary administer relevant acts? | Chapter 42.45 defines and authorizes notarial acts, including verification on oath or affirmation. [L1] | The exact act and lawful execution of this program's documents. |
| What does verification on oath establish? | The officer determines required identity and attribution of the signature; the signer makes the truth declaration. [L1, section .030(2)] | Independent validation of the claim, sources, research inference or political conclusion. |
| What is false swearing? | RCW 9A.72.040 addresses a knowingly false statement under an oath required or authorized by law and classifies the offense as a gross misdemeanor. [L3] | Whether particular conduct satisfies every element and is constitutionally prosecutable in the relevant forum. |
| Is every false answer perjury? | No such conclusion follows. First-degree perjury adds materiality and an official proceeding; the definition includes language concerning notaries that requires analysis of the event. [L4] | Labeling the encounter private does not itself decide the statutory issue. |
| Does “I believed it” or correction end the inquiry? | RCW 9A.72.080 addresses unqualified assertions beyond actual knowledge. Section .060 states a bounded retraction defense for perjury or false swearing, subject to its timing and same-proceeding conditions. [L3-L4] | A project correction policy cannot supply criminal immunity or promise that a statutory defense applies. |
| Can a generic declaration replace the notary? | Chapter 5.50 gives qualifying unsworn declarations legal effect in specified circumstances. [L11] | Adding penalty-of-perjury words to any private statement does not itself establish the statutory route. |
| Is political inaccuracy generally prohibited? | Current RCW 29B.30.070 concerns specified defamatory political advertising and electioneering communications, with statutory conditions. [L12] | It is not authority for a general government determination of every political assertion. |

**Constitutional application.** Rickert concerned Washington's regulation of campaign statements; Alvarez invalidated the challenged federal false-claim prohibition through differing rationales. Neither decision adjudicated this proposed voluntary interview format. Their treatment of false speech does not establish that all false speech is unprotected, or that the program has blanket immunity from constitutional questions. [L9-L10]

**Current citation.** The political-advertising provision is RCW 29B.30.070; the former reference was RCW 42.17A.335. Current and former section numbers can be retained together for source history, with the operative citation identified. [L12]

**Unresolved facts for counsel.** Exact questions and adopted answers; signer and notary locations; intended use; notary commission and endorsements; recording configuration; host entity and campaign relationships; public labels; and the contemplated government role. The supplied documents do not contain a signed legal opinion covering all those facts. [D4, D6, D9, D11; R1]

**Pennsylvania offense wording.** Washington's false-swearing provision includes oaths required or authorized by law. Pennsylvania's “other false swearing” provision refers to a statement required by law to be sworn or affirmed; its official-matters provision has separate conditions. A voluntary appearance cannot be assumed to satisfy the same offense elements in both states. [L3, L8]

**Recognition is one legal question.** Pennsylvania recognizes qualifying acts performed by authorized officers in another state. That does not establish truth, every evidentiary admissibility requirement, Washington territorial jurisdiction, or an interstate surrender procedure. Pennsylvania's use of “Commonwealth” does not create a separate statehood barrier to the recognition statute. [L5-L8; D10 section 4.8]

## September 2026 versus January 2027

The official RCW 42.45.280 page displays both current and future text. The version effective January 1, 2027 expressly adds a standalone remote oath or affirmation procedure and additional tangible-record procedures. The repository's 2026 design uses a defined signed electronic record and verification on oath or affirmation. Later law is not present authority for an earlier act. The precise 2027 workflow still depends on its operative text and implementing rules. [L2; R1]

**Recording has a concrete technical constraint.** WAC 308-30-330 requires the retained remote-notarial audiovisual recording to exclude images of the electronic record that is the subject of the act. It also specifies secure access, backup, at least ten years of retention and continuity arrangements. This is a separate object from a host's consented production recording or a public display of a signed statement. [L13]

**Unresolved implementation detail.** The evidence does not establish that a proposed screen-sharing or recording configuration meets that constraint. The record needs the actual platform behavior and the notary's retention arrangements; a conceptual diagram cannot verify either. [D4, D9; L13]

## What the precedents establish

Washington's Fair Campaign Practices Code is in WAC 390-32. The companion rule expressly states that failure to subscribe is not a violation of Title 29B. The chapter also describes a complaint-publication process involving a complaint and opportunity for response, subject to the rule's conditions. That process is a distinct published procedure, not an evaluation of this proposal. [P1]

The League of Women Voters of Clark County describes its Fair Campaign Practices Project and invitations to registered candidates. Its pledge materials demonstrate that a voluntary campaign-conduct program is administered. A signer list is an administrative record; it does not establish a causal effect on factual accuracy, voter comprehension, or acceptance of a notarized variant. [P2]

## Organizational status matters

“Open to people from different parties” describes access. It does not establish that the operator is institutionally nonpartisan, has a particular tax status, or qualifies for a campaign-finance exception. D9 p.9 expressly leaves the actual contracting entity, control, funding and campaign relationships to be identified.

IRS guidance for a 501(c)(3) organization's candidate appearances describes equal opportunity for candidates seeking the same office, absence of support or opposition, and no political fundraising. Its application depends on the actual event and organization. Merely calling a program educational is not the missing factual analysis. [P3]

Federal candidate-debate rules also have defined requirements. FEC guidance describes eligible staging organizations, at least two candidates, event structure and pre-established objective selection criteria. A series of one-person interviews is not automatically a debate within that framework. Federal guidance is not a substitute for analysis of a Washington state/local event. [P4]

No supplied source establishes that Digital Ground Game, the RNC, DNC, a state party, or a public agency has committed to this program. National party committees, state parties, local organizations and individual campaigns are distinct possible participants; one actor's approval would not document the others' approval.

## A traceable public statement

A short packet's claim can point to an identifier such as `LEGAL-WA-003`. Its supporting record can contain: exact text, source, pinpoint, jurisdiction, effective period, interpretation limits, unresolved question, responsible reviewer and revision date. This is the relationship already contemplated by the project's source ledger, gap matrix and reference-management notes. [D7; R1]

For example, “Washington authorizes relevant notarial acts” can cite chapter 42.45. The broader sentence “this program is lawful everywhere” would require additional authority and facts that this record does not supply. A single link to the notarial chapter cannot substantiate both statements. [L1-L8]

## Execution and evidence are different fields

A record may have an execution state, a review state and a publication state at the same time. Completion of the notarial act does not resolve the evidence review. A later dispute does not rewrite what was signed. Restricted public access does not necessarily mean the underlying evidence was destroyed. These distinctions follow the source documents' separate lanes and custody rules. [D4, D9; R1]

# 4.9 Evidence and evaluation questions

**No completed evaluation of this exact program was supplied or located in this targeted review.** The evidence record includes experiments on honesty commitments, general evaluation methods, and the existence of voluntary campaign programs. These are different types of evidence. [D8-D9; P1-P2; E1-E3]

The behavioral literature examines related interventions, not the complete proposed Washington format. A 2024 meta-analysis reported an honesty-norm subgroup effect of Hedges’s g = 0.27 (95% CI 0.19–0.36) and evidence of publication bias. Its 91,683 participants and 445 effects cover all studied social-commitment types; those totals do not describe the oath subgroup alone. [Zickfeld et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38619478/).

A subsequent randomized megastudy assigned 21,506 US/UK participants to 21 oath variants or no oath in an incentivized online tax-reporting game. Pooled mean compliance was 86.2% with an oath and 82.3% without one; the authors reported d = 0.09. Compliance meant reported income divided by earned income. The experiment tested neither notarization nor criminal penalties, and its results are not an estimate of campaign effects. [Zickfeld et al., 2025](https://www.runi.ac.il/yedion/facultypublications/getfile.ashx?fileType=3&publicationId=8196&username=s.ayal).

This review inspected the megastudy’s full text but verified the meta-analysis through its abstract and institutional record. The 2012 signing-position paper was retracted in 2021. A correction to the later Kristal replication appeared in 2026; its bibliographic record was verified, but the complete correction was inaccessible. Its substantive implications therefore remain unresolved here. [Retraction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8463784/), [correction record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42372171/).

An oath experiment can examine behavior in its task and sample. It does not directly establish candidate recruitment, effects of notarization, criminal deterrence, public interpretation of a seal, or acceptance by a political party. A study of willingness to participate does not by itself estimate a change in factual accuracy. [E1-E2; D8-D9]

# 5. Conclusion and analytical synthesis

## 5. Conclusion

The source record establishes that the proposal has a history, a legal foundation for relevant notarial acts, existing voluntary-program analogues, detailed operating ideas and a plan for evaluation. It does not establish complete legal clearance, actual organizational acceptance, a completed bill or measured effects of the full format. A consolidated specification can make those distinctions and the remaining decisions visible. [D1-D11; R1; L1-L13; P1-P4]

## 6. Deconstructive analysis: separate the claims

The proposal involves at least six propositions: an act is authorized; the chosen procedure performs it correctly; a receiving jurisdiction recognizes it; particular conduct satisfies an offense; the public understands the presentation; and an organization accepts the program. Each requires different evidence. Their coexistence in one packet does not permit evidence for one proposition to answer the others. [D10-D11]

The label “nonpartisan” also contains distinct factual questions: access, selection, ownership, funding, editorial control and institutional status. The documents describe intended access and symmetric procedures. They do not supply a completed organizational or campaign-finance determination. [D9 p.9; P3-P4]

## 7. Reconstructive analysis: start with one record

The smallest object in the existing design is a defined proposition with its source, scope and qualification. It can be associated with a participant's exact answer, a version, execution information, an evidence review and a correction. Those associations preserve different facts; they do not create a truth guarantee. [D4-D5; D9 section 7]

A hypothetical claim illustrates the distinction: “Report R states estimate X for population P during period T.” Reviewing R can address what the report says. It does not, without additional evidence, establish that X is accurate, that a policy caused X, or that the result will repeat elsewhere. The same distinctions apply whether the claim is sworn or ordinarily signed. [D11 section 1]

## 8. Middle-out synthesis: connect the record to administration

Between the individual statement and a public-facing packet are the rules for who asks, reviews, publishes, corrects and hears disputes. The source files contain many of these controls but leave institutional appointments, deadlines and version choices open. The exception maps in section 4 make that intermediate layer explicit.

For portability, D9 separates a common documentation design from local implementation supplements. D10-D11 demonstrate why a new state supplement must examine substantive law, not just substitute a state name. These documents do not establish an interstate agreement or a nationwide enforcement system.

The completed work here is a **comparison and reference specification**. It is suitable for readers to examine the same evidence and open questions; it is not a statement that those readers agree with the proposal.

# 12. Inference review

**Robustness finding:** Legal mechanisms and existing programs are documented. The specific program's effects and organizational acceptance remain unmeasured in the supplied record. This review performs no new meta-analysis and assigns no policy or political score.

**Quantitative limits.** No new pooled estimate, Q, tau-squared, I-squared, funnel analysis or Egger test is calculated. Multiple claims per speaker are clustered observations. Experimental tasks, populations, oath wording, incentives, outcome definitions and publication methods differ; those differences limit direct transfer.

**Rival explanations.** Preparation time, question selection, participant self-selection, editorial review, demand effects and reduced information output could explain a lower observed error count. A changed audience impression could reflect the seal's appearance rather than better truth discrimination. [D8-D9; E1-E2]

**Counterfactuals.** If factual output changes equally with an ordinary signed record, that would not isolate an oath contribution. If errors fall while substantive answers fall, the result needs both denominators. If audiences trust both correct and incorrect claims more, trust alone does not demonstrate improved calibration. Actual adoption requires documented decisions, regardless of any experimental result.
