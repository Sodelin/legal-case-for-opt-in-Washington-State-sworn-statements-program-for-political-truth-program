# Consequential-claim gap matrix

| WA-OATH-01 | A Washington notary may administer an oath or affirmation and take a verification on oath or affirmation. | Supported | RCW 5.28.010–.020; RCW 42.45.010(8), (18); RCW 42.45.020. | A verification requires a signed statement in a record; standalone oral-oath certificate mechanics contain a DOL/statutory tension. | Use a fixed-record jurat unless counsel and the commissioned notary resolve the oral certificate issue. |
| WA-FS-01 | A knowingly false statement under an oath required or authorized by law can constitute false swearing without materiality or an official proceeding. | Supported with material application limits | RCW 9A.72.040; RCW 9A.72.010(5); *In re Recall of Pearsall-Stipek*, 141 Wn.2d 756; 1995 Wash. | WA-PERJ-01 | A private DGG notarial session necessarily qualifies as an “official proceeding” for first-degree perjury. | Unsupported as a categorical claim | RCW 9A.72.010(4) includes a notary or other person taking testimony/depositions; RCW 9A.72.020 supplies elements. | The statute requires a “proceeding heard before” the listed body/person. | WA-REMOTE-2026-01 | During 2026, a remote signed electronic verification on a defined record is authorized. | Supported with compliance limitations | RCW 42.45.280 effective until 2027-01-01; WAC 308-30-290 through -330; RCW 42.45.030(2). | Requires notary physically in Washington, correct venue/endorsements, compliant platform, identity process, same-record confirmation, electronic signature/certificate association, audiovisual recording, journal, security, and retention. | Verify commission/endorsements/provider; dry-run the complete chain and interruption restart. |

| WA-LEWIS-01 | *State v. Lewis* establishes that no theatrical ritual is required for an oath. | Supported with narrow holding | *State v. | WA-SMITH-OTTON-01 | *Smith* and *Otton* establish that a private notarial session is an official proceeding for perjury. | Unsupported; likely category error | *State v. | WA-PROOF-01 | A contradiction or fact-check alone establishes perjury or false swearing. | Unsupported as a categorical claim | *State v. Arquette*, 178 Wn. | WA-PERJ2-01 | An ordinary DGG interview supports second-degree perjury merely because a notary participates or voters may be misled. | Unsupported on ordinary facts | RCW 9A.72.030; RCW 9A.04.110(23). | The statute requires an insurance examination or intent to mislead a public servant in performance of a duty. | WA-RETRACT-01 | A later correction automatically prevents liability. | Unsupported as categorical claim | RCW 9A.72.060. | Statutory protection requires retraction in the same proceeding before exposure becomes manifest and before substantial effect. | WA-UNKNOWN-01 | An unqualified answer about a fact the participant does not know can be treated like a knowingly false statement. | Supported as statutory rule | RCW 9A.72.080. | Application remains fact-intensive; wording must avoid pressuring guesses. | Put “I do not know / cannot certify / need to qualify” options in every form and script. |
| WA-JUR-01 | Washington has automatic jurisdiction over an out-of-state participant using a Washington remote notary. | Unsupported as categorical claim | RCW 9A.04.030(1), (5), and (6). | Subsection (6) expressly covers chapter 5.50 declarations, not every notarized statement. | WA-EXTR-01 | Extradition is legally conceivable after a valid charge but is automatic or likely. | First clause supported; second clause unsupported | Chapter 10.88 RCW; RCW 10.88.250; RCW 10.96.060. | Extradition requires charging and executive/judicial processes; practical likelihood for a gross misdemeanor is not established by the statutes. | State procedure only; do not estimate likelihood without prosecutor/counsel evidence. |
