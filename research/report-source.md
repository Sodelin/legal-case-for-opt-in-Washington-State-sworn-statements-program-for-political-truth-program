# Research source: Digital Ground Game sworn-statement memorandum

**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-30  
**Role:** Primary-source research record for the canonical memorandum.  
**Scope:** A private, voluntary Digital Ground Game (DGG) pilot anchored in Washington law; Pennsylvania as comparator; federal law for constitutional and federal-offense boundaries.

## Controlling thesis under review

DGG can operate a carefully designed voluntary sworn-record format in Washington. The most defensible present criminal-law reference point is potential Washington false swearing, not an assurance of first-degree perjury. The project should be justified principally as a commitment, provenance, and audience-calibration system—not as a private prosecution mechanism.

## Washington criminal-law authorities

### Chapter 9A.72 RCW

- [Chapter 9A.72 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true) — current definitions and offenses. Key propositions:
  - RCW 9A.72.010(2): materiality means a false statement that could affect the course or outcome of the proceeding.
  - RCW 9A.72.010(3): definition of oath; written statement routes.
  - RCW 9A.72.010(4): official proceeding includes proceedings before listed governmental bodies and officials, including a notary or another person taking testimony or depositions.
  - RCW 9A.72.010(5): an oath is required or authorized by law when specifically provided by statute/rule **or** administered by a person legally authorized to administer oaths.
  - RCW 9A.72.020: first-degree perjury requires a materially false statement, known false, under an authorized oath, in an official proceeding; class B felony.
  - RCW 9A.72.030: second-degree perjury concerns an insurance examination under oath or an intent to mislead a public servant; class C felony. It is not the ordinary DGG theory.
  - RCW 9A.72.040: false swearing requires a knowingly false statement under an oath required or authorized by law; gross misdemeanor. It does not state an official-proceeding or materiality element.
  - RCW 9A.72.050: inconsistent material statements in official proceedings may be pleaded in the alternative without proving which was false.
  - RCW 9A.72.060: retraction is a narrow same-proceeding rule, limited by timing and effect.
  - RCW 9A.72.070: specified oath irregularities are not defenses.
  - RCW 9A.72.080: an unqualified statement of what the speaker does not know to be true is treated like a knowingly false statement.

### Statutory history and adverse authority

- [*State v. Hovrud*, 60 Wn. App. 573, 805 P.2d 250 (1991)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1991/60-wash-app-573.html) — under the then-current definition, an insurance-policy oath not specifically provided by statute or regulation did not support false swearing. The decision applies strict construction and fair-warning principles.
- [1995 Washington Laws chapter 285, section 30](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/1995-96/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1557-S2.SL.htm) — after *Hovrud*, amended the definition to add the alternative route now in RCW 9A.72.010(5): an oath administered by a person authorized by state or federal law. This materially reduces *Hovrud*'s force against a modern oath actually administered by an authorized Washington notary. No reported appellate decision squarely applying the amended definition to a voluntary private-media notarial act was located.

### Oath and proceeding cases

- [*In re Recall of Pearsall-Stipek*, 141 Wn.2d 756, 766–71, 10 P.3d 1034 (2000)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wa-supreme-court/1486118.html) — the Washington Supreme Court held in a recall-sufficiency case that false swearing has no materiality limitation and can apply to knowingly false statements under oath in or out of official proceedings. The decision confirms the statutory distinction but is not a criminal conviction and does not address a private political-media format.
- [*State v. Lewis*, 85 Wn.2d 769, 539 P.2d 677 (1975)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1975/43613-1.html) — no prescribed ritual is necessary when an unequivocal act shows deliberate assumption of the obligation of an oath. The majority limited its holding to the perjury question on those facts and did not generally validate an otherwise defective notarization. The archival pilot packet's citation “72 Wn.2d 427” is incorrect.
- [*State v. Jacobson*, 74 Wn. App. 715, 876 P.2d 916 (1994)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1994/74-wash-app-715.html) — a written affidavit prepared for, submitted to, and used in a court hearing can support first-degree perjury; a statement may be “in” an official proceeding if given in or in aid of it. This does not decide whether a private media session before a notary is itself an official proceeding.
- [*State v. Arquette*, 178 Wn. App. 273, 283–90, 314 P.3d 426 (2013)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/D2%2042546-7-II%20%20Published%20Opinion.pdf) — Washington's heightened perjury-proof rule requires direct contradictory testimony plus a second direct witness or sufficiently strong independent corroboration; the court reversed because the corroboration was not inconsistent with innocence. The current pattern instruction extends a corroboration formulation to false swearing, but no appellate false-swearing conviction squarely applying it was located.
- [*State v. Smith*, 97 Wn.2d 856 (1982)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1982/48378-7-1.html) — concerns “other proceeding” under ER 801(d)(1)(i), not the criminal-code definition of official proceeding.
- [*State v. Otton*, 185 Wn.2d 673 (2016)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/2016/91669-1.html) — likewise an evidence-rule decision. *Smith* and *Otton* may illustrate that formality and oath can add evidentiary reliability, but they do not prove a DGG session satisfies RCW 9A.72.010(4).

## Washington notarial authorities

- [Chapter 5.28 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=5.28&full=true) — Washington notaries may administer oaths generally; the statutory form for oral testimony includes a promise to make “true answers.” This supplies a strong in-person standalone-oath route.
- [Washington DOL, “Administering oath or affirmation”](https://dol.wa.gov/book/export/html/3756) and [“Taking verification on oath or affirmation”](https://dol.wa.gov/guides/washington-state-notary-public-guide/specific-notarial-acts/taking-verification-oath-or-affirmation) — administrative guidance distinguishes a primarily verbal oath from a signed written verification. The oath page says no certificate is required for a verbal oath, while RCW 42.45.130 states that a notarial act must be evidenced by a certificate. Use a fixed-record jurat unless DOL, counsel, and the commissioned notary resolve that tension for a standalone oral act.
- [Chapter 42.45 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.45&full=true) — Washington Revised Uniform Law on Notarial Acts.
- [RCW 42.45.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.010) — “notarial act” includes administering an oath and taking a verification; a “record” may be tangible or electronic; a verification is a declaration under oath that a statement in a record is true.
- [RCW 42.45.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.030) — for a verification, the individual must make the statement and sign the statement in the notary's presence as legally defined.
- [RCW 42.45.040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.040) — personal appearance, with the remote-act exception.
- [RCW 42.45.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.050) — identity standards.
- [RCW 42.45.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.060) — a notary may refuse if not satisfied about competence/capacity or knowing and voluntary signature, and generally may refuse unless other law prohibits it.
- [RCW 42.45.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.020) — a notarial act involving the notary's or specified close relations' direct beneficial interest is voidable. An independent notary reduces conflict and endorsement risk.
- [RCW 42.45.130](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.130) — contemporaneous certificate, title, jurisdiction, commission-expiration, remote-technology notation, and attachment/association requirements.
- [RCW 42.45.180](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.180) — contemporaneous journal, required contents, security, and ten-year retention.
- [RCW 42.45.230](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.230) — among other restrictions, a notary may not retain record copies solely for the notarial transaction or improperly practice law, select a certificate for another, act as a court reporter, or use misleading advertising.
- [RCW 42.45.280 (effective until 2027-01-01)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.280) — current remote-notarial-act framework: communication technology, identity, record confirmation, audiovisual recording, certificate, and retention.
- [Chapter 308-30 WAC](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=308-30&full=true) — current notary rules. Important provisions include:
  - WAC 308-30-290: remote verification on oath or affirmation is authorized; standalone remote administration of an oath is not listed.
  - WAC 308-30-300 and -310: identity proofing and communication technology.
  - WAC 308-30-320: remote jurat wording (“signed and sworn … by means of communication technology”).
  - WAC 308-30-330: password or equivalent protection, backup, repository terms, and at least ten-year retention for the audiovisual notarial recording; the recording must not include images of the electronic record being notarized.
  - WAC 308-30-020: notarial venue is the Washington place where the notary is physically located, not the participant's location.
  - WAC 308-30-110: a Washington notary performs the act while physically in Washington.
  - WAC 308-30-200: the journal is the notary's exclusive property; a remote-act entry notes the remote method and need not contain the remote signer's journal signature.
  - WAC 308-30-220: current fee ceilings are $15 for listed in-person acts and $25 for a remote notarial act, apart from separately disclosed permissible costs.
  - WAC 308-30-230: a notary may not use the office/title/seal to endorse or promote a product, service, contest, or other offering.
- [Washington Department of Licensing notary portal](https://dol.wa.gov/professional-licenses/notaries-public) — licensing and current administrative guidance. Statute/rule controls if summaries conflict.

## Washington territorial, timing, and interstate authorities

- [RCW 9A.04.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.030) — territorial jurisdiction. Relevant routes include a crime committed wholly or partly in Washington, an out-of-state act affecting people or property in Washington that would be criminal if committed inside the state, and an express route for specified chapter 5.50 declarations made outside Washington. The chapter 5.50 route does not categorically cover every remote-notarized DGG record.
- [*State v. Jacobson*, 74 Wn. App. 715](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1994/74-wash-app-715.html) — applies RCW 9A.04.030(5) where an out-of-state property transfer affected Washington residents' property interests and frustrated a Washington court order. This is a much stronger Washington effect than the ordinary publication of a political interview; use only as an illustration of the required nexus.
- [RCW 9A.04.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.080) — ordinary limitation periods and tolling while the accused is not usually and publicly resident in Washington.
- [*State v. Willingham*, 169 Wn.2d 192, 234 P.3d 211 (2010)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/2010/840369-opn.html) — a short out-of-state trip did not necessarily end Washington residency; the cases applying tolling involved relocation or residence outside Washington.
- [*State v. McDonald*, 100 Wn. App. 828, 1 P.3d 1176 (2000)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/2000/100-wash-app-828.html) — out-of-state residence tolled the limitation period even where the person did not conceal herself and return mechanisms existed; rejected the asserted Commerce Clause challenge.
- [RCW 9A.20.021](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.20.021) — statutory maximums: class B felony, ten years and/or $20,000; class C felony, five years and/or $10,000; gross misdemeanor, up to 364 days and/or $5,000. These are ceilings, not predictions of charge or sentence.
- [Chapter 10.88 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=10.88&full=true) — Washington Uniform Criminal Extradition Act.
- [RCW 10.88.250](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.88.250) — permits, but does not require, a governor to surrender a person who was not in the demanding state when an out-of-state act intentionally resulted in a crime there.
- [RCW 10.96.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.96.060) — criminal process to recipients inside or outside Washington for matters within RCW 9A.04.030. It does not itself establish territorial jurisdiction or guarantee extradition.

Chapter 10.88 also supplies notice, counsel, judicial-process, and executive-discretion protections. Its special warrantless-arrest route in RCW 10.88.330 requires an offense punishable by more than one year; false swearing's 364-day maximum does not satisfy that particular route. That does not decide other available process or another state's corresponding law.

## Washington recording and records authorities

- [RCW 9.73.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.030) — generally requires all-party consent to record private communications/conversations; an announcement recorded with the conversation can establish consent under subsection (3).
- [RCW 9.73.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.050) — unlawfully obtained recordings are generally inadmissible in Washington courts.
- [RCW 9.73.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.060) — civil remedy, including actual damages, statutory damages, and attorney fees.
- [RCW 9.73.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.080) — knowing violation of chapter 9.73 is a gross misdemeanor.

Required notarial recording and retention do not equal consent to editorial publication. DGG needs distinct, recorded consent and a written release for: (1) the notarial act and statutory archive; (2) the program's production recording; and (3) editing, excerpting, publication, syndication, and preservation.

An ordinary Zoom/Meet call is not enough unless the complete workflow also satisfies the remote-notary rules. If a participant exits the remote-notary workflow, current WAC 308-30-310 requires restarting identity verification. Credential-analysis and knowledge-based-authentication data must remain outside DGG's custody; the notary may not see or record KBA answers.

## Charging authority

- [RCW 36.27.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=36.27.020) — prosecuting attorneys represent the state and prosecute criminal actions. DGG may preserve and refer evidence, but it cannot promise a charge, conviction, sentence, or extradition.

## Federal constitutional, media, and election-law authorities

- [*Manhattan Community Access Corp. v. Halleck*, 587 U.S. 802, 808–09, 812–14 (2019)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/587BV.pdf) — the Free Speech Clause ordinarily regulates governmental, not private, abridgment; a license, contract, or monopoly does not by itself create state action. Applying this rule to DGG is an inference because no controlling case concerns a private publisher using a commissioned notary.
- [*303 Creative LLC v. Elenis*, 600 U.S. 570 (2023)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-476_c185.pdf) and [*Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo*, 418 U.S. 241, 256–58 (1974)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-418/pdf/USREPORTS-418-241.pdf) — government-compelled expression and compelled publication/editorial interference are materially different from a private voluntary format. DGG should not propose the format as a governmental qualification for candidacy or public access.
- [*New York Times Co. v. Sullivan*, 376 U.S. 254, 279–80 (1964)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-376/pdf/USREPORTS-376-254.pdf) and [*Monitor Patriot Co. v. Roy*, 401 U.S. 265, 271–72 (1971)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-401/pdf/USREPORTS-401-265.pdf) — candidate/public-official coverage and the actual-malice standard. That fault standard is not immunity from a false status or implication.
- [*Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.*, 497 U.S. 1, 18–21 (1990)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-497/pdf/USREPORTS-497-1.pdf), [*Masson v. New Yorker Magazine*, 501 U.S. 496, 516–17 (1991)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-501/pdf/USREPORTS-501-496.pdf), and [*Time, Inc. v. Hill*, 385 U.S. 374, 387–88 (1967)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-385/pdf/USREPORTS-385-374.pdf) — provably false implications, material gist/sting, and public-interest false light. A literally accurate “declined” status can still be risky if presentation implies lying, fear of perjury, or failure of an integrity test.
- [18 U.S.C. §§ 1621 and 1623](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/pdf/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap79.pdf) and [18 U.S.C. § 1001](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/pdf/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap47-front.pdf) — an ordinary private interview does not acquire federal perjury or federal false-statement status merely because the guest is a federal candidate or officeholder.
- [47 U.S.C. § 315(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title47/pdf/USCODE-2024-title47-chap5.pdf) — candidate-use rules for broadcast-station licensees, including news-program exceptions. No general federal equal-time rule was located for an ordinary website interview.
- [52 U.S.C. § 30101(9)(B)(i)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title52/pdf/USCODE-2024-title52-subtitleIII-chap301-subchapI.pdf), [11 C.F.R. § 100.73](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-100/subpart-C/section-100.73), [11 C.F.R. § 109.21](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-109/subpart-C/section-109.21), and [11 C.F.R. § 110.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-110/section-110.13) — media/press exemption, coordinated-communication test, and staged-debate rules. DGG's ownership, control, FEC status, payment, candidate involvement, and format must be determined before relying on an exemption.

## Pennsylvania comparator

- [57 Pa.C.S. §§ 302, 304–306.1, 311, and 315](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute&txtType=PDF&ttl=57) — Pennsylvania notarial definitions, conflicts, act requirements, remote notarization, recognition of out-of-state notarial acts, and certificate rules. Pennsylvania is a strong notarial-mechanics analogue: an authorized Pennsylvania notary located in Pennsylvania may remotely notarize for a U.S. signer elsewhere, and Pennsylvania recognizes a valid Washington notarial act. Recognition supplies notarial effect; it does not import Washington criminal law.
- [4 Pa. Code chapter 167, subchapter H](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/004/chapter167/subchapHtoc.html) and [§ 167.86](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/004/chapter167/s167.86.html) — permanent Pennsylvania remote-notary rules effective 2026-03-28, including technology, identity, audiovisual recording, and ten-year retention requirements.
- [Pennsylvania Department of State remote-notarization guidance](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dos/resources/notaries-resources-and-information/electronic-or-remote-notarization) — current administrative resource; statute and final regulations control.
- [18 Pa.C.S. §§ 4501 and 4902–4904](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute&txtType=PDF&ttl=18) — Pennsylvania perjury requires a materially false sworn statement in an official proceeding; false swearing generally requires an official proceeding, intent to mislead a public servant, or a statement the law requires to be sworn; unsworn falsification likewise depends on specified governmental/legal gateways. A private voluntary DGG statement ordinarily does not fit these elements merely because it is notarized.
- [18 Pa.C.S. § 102](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute&txtType=PDF&ttl=18) — Pennsylvania territorial jurisdiction can locate conduct or results in Pennsylvania but cannot supply a missing offense element.
- [*Rubin v. CBS Broadcasting Inc.*, 170 A.3d 560, 568 (Pa. Super. Ct. 2017)](https://www.pacourts.us/assets/opinions/Superior/out/090817e.pdf?cb=1) — Pennsylvania recognizes false-impression risk from selective presentation of true facts; use exact invitation/response statuses and contextual disclaimer.

**Comparator conclusion:** Pennsylvania can replicate identity, oath, remote appearance, certificate, and recognition mechanics. It is not a parallel private false-swearing deterrence jurisdiction. DGG should describe a Pennsylvania implementation as an integrity/provenance device unless Pennsylvania counsel identifies an independent legally required oath or governmental proceeding.

## Empirical authorities supporting a bounded pilot—not a guaranteed outcome

- [Zickfeld et al., “Effectiveness of ex ante honesty oaths in reducing dishonesty depends on content,” *Nature Human Behaviour* 9 (2025), 169–187](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02009-0) — megastudy of 21 oath variants and a no-oath condition involving 21,506 UK/US participants in an incentivized tax-reporting game. Ten variants significantly increased compliance by 4.5–8.5 percentage points; results differed by content. Transfer to political interviews is untested.
- [Jacquemet et al., “Do truth-telling oaths improve honesty in crowd-working?” *PLOS ONE* 16 (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7810327/) — roughly 1,400 online workers. A voluntary oath reduced the frequency of maximally false reports by roughly 27%, modestly reduced mean overreporting, and did not reduce shirking. The authors expressly characterize the oath as not a panacea.
- [Jacquemet et al., “Truth Telling Under Oath,” *Management Science* 65 (2019), 426–438](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2892) — laboratory results showed stronger reductions in lying in a context that made the moral meaning of lying salient and weaker effects in a neutral context.

These studies support testing a specific, voluntary commitment intervention. They do not prove that notarization improves political truth, that sanctions will be enforced, or that audiences will interpret the format correctly. DGG should preregister outcomes and adverse effects.

## Archival-input defects and quarantine decisions

1. The pilot packet miscites *Lewis* as 72 Wn.2d 427; correct citation is 85 Wn.2d 769.
2. *Smith* and *Otton* are evidence-rule cases, not direct RCW 9A.72 criminal-element cases.
3. Any 2026 workflow treating a wholly oral remote interview as independently authorized is quarantined; use a signed electronic verification.
4. “DDG” in archival drafts is not silently changed; the canonical work uses DGG.
5. Any statement that notarization certifies truth, that a refusal implies dishonesty, or that prosecution/extradition is automatic is prohibited.
