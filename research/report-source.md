## Controlling thesis under review

Washington law supplies a plausible basis to continue evaluating a carefully designed voluntary sworn-record format. The most defensible present criminal-law reference point is potential Washington false swearing, not an assurance of first-degree perjury. While the remand remains open, the only permitted Phase 0 work is a pure fictional mock with no real signer, oath, or notarial act. A nonpublic, in-person, Washington-only paper record remains a possible future actual-test risk control after reviewed supersession and all gates. The project should be evaluated principally as a commitment, provenance, and audience-calibration system—not as a private prosecution mechanism.

## Washington criminal-law authorities

### Chapter 9A.72 RCW

- [Chapter 9A.72 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9A.72&full=true) — current definitions and offenses. Key propositions:
  - RCW 9A.72.010(2): materiality means a false statement that could affect the course or outcome of the proceeding.
  - RCW 9A.72.010(3): definition of oath; written statement routes.
  - RCW 9A.72.010(4): official proceeding includes proceedings before listed governmental bodies and officials, including a notary or another person taking testimony or depositions.
  - RCW 9A.72.010(5): an oath is required or authorized by law when specifically provided by statute/rule **or** administered by a person legally authorized to administer oaths.
  - RCW 9A.72.020: first-degree perjury requires a materially false statement, known false, under an authorized oath, in an official proceeding; class B felony.
  - RCW 9A.72.030: second-degree perjury concerns an insurance examination under oath or an intent to mislead a public servant; class C felony. It is not the ordinary DGG theory.
  - RCW 9A.72.040: false swearing requires a knowingly false statement under an oath required or authorized by law; gross misdemeanor. It does not state an official-proceeding or materiality element.
  - RCW 9A.72.050: inconsistent material statements in official proceedings may be pleaded in the alternative without proving which was false.
  - RCW 9A.72.060: retraction is a narrow same-proceeding rule, limited by timing and effect.
  - RCW 9A.72.070: specified oath irregularities are not defenses.
  - RCW 9A.72.080: an unqualified statement of what the speaker does not know to be true is treated like a knowingly false statement.

### Statutory history and adverse authority

- [*State v. Hovrud*, 60 Wn. App. 573, 805 P.2d 250 (1991)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1991/60-wash-app-573.html) — under the then-current definition, an insurance-policy oath not specifically provided by statute or regulation did not support false swearing. The decision applies strict construction and fair-warning principles.
- [1995 Washington Laws chapter 285, section 30](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/1995-96/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1557-S2.SL.htm) — after *Hovrud*, amended the definition to add the alternative route now in RCW 9A.72.010(5): an oath administered by a person authorized by state or federal law.
- [2001 Washington Laws chapter 171, sections 1–2](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2001-02/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/House/1692.SL.htm) — expressly reenacted the 1995 definition without change in response to *State v. Thomas*, 103 Wn. App. 800. The amended and reenacted text materially reduces *Hovrud*'s force against a modern oath administered by an authorized Washington notary. No reported appellate decision squarely applying the authorized-administrator route to a voluntary private-media notarial act was located.

### Oath and proceeding cases

- [*In re Recall of Pearsall-Stipek*, 141 Wn.2d 756, 774–78, 10 P.3d 1034 (2000)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wa-supreme-court/1486118.html) — the Washington Supreme Court held in a recall-sufficiency case that false swearing has no materiality limitation and can apply to knowingly false statements under oath in or out of official proceedings; retraction is discussed at page 779. The decision confirms the statutory distinction but is not a criminal conviction and does not address a private political-media format.
- [*State v. Lewis*, 85 Wn.2d 769, 539 P.2d 677 (1975)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1975/43613-1.html) — no prescribed ritual is necessary when an unequivocal act shows deliberate assumption of the obligation of an oath. The case involved the notary's absence, and the majority limited its holding to the perjury question on those facts; it did not generally validate defective notarization. The archival pilot packet's citation “72 Wn.2d 427” is incorrect.
- [*State v. Jacobson*, 74 Wn. App. 715, 876 P.2d 916 (1994)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1994/74-wash-app-715.html) — a written affidavit prepared for, submitted to, and used in a court hearing can support first-degree perjury; a statement may be “in” an official proceeding if given in or in aid of it. This does not decide whether a private media session before a notary is itself an official proceeding.
- [*State v. Arquette*, 178 Wn. App. 273, 283–90, 314 P.3d 426 (2013)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/D2%2042546-7-II%20%20Published%20Opinion.pdf) — Washington's heightened perjury-proof rule requires direct contradictory testimony plus a second direct witness or sufficiently strong independent corroboration; the court reversed because the corroboration was not inconsistent with innocence. [WPIC 118.12, “Perjury or False Swearing—Requirements of Proof”](https://govt.westlaw.com/wciji/Document/Iefb2872ae10d11daade1ae871d9b2cbe?contextData=%28sc.Default%29&originationContext=documenttoc&transitionType=CategoryPageItem&viewType=FullText), extends a corroboration formulation to false swearing, but it is committee guidance rather than a binding holding, and no appellate false-swearing conviction squarely applying it was located.
- [*State v. Smith*, 97 Wn.2d 856 (1982)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1982/48378-7-1.html) — concerns “other proceeding” under ER 801(d)(1)(i), not the criminal-code definition of official proceeding.
- [*State v. Otton*, 185 Wn.2d 673 (2016)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/2016/91669-1.html) — likewise an evidence-rule decision. *Smith* and *Otton* may illustrate that formality and oath can add evidentiary reliability, but they do not prove a DGG session satisfies RCW 9A.72.010(4).

## Washington notarial authorities

- [Chapter 5.28 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=5.28&full=true) — Washington notaries may administer oaths generally; the statutory form for oral testimony includes a promise to make “true answers.” This supplies a strong in-person standalone-oath route.
- [Washington DOL, “Administering oath or affirmation”](https://dol.wa.gov/guides/washington-state-notary-public-guide/specific-notarial-acts/administering-oath-or-affirmation) and [“Taking verification on oath or affirmation”](https://dol.wa.gov/guides/washington-state-notary-public-guide/specific-notarial-acts/taking-verification-oath-or-affirmation) — administrative guidance distinguishes a primarily verbal oath from a signed written verification. The oath page says no certificate is required for a verbal oath, while RCW 42.45.130 states that every notarial act must be evidenced by a certificate. Use a fixed-record jurat unless rulemaking, authoritative interpretation, or controlling law resolves the tension; counsel or an individual notary cannot eliminate a statutory conflict.
- [Chapter 42.45 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.45&full=true) — Washington Revised Uniform Law on Notarial Acts.
- [RCW 42.45.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.010) — “notarial act” includes administering an oath and taking a verification; a “record” may be tangible or electronic; a verification is a declaration under oath that a statement in a record is true.
- [RCW 42.45.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.030) — for a verification, the individual must make the statement and sign the statement in the notary's presence as legally defined.
- [RCW 42.45.040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.040) — personal appearance, with the remote-act exception.
- [RCW 42.45.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.050) — identity standards.
- [RCW 42.45.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.060) — a notary may refuse if not satisfied about competence/capacity or knowing and voluntary signature, and generally may refuse unless other law prohibits it.
- [RCW 42.45.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.020) — a notarial act involving the notary's or specified close relations' direct beneficial interest is voidable. An independent notary reduces conflict and endorsement risk.
- [RCW 42.45.130](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.130) — contemporaneous certificate, title, jurisdiction, commission-expiration, remote-technology notation, and attachment/association requirements. A certificate evidences the act and certifies specified compliance/determinations; the statutory short form is not the only compliant form.
- [RCW 42.45.180](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.180) — contemporaneous journal, required contents, security, and retention for ten years after the last act chronicled. A Washington attorney-notary is exempt when professional-practice documentation is otherwise maintained.
- [RCW 42.45.230](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.230) — among other restrictions, a notary may not retain record copies solely for the notarial transaction or improperly practice law, select a certificate for another, act as a court reporter, or use misleading advertising.
- [RCW 42.45.280 (effective until 2027-01-01)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.45.280) — current remote-notarial-act framework: communication technology, alternative identity routes, record confirmation, audiovisual recording, certificate, and retention. Personal knowledge, a qualifying credible witness, or two types of identity proofing can establish identity. A participant outside the United States triggers additional transaction-nexus and foreign-law conditions.
- [Chapter 308-30 WAC](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=308-30&full=true) — current notary rules. Important provisions include:
  - WAC 308-30-290: remote verification on oath or affirmation is authorized; standalone remote administration of an oath is not listed.
  - WAC 308-30-300 and -310: identity and communication technology. Credential analysis plus dynamic knowledge-based authentication applies when neither personal knowledge nor a qualifying credible witness supplies satisfactory evidence. Identity verification restarts when the remotely located individual must exit the workflow.
  - WAC 308-30-320: remote jurat wording (“signed and sworn … by means of communication technology”).
  - WAC 308-30-330: password or equivalent protection, backup, repository terms, and at least ten-year retention for the audiovisual notarial recording; the recording must not include images of the electronic record being notarized.
  - WAC 308-30-020: notarial venue is the Washington place where the notary is physically located, not the participant's location.
  - WAC 308-30-110: a Washington notary performs the act while physically in Washington.
  - WAC 308-30-200: the journal is the notary's exclusive property; a remote-act entry notes the remote method and need not contain the remote signer's journal signature.
  - WAC 308-30-220: current fee ceilings are $15 for a listed in-person act and $25 total for a remote notarial act. Express add-ons are actual copying costs and pre-agreed travel disclosed as additional and not required by law.
  - WAC 308-30-230: a notary may not endorse or promote an offering when the notary's title or seal is used. A broader no-testimonial rule is DGG policy.
- [Washington Department of Licensing notary portal](https://dol.wa.gov/professional-licenses/notaries-public) — licensing and administrative guidance. Some live DOL Guide pages remain stale about remote authority, signer location, and fees. Current RCW, WAC, session laws, and the Washington State Register control over conflicting guide prose.

## Washington territorial, timing, and interstate authorities

- [RCW 9A.04.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.030) — territorial jurisdiction. Relevant routes include a crime committed wholly or partly in Washington, an out-of-state act affecting people or property in Washington that would be criminal if committed inside the state, and an express route for specified chapter 5.50 declarations made outside Washington. The chapter 5.50 route does not categorically cover every remote-notarized DGG record.
- [*State v. Jacobson*, 74 Wn. App. 715](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/1994/74-wash-app-715.html) — applies RCW 9A.04.030(5) where an out-of-state property transfer affected Washington residents' property interests and frustrated a Washington court order. This is a much stronger Washington effect than the ordinary publication of a political interview; use only as an illustration of the required nexus.
- [RCW 9A.04.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.04.080) — ordinary limitation periods and tolling while the accused is not usually and publicly resident in Washington. The ordinary period is three years for an otherwise unlisted felony and two years for a gross misdemeanor. A fact-specific ten-year branch applies to a felony committed by a public officer in connection with official duties, as a breach of public duty, or in violation of the oath of office; that could affect a first-degree-perjury theory involving an officeholder but does not change false swearing's two-year period.
- [*State v. Willingham*, 169 Wn.2d 192, 234 P.3d 211 (2010)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/2010/840369-opn.html) — a short out-of-state trip did not necessarily end Washington residency; the cases applying tolling involved relocation or residence outside Washington.
- [*State v. McDonald*, 100 Wn. App. 828, 1 P.3d 1176 (2000)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/court-of-appeals/2000/100-wash-app-828.html) — out-of-state residence tolled the limitation period even where the person did not conceal herself and return mechanisms existed; rejected the asserted Commerce Clause challenge.
- [RCW 9A.20.021](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9A.20.021) — statutory maximums: class B felony, ten years and/or $20,000; class C felony, five years and/or $10,000; gross misdemeanor, up to 364 days and/or $5,000. These are ceilings, not predictions of charge or sentence.
- [Chapter 10.88 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=10.88&full=true) — Washington Uniform Criminal Extradition Act.
- [RCW 10.88.250](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.88.250) — governs Washington as the asylum state: Washington's governor may surrender a person found in Washington to another demanding state when the statutory conditions are met, including specified nonfugitive conduct. It does not govern another state's surrender of a person to Washington.
- [RCW 10.88.400](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.88.400), [RCW 10.88.410](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.88.410), and [18 U.S.C. § 3182](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section3182&num=0&edition=prelim) — address an outgoing Washington demand and the federal fugitive framework. Actual surrender by another state, especially for a person never present in Washington, depends on that asylum state's law and executive/judicial process.
- [RCW 10.96.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.96.060) — criminal process to recipients inside or outside Washington for matters within RCW 9A.04.030. It does not itself establish territorial jurisdiction or guarantee extradition.

Chapter 10.88 also supplies notice, counsel, judicial-process, and executive-discretion protections. Its special warrantless-arrest route in RCW 10.88.330 requires an offense punishable by more than one year; false swearing's 364-day maximum does not satisfy that particular route. That does not decide other available process or another state's corresponding law.

## Washington recording and records authorities

- [RCW 9.73.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.030) — generally requires all-party consent to record private communications/conversations; an announcement recorded with the conversation can establish consent under subsection (3).
- [RCW 9.73.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.050) — unlawfully obtained recordings are generally inadmissible in Washington courts.
- [RCW 9.73.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.060) — civil remedy, including actual damages, statutory damages, and attorney fees.
- [RCW 9.73.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=9.73.080) — knowing violation of chapter 9.73 is a gross misdemeanor.

Required notarial recording and retention do not equal consent to editorial publication. RCW 9.73.030(3) may establish recording consent through a recorded reasonably effective announcement. As a stricter project and media-risk policy, DGG should also require a written release covering: (1) the notarial act and statutory archive; (2) the program's production recording; and (3) editing, excerpting, publication, syndication, and preservation.

An ordinary Zoom/Meet call is not enough unless the complete workflow also satisfies the remote-notary rules. If a participant must exit the remote-notary workflow, current WAC 308-30-310 requires restarting identity verification. Credential analysis and dynamic knowledge-based authentication apply only when the identity-proofing route is used; WAC 308-30-300 requires that the notary not see or record the KBA questions or answers. Keeping all credential-analysis material outside DGG's custody is an additional security policy.

## Charging authority

- [RCW 36.27.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=36.27.020) — prosecuting attorneys represent the state and prosecute criminal actions. DGG may preserve and refer evidence, but it cannot promise a charge, conviction, sentence, or extradition.

## Federal constitutional, media, and election-law authorities

- [*Manhattan Community Access Corp. v. Halleck*, 587 U.S. 802, 808–09, 812–14 (2019)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/587BV.pdf) — the Free Speech Clause ordinarily regulates governmental, not private, abridgment; a license, contract, or monopoly does not by itself create state action. Applying this rule to DGG is an inference because no controlling case concerns a private publisher using a commissioned notary.
- [*303 Creative LLC v. Elenis*, 600 U.S. 570 (2023)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-476_c185.pdf) and [*Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo*, 418 U.S. 241, 256–58 (1974)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-418/pdf/USREPORTS-418-241.pdf) — government-compelled expression and compelled publication/editorial interference are materially different from a private voluntary format. DGG should not propose the format as a governmental qualification for candidacy or public access.
- [*New York Times Co. v. Sullivan*, 376 U.S. 254, 279–80 (1964)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-376/pdf/USREPORTS-376-254.pdf) and [*Monitor Patriot Co. v. Roy*, 401 U.S. 265, 271–72 (1971)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-401/pdf/USREPORTS-401-265.pdf) — candidate/public-official coverage and the actual-malice standard. That fault standard is not immunity from a false status or implication.
- [*Sisley v. Seattle Public Schools*, 180 Wn. App. 83 (2014)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/693166.pdf) and [*Jha v. Khan*, 24 Wn. App. 2d 377 (2022)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/837681.pdf) — Washington defamation-by-implication and false-light law require a provably false factual statement or impression. Material omission can create one, but mere negative implication or juxtaposition of substantially true facts generally is insufficient. [*Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.*, 497 U.S. 1, 18–21 (1990)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-497/pdf/USREPORTS-497-1.pdf), [*Masson v. New Yorker Magazine*, 501 U.S. 496, 516–17 (1991)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-501/pdf/USREPORTS-501-496.pdf), and [*Time, Inc. v. Hill*, 385 U.S. 374, 387–88 (1967)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-385/pdf/USREPORTS-385-374.pdf) supply related federal boundaries. A disclaimer is a risk control, not a safe harbor.
- [18 U.S.C. §§ 1621 and 1623](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/pdf/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap79.pdf), [28 U.S.C. § 1746](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title28-section1746&num=0&edition=prelim), and [18 U.S.C. § 1001](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/pdf/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap47-front.pdf) — an ordinary statement confined to a private interview does not acquire federal perjury or false-statement status merely because the guest is a federal candidate or officeholder. A qualifying § 1746 declaration, submission in a federally authorized proceeding, or statement routed through a private intermediary for use in a federal matter requires a different analysis.
- [47 U.S.C. § 315(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title47/pdf/USCODE-2024-title47-chap5.pdf), [47 C.F.R. § 73.1941](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-73/subpart-H/section-73.1941), [47 C.F.R. § 76.205](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-76/subpart-G/section-76.205), and [FCC Media Bureau DA 26-68](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-68A1.pdf) — “equal opportunities” and fact-specific bona fide-news rules for regulated distribution; an ordinary website alone is outside the broadcast rule, but separate cable-system, DBS, and SDARS origination rules may apply.
- [52 U.S.C. § 30101(8)(B)(i), (9)(B)(i)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title52/pdf/USCODE-2024-title52-subtitleIII-chap301-subchapI.pdf), [11 C.F.R. § 100.73](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-100/subpart-C/section-100.73), [11 C.F.R. § 100.132](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-100/subpart-E/section-100.132), [11 C.F.R. § 109.21](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-109/subpart-C/section-109.21), and [11 C.F.R. § 110.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-110/section-110.13) — contribution/expenditure press exemptions, coordinated-communication test, and staged-debate rules. Coordination uses payer, content, and conduct elements and does not require a formal agreement. A debate requires at least two candidates, no structure promoting one, and pre-established objective criteria.
- [WAC 390-05-400](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=390-05-400) — adjusts RCW 29B.25.120(2)'s printed $100 amount to $1,000 effective 2026-01-01. Washington Title 29B classifications and adjusted thresholds require a separate fact-specific decision tree.

### False political speech, government involvement, and coercion

- [Washington Constitution article I, sections 5–7 and 12](https://leg.wa.gov/state-laws-and-rules/washington-state-constitution/?showall=true) — protects speech, conscience-binding administration of oaths, privacy, and equal privileges or immunities. Section 6 supports accommodating a signer's conscience; it does not make every oath-backed statement criminally punishable.
- [RCW 29B.30.070](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29B.30.070) — Washington's current post-*Rickert* provision is narrow: it addresses specified candidate, incumbency, and endorsement statements that constitute libel or defamation per se, requires actual malice, and uses clear-and-convincing proof. No located Washington appellate merits decision definitively upholds the recodified provision. It is not a general governmental license to decide political truth.
- [*Rickert v. Public Disclosure Commission*, 161 Wn.2d 843, 168 P.3d 826 (2007)](https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/index.cfm?fa=opinions.showOpinion&filename=777691MAJ) — the Washington Supreme Court invalidated the former false-campaign-speech law as reaching protected, nondefamatory speech. The five-justice result is narrower than the strongest “government truth arbiter” reasoning, which did not command five votes. Obtain the Washington Reports text and a current citator before relying on a particular formulation.
- [*State ex rel. Public Disclosure Commission v. 119 Vote No! Committee*, 135 Wn.2d 618, 957 P.2d 691 (1998)](https://law.justia.com/cases/washington/supreme-court/1998/64332-6-1.html) — five justices supported invalidity in the ballot-measure application, but the broad lead reasoning had only three votes; two concurring justices reserved a narrower actual-malice candidate-defamation law. Use as strong adverse authority to governmental truth adjudication, with its fractured posture stated.
- [*United States v. Alvarez*, 567 U.S. 709 (2012)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/567bv.pdf) — false statements are not a categorical unprotected class. The four-Justice plurality and the two-Justice concurrence used different reasoning. Perjury, fraud, impersonation, and false statements to government protect concrete legal or governmental interests; merely adding an oath to broad political speech does not itself establish the necessary constitutional fit.
- [*Susan B. Anthony List v. Driehaus*, 573 U.S. 149 (2014)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/573BV.pdf) — unanimous standing and ripeness decision showing that a complaint, probable-cause process, and interim stigma can impose pre-enforcement chill. The Court did not decide the First Amendment merits.
- [*Ancheta v. Watada*, 135 F. Supp. 2d 1114 (D. Haw. 2001)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/135/1114/2503374/) — the closest located pledge comparator. Hawaii's code was nominally voluntary, but public identification of nonsigners and censure of signers produced unconstitutional coercion and chill. This is persuasive federal trial-court authority outside Washington, available here only through a case mirror; later history and citator treatment remain open.
- [*Agency for International Development v. Alliance for Open Society International*, 570 U.S. 205 (2013)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/570bv.pdf) — government may define a funded program but ordinarily cannot leverage funding to require an organization-wide affirmation outside it. A sworn-record option becomes materially riskier if linked to ballot access, debates, funding, contracts, licenses, credentials, or official recognition.
- [*Shurtleff v. Boston*, 596 U.S. 243 (2022)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/20-1800_7lho.pdf) — history, public perception, and actual control distinguish government speech from a forum for private speech. A governmental repository cannot simply call entries private while excluding disfavored viewpoints; treating entries as government speech requires real adoption, attribution, and control.
- [*Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta*, 594 U.S. 595 (2021)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-251_p86b.pdf), [*McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission*, 514 U.S. 334 (1995)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/514bv.pdf), [*Doe v. Reed*, 561 U.S. 186 (2010)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/561bv.pdf), and [*Citizens United v. FEC*, 558 U.S. 310, 366–71 (2010)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/558bv.pdf) — identity and associational disclosure requires contextual, narrowly tailored analysis. A signer may voluntarily make identity integral to an attestation, but a blanket list of nonparticipants or compelled collection of unnecessary identifiers raises different risks.
- [*Brown v. Hartlage*, 456 U.S. 45 (1982)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-456/pdf/USREPORTS-456-45.pdf) — election consequences for a good-faith mistaken promise that was promptly retracted violated the First Amendment. The case supports a fair correction/version-history design; it does not make a later correction an automatic defense under RCW 9A.72.060.

**Design consequence:** the most defensible Phase 0 is a genuinely private, candidate-neutral, nonpublic research archive that authenticates identity, authorship, date, exact words, and version history. No truth tribunal, complaint verdict, government “truthful candidate” badge, censure, nonparticipant list, ballot/debate/benefit condition, or enforcement recommendation is supported.

### Public records, privacy, data, and outreach controls

- [Chapter 42.56 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.56&full=true), especially [RCW 42.56.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.56.010), [.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.56.050), and [.230](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=42.56.230) — records an agency prepares, owns, uses, or retains in connection with government conduct may be public records. Privacy requires both highly offensive disclosure and no legitimate public concern, and exemptions are specific. No generic exemption was located for candidate sworn submissions. If government receives a record, use data minimization and assume disclosure/retention review is required.
- [RCW 40.14.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=40.14.010), [Washington State Archives electronic-records guidance](https://www.sos.wa.gov/archives/help-government-agencies/managing-electronic-records), and [retention-schedule guidance](https://www.sos.wa.gov/archives/help-government-agencies/help-using-records-retention-schedules) — electronic and social/web records can be retainable public records. The correct retention period depends on the agency function and applicable schedule; DGG should not invent a universal period.
- [Chapter 1.80 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=1.80&full=true) — electronic records and signatures generally may have legal effect, but an agency controls whether and how it accepts them and must address security and auditability. UETA does not replace notarial, public-records, or program-specific controls.
- [RCW 19.255.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.255.010) and [chapter 19.375 RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.375&full=true) — unauthorized acquisition of unsecured personal information can trigger breach duties; an ordinary audio/video record is not automatically a biometric identifier, but a voiceprint or other automated measurement used to identify a person can be. DGG should prohibit face/voice-template identification absent a separate legal and security review.
- [PDC grassroots-lobbying guidance](https://pdc.wa.gov/registration-reporting/lobbying/grassroots-lobbying), [RCW 29B.50.100](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29B.50.100), and [WAC 390-20-150](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=390-20-150) — public websites, email campaigns, meetings, or advertising designed primarily to influence legislation may be grassroots lobbying. On the cutoff date, PDC lists adjusted thresholds of more than $1,500 in one month or $3,000 in three months, with L-6 timing and sponsor rules. Recheck immediately before release.
- [RCW 29B.10.410](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=29B.10.410), [RCW 29B.30.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=29B.30.050), and [PDC independent-expenditure guidance](https://www.pdc.wa.gov/registration-reporting/independent-expenditures/what-report) — candidate-, ballot-, or election-directed mass communications, including digital communications, can trigger political-advertising, sponsor-identification, or reporting rules. ## Pennsylvania comparator

- [57 Pa.C.S. §§ 302, 304–306.1, 311, 315, and 320](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/57/57.HTM) — Pennsylvania notarial definitions, conflicts, act requirements, remote notarization, recognition of out-of-state notarial acts, certificates, and records. Pennsylvania is a strong notarial-mechanics analogue: an authorized Pennsylvania notary located in Pennsylvania may remotely notarize for a U.S. signer elsewhere, and Pennsylvania recognizes a valid Washington notarial act. Recognition supplies notarial effect; it does not import Washington criminal law.
- [4 Pa. Code chapter 167, subchapter H](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/004/chapter167/subchapHtoc.html), [§ 167.86](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/004/chapter167/s167.86.html), and the [Pennsylvania Bulletin final-form rule](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pabulletin/data/vol56/56-13/438.html) — rules effective 2026-03-28, including alternative identity routes, technology, electronic-certificate association, audiovisual recording, and ten-year retention. “Tamper evident” concerns the electronic certificate's integrity and association, not every underlying tangible record.
- [Pennsylvania Department of State remote-notarization guidance](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dos/resources/notaries-resources-and-information/electronic-or-remote-notarization) — current administrative resource; statute and final regulations control.
- [18 Pa.C.S. §§ 4501 and 4902–4904](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/18/00.049..HTM) — Pennsylvania perjury requires a materially false sworn statement in an official proceeding; false swearing generally requires an official proceeding, intent to mislead a public servant, or a statement the law requires to be sworn; unsworn falsification likewise depends on specified governmental/legal gateways. That a private voluntary DGG statement ordinarily does not fit these elements merely because it is notarized is a statutory inference, not a located appellate holding.
- [18 Pa.C.S. § 102](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute&txtType=PDF&ttl=18) — Pennsylvania territorial jurisdiction can locate conduct or results in Pennsylvania but cannot supply a missing offense element.
- [*Rubin v. CBS Broadcasting Inc.*, 170 A.3d 560, 568 (Pa. Super. Ct. 2017)](https://www.pacourts.us/assets/opinions/Superior/out/090817e.pdf?cb=1) — Pennsylvania recognizes false-impression risk from selective presentation of true facts; use exact invitation/response statuses and contextual disclaimer.

**Comparator conclusion:** Pennsylvania can replicate identity, oath, remote appearance, certificate, and recognition mechanics. It is not a parallel private false-swearing deterrence jurisdiction. DGG should describe a Pennsylvania implementation as an integrity/provenance device unless Pennsylvania counsel identifies an independent legally required oath or governmental proceeding.

## Empirical authorities supporting a bounded pilot—not a guaranteed outcome

- [Zickfeld et al., “Effectiveness of ex ante honesty oaths in reducing dishonesty depends on content,” *Nature Human Behaviour* 9 (2025), 169–187](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02009-0) — megastudy of 21 oath variants and a no-oath condition involving 21,506 UK/US participants in an incentivized tax-reporting game. Ten variants significantly increased compliance by 4.5–8.5 percentage points; results differed by content. Transfer to political interviews is untested.
- [Jacquemet et al., “Do truth-telling oaths improve honesty in crowd-working?” *PLOS ONE* 16 (2021)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0244958) — 1,410 observations collected and 1,366 analyzed. Payoff-maximizing ten-head reports fell from 17.8% to 12.9% (4.9 percentage points; roughly 27% relative), mean reporting fell from 6.33 to 6.06, and the 42% to 40% shirking change was nonsignificant. Ten heads can occur truthfully; do not call every such report a proven lie. The authors characterize the oath as no panacea.
- [Jacquemet et al., “Truth Telling Under Oath,” *Management Science* 65 (2019), 426–438](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2892) — the main within-subject study involved 240 senders and 960 decisions. The neutral oath change was nonsignificant; the loaded oath reduced lying by 8.3 percentage points in that design. A separate between-subject robustness design produced a larger loaded-oath difference. Do not merge the designs or present the abstract's headline as a pure universal oath effect.

These studies support testing a specific, voluntary commitment intervention. They do not prove that notarization improves political truth, that sanctions will be enforced, or that audiences will interpret the format correctly. DGG should preregister outcomes and adverse effects.
